## What PPF Actually Does

Paint protection film is an 8-mil polyurethane film that bonds physically to your paintwork. It takes the hit before your clear coat does. Stone chips, gravel spray, road debris, minor car park scrapes — the film absorbs them.

The self-healing property is genuine: surface-level scratches in the film warm up and disappear, either from direct sunlight or a pour of warm water. That's the thermoplastic structure of the film responding to heat.

What PPF doesn't do: repel water, protect against UV oxidation, or prevent bird drop etching. Leave a sulphur-heavy dropping on unprotected PPF in 32°C Melbourne heat and the film surface will mark. The paint underneath stays safe — but the film itself will need attention.

## What Ceramic Coating Actually Does

A ceramic coating chemically bonds with your clear coat and creates a semi-permanent hydrophobic layer. Water beads and sheets off. Bird drops, insect splatter, and most road contamination sit on top of the coating surface rather than bonding to your paint.

In Melbourne's bayside suburbs — Brighton, Sandringham, Mordialloc — UV intensity from October through March is among the highest in metropolitan Australia. A ceramic-coated car parked outdoors in that environment oxidises significantly slower than unprotected paint. The hydrophobic layer also makes the weekly wash faster: contamination releases easier and re-bonding is reduced.

What ceramic can't do: stop physical impact. A stone chip at 100 km/h on the Eastern Freeway goes straight through a ceramic coating. The chemical bond doesn't absorb kinetic energy.

## Where Melbourne Makes the Choice Clearer

Melbourne's paint threats split geographically.

**Eastern Freeway and Eastlink commuters** deal with aggressive stone chip exposure — coarse aggregate, high truck traffic, and elevated speeds combine to make front-end chip damage near-certain within a year on unprotected paint. PPF on the partial front (bonnet, bumper, mirrors) is the only reliable answer. Ceramic alone won't stop chips.

**Bayside and inner east drivers** — Brighton, Toorak, Hawthorn, St Kilda — deal primarily with UV intensity, bird drop acidity, and car park contamination. These are ceramic coating threats. Bird dropping pH runs 3.5 to 4.5, and at Melbourne's summer surface temperatures, that acidity can etch permanently into unprotected clear coat in under 10 minutes on a 30°C-plus day.

Many Pristine customers live in both environments. A Toorak car that commutes on the Eastern Freeway needs both products.

## Why Most Melbourne Drivers End Up Using Both

The combination is straightforward once you understand what each product does.

PPF goes on high-impact zones first: front bumper, bonnet leading edge, front guards, wing mirrors, door leading edges. Ceramic or graphene coating goes on top — over the PPF and across the full vehicle. Ceramic bonds to PPF exactly as it bonds to clear coat.

The result: physical impact protection where you need it most, hydrophobic UV and contaminant resistance across the whole surface.

Starting prices for a combined package: PPF from $3,000, ceramic from $750 on top. That's less than the cost of a partial panel respray after a season of unprotected freeway driving — and your paint stays original under the film.

## FAQ

**Does ceramic coating stick to PPF?**
Yes. Ceramic and graphene coatings bond to PPF the same way they bond to clear coat. Standard practice is PPF first, cure it fully, then coat on top. The ceramic protects the film surface and adds the hydrophobic finish across the whole vehicle.

**Can I apply ceramic on top of PPF?**
Yes — and Pristine recommends it on all combined PPF and coating packages. The coating layer protects the film surface from bird drop etching and UV degradation, which extends the working life of the PPF underneath.

**Which is more expensive, PPF or ceramic coating in Melbourne?**
PPF starts at $3,000 for a partial front installation in Melbourne. Ceramic coating starts at $750 for full-vehicle coverage, graphene at $999. On a per-year basis: ceramic at 8 years works out at around $94 per year, graphene at 9 years at around $111 per year.

**Does PPF prevent bird drop damage?**
PPF prevents bird drops from reaching your paint — the film takes the contact. But the film surface itself can still be marked by acidic drops left in summer heat. Ceramic or graphene coating on top of PPF repels bird drops from the film surface before they can sit and etch.
