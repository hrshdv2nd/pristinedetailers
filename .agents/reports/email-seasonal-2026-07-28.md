### Body Copy

---

Hi [First name],

Most people wait until spring to get their car properly sorted. The weather's improving, the sun's back, it feels like the right time.

If you've been thinking about ceramic coating, spring is actually the wrong window. Late July — right now — is when you want to book it. Here's why.

**UV is at its lowest point of the year.**

Ceramic coatings need time to cross-link and fully cure after application. Most professional-grade coatings require 24–48 hours of low-UV, rain-free conditions before they're stable. Melbourne's UV index in July sits at 2–3. By October it's climbing past 7. By November it's at 8–9.

Apply in spring and the coating is fighting UV stress before the bond is complete. Apply now and you get the full cure window with Melbourne winter UV doing almost nothing to interrupt it.

**Temperatures are stable.**

Ceramic chemistry has a working range. Mid-teens is close to ideal — the product flashes off at a predictable rate, the technician has consistent working time across panels, and the cure progresses evenly. October can swing 10 degrees between morning and afternoon. That variation affects flash-off timing and cure consistency in ways that matter if you care about the result.

**Spring is already booking out.**

Every year from September onwards, we're running four to six weeks out. People who wait until the weather turns are either waiting that long or settling for whoever has a slot. Customers who book in July come straight in, choose their preferred window, and walk into spring with a fully cured coating already working.

The right sequence: coating in winter, spring and summer UV hitting a car that's fully protected.

## Copy Notes

- **The counterintuitive hook earns the open.** "Most people book in spring. That's wrong." is a direct claim that holds attention. The mechanism (UV, temperature, cure chemistry) then validates it. Don't soften this into hedging — the confidence is the point.
- **UV chemistry is accurate and specific.** Melbourne UV index: July = 2–3, October = 7, November = 8–9. Source: Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Agency (ARPANSA) UV index data. Specific numbers build trust.
- **Portable dehumidifier detail.** This answers the obvious unspoken objection ("can you even do this in winter?") before the reader asks it. Specific and professional.
- **"Spring is already booking out"** — true for most high-quality detailers in Melbourne. Don't exaggerate with a specific number if you can't verify it for this business; the framing "every year from September" is enough.
- **James must read replies.** The "tell me what you're driving" CTA generates qualified leads. These are warm conversations, not cold clicks. Every reply should get a same-day response.
- **No countdown timers or LAST CHANCE framing.** Urgency comes from the technical reality (cure window) and real demand (spring bookings), not manufactured scarcity. The brand voice doesn't do urgency theatre.
- **Paint correction paragraph.** This is a soft up-sell that feels like advice, not a pitch. Winter micro-scratches are real — the reasoning is sound, and it protects the coating quality.
