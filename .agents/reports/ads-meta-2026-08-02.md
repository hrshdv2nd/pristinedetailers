# Meta Ads — Membership
**Date:** 2026-08-02 (Sunday prep run — ready to launch Thursday 6 August)
**Week:** 31 → Rotation Week 7 → **Membership**
**Platforms:** Facebook Feed + Instagram Feed
**Campaign objective:** Conversions (membership sign-up)
**Landing page:** `/membership` → `/booking`
**Target location:** Melbourne, VIC — Toorak, South Yarra, Hawthorn, Brighton, Malvern, Sandringham

---

## Seasonal Context

Three distinct buyer segments are at peak membership consideration in August:

**The Lapsed Booker.** Melbourne's winter (June–August) is the single biggest maintenance slippage window of the year. Owners who care about their cars — and who have booked Pristine before, or researched detailing before — fell behind somewhere between April and now. The car doesn't look terrible yet, but the owner knows it's been four months and they keep meaning to sort it. This is the highest-volume membership prospect in August. The ad that names their exact experience ("you meant to rebook in March") converts without needing a discount or urgency tactic because the product is the solution to the stated problem.

**The Ceramic Coating Holder Seeking Maintenance Structure.** Owners who have a ceramic coating (applied by Pristine or another provider) know — or are beginning to realise — that the coating needs maintenance details to perform as advertised. Without regular washing and periodic decontamination, ceramic coatings lose their hydrophobic properties faster than the warranty implies. The membership that includes bi-monthly contact with a certified technician is the correct product for this buyer. The Signature tier ($149/mo) is the specific offer — it includes ceramic maintenance, which is not offered on the Essential tier.

**The EOFY New-Car Owner, 8 Weeks In.** Cars purchased in June for EOFY tax purposes are now 8 weeks old. These owners have cleared the "it's too new to need anything" mental block, and the first maintenance question is active. Unlike PPF (a one-off $3,000 decision), membership is the right framing here: one sign-up now, and the car is maintained without further decisions. This segment overlaps with the PPF EOFY audience from last week — target new-vehicle purchase signals in a separate ad set.

**August specificity — bird droppings and winter grime.** August marks the start of nesting season for magpies and noisy miners across Melbourne's Inner East and Bayside. These birds produce acidic droppings that etch clear coats within 48–72 hours in ambient temperature, even without direct sun. For an owner of a Porsche parked on a tree-lined street in Toorak or a Range Rover under the figs in Brighton, this is not an abstract risk — it is a visible, recurring problem. The membership's monthly-contact model means a technician spots and treats droppings between scheduled details. This hook works best in the post-join landing page copy and onboarding email, not in cold prospecting ads where the product decision hasn't been made yet.

Run the three ad variants simultaneously in separate ad sets, not combined. Each targets a distinct psychological state; pooling them makes performance data unreadable and prevents optimization.

## Ad Variants

### Variant 1 — The Lapsed Booker (Angle: The rebooking that never happened)

**Primary text (111 chars):**
> You meant to rebook your detail in March. Then April. Winter's here. The membership handles this — from $99/mo.

**Headline (26 chars):**
> Set & Forget. From $99/mo.

**Description (26 chars):**
> We come. You don't rebook.

**Why this works:** Opens with an experience, not a product. "You meant to rebook your detail in March. Then April." is not a line anyone invented for an ad — it is the verbatim internal monologue of the Lapsed Booker, reproduced exactly as they've said it to themselves. Seeing that sentence in a Facebook feed creates immediate recognition: this ad knows what I've been doing, or not doing. Recognition is the prerequisite for trust. "Winter's here" adds seasonal specificity — the lapsed booker in August is a Melbourne-specific timing, and naming the season grounds the ad in reality rather than evergreen messaging. "The membership handles this" is the resolution without any superlative: no claim about quality, no promise of transformation, just a structural description of what the product does. "From $99/mo" provides the entry price to close off the objection that hasn't been raised yet (Is this expensive?) before it forms.

Headline "Set & Forget. From $99/mo." is the mental model the buyer needs to make the join decision. The description "We come. You don't rebook." mirrors the ad's core value proposition back in the lowest possible reading-complexity format. These four words are what the buyer gets from this transaction: a service that comes to them without them having to think about it again.

**Audience:** Broad Inner East and Bayside cold audience — previous Pristine website visitors (30+ days, non-converter), Melbourne vehicle owner interests (BMW, Mercedes, Audi, Tesla, Porsche), income signals (property investor, prestige retail, private travel), postcodes 3101–3148, 3186–3191. This is the primary acquisition audience for V1.

### Variant 2 — The Ceramic Coating Holder (Angle: Your coating needs this to work)

**Primary text (115 chars):**
> A ceramic coating lasts 5 years. Maintenance details every 6 weeks keep it that way. Signature membership, $149/mo.

**Headline (25 chars):**
> Save 35% With Membership.

**Description (27 chars):**
> Members save up to 35% p.a.

**Why this works:** Opens with a technical fact that the ceramic coating owner already believes to be true — "a ceramic coating lasts 5 years" is the standard claim they were given when they bought the product. "Maintenance details every 6 weeks keep it that way" is the clause their installer probably mentioned but they may not have acted on. This ad is not educating them about ceramic coatings; it is reminding them of maintenance they know they should be doing. The gap between "I have a ceramic coating" and "I have a ceramic coating that is being properly maintained" is the product being sold.

"Signature membership, $149/mo." comes at the end rather than the start because the buyer needed the technical rationale before the price. Price-first for a maintenance product produces price comparisons; rationale-first produces value acceptance. The Signature tier is the only tier that includes ceramic maintenance — this matters for this ad set. Do not run this variant against Essential tier ($99/mo) targets; the feature being sold (ceramic maintenance) is Signature-specific.

Headline "Save 35% With Membership." delivers the annual savings proof in a headline format. Description "Members save up to 35% p.a." restates it with the qualifier — "up to" keeps the claim clean.

**Audience:** Custom audience — past Pristine service customers who received a ceramic coating (from CRM list, uploaded as a custom audience). If CRM audience is too small for delivery (under 300 people), extend to: website visitors who viewed `/services/ceramic-coating` in the last 90 days; users who engaged with any Pristine ceramic coating social content in the last 60 days; interest-based overlay: ceramic coating interest + Melbourne + high-income signals. This is a warm audience with product knowledge, not cold prospecting.

**Primary text (101 chars):**
> Your BMW is in Hawthorn. Winter grime, bird droppings, water spots. The membership handles all of it.

**Headline (26 chars):**
> We Come. You Just Show Up.

**Description (27 chars):**
> Toorak, Brighton, Hawthorn.

**Why this works:** "Your BMW is in Hawthorn" is the opening because it names the car and the suburb in five words, which is the fastest possible signal to the right buyer that this ad is for them and not for the general public. A BMW owner who lives or works in Hawthorn reads this and stops scrolling. The suburb specificity is not just localisation; it reflects the actual target geography. Hawthorn's tree canopy, bird life, and proximity to the Eastern Freeway make it the most specific suburb for the winter grime/droppings combination.

"Winter grime, bird droppings, water spots." names three specific maintenance problems without interpretation or explanation. These are things the BMW owner in Hawthorn is looking at on their car right now, in August, without necessarily having a plan for any of them. The list does not need a verb or a claim — it is a diagnosis, not a pitch. "The membership handles all of it." is the resolution: three words, no superlative, complete.

Headline "We Come. You Just Show Up." and description "Toorak, Brighton, Hawthorn." extend the specificity: the geography confirms coverage, the headline resolves the logistics anxiety that underlies every mobile service purchase for a professional who has never used a mobile detailer before.

### Lookalike Audiences

- **Seed 1:** Current and past membership subscribers (all-time) — highest-quality seed; 1% LAL is the priority and should receive 60% of lookalike budget; membership subscribers are the exact conversion signal
- **Seed 2:** All-time high-value service bookers (ceramic coating + PPF completions) — membership converters skew heavily from previous high-ticket service buyers; this seed expands coverage beyond the small membership customer base
- **Seed 3:** Booking page visitors who spent more than 60 seconds on `/membership` in the last 90 days — strong consideration signal even without conversion; these users compared tiers and are the most likely non-converter pool
- **Run:** 1% LAL (precision, Inner East + Bayside, same geography as prospecting campaigns) and 3% LAL (scale, broader Melbourne metro) as separate ad sets; 70% budget to 1%, 30% to 3%
- **Exclude:** Active current members — showing acquisition ads to people already subscribed wastes spend and creates confusion. Route active members to the Signature upgrade flow, not the join campaign.

## Retargeting — /booking and /membership Page Abandoners

For users who visited `/membership` or began the membership sign-up flow but did not complete (window: 7 days):

**Primary text (96 chars):**
> Still considering the membership? The next detail is scheduled the moment you join. From $99/mo.

**Headline (27 chars):**
> Join. Your Detail Gets Set.

**Description (27 chars):**
> From $99/mo. No commitment.

**Targeting window:** 7 days post-visit
**Frequency cap:** 3× per week — membership is lower-deliberation than a $3,000 PPF purchase; the price point ($99/mo entry) allows slightly higher frequency without triggering the resistance that over-serving a high-ticket audience creates

**Why this works:** The buyer who reached the membership page and left has already resolved the product question (they know what the membership is) and the price question (they saw $99 and $149/mo and didn't immediately bounce on price). The stall is one of three things: (1) uncertainty about what's actually included in each tier, (2) inertia — they'll do it later, (3) a logistical question (do you service my suburb?). "The next detail is scheduled the moment you join" answers objection (2) by reframing the join decision as the action that triggers the result — not signing up and then thinking about what happens next, but signing up and immediately being in motion. "From $99/mo" restates the entry price rather than the premium. "No commitment" in the description is the most important four characters in the retargeting stack: the membership is month-to-month, and if the buyer thinks they're locked in for 12 months they won't convert. This needs to be explicitly stated in the retargeting creative even if it's on the membership page, because the buyer may not have read far enough to find it.

**Exclusions:**
- Users who completed a membership sign-up after their visit
- Current active members
- Users who bounced in under 10 seconds (no genuine interest signal)
- PPF buyers in the last 30 days (these buyers are already in a conversion relationship; route to membership upsell in post-purchase email sequence, not retargeting ads)

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance for membership ads:**

- **Hero static (V1 — lapsed booker):** A Pristine technician in branded workwear at a residential driveway in an Inner East Melbourne street — established trees visible, double-brick garage or period home in background. The car is mid-detail: clean panels visible on one side, the technician working on the other. No model or aspirational photography. The scene is unmistakably Melbourne and unmistakably real. Caption overlay (minimal, clean): "The detail comes to you. Every month." This is the visual promise for the set-and-forget frame.

- **Hero static (V2 — ceramic holder):** Close-up of a ceramic coating water-bead shot on a prestige vehicle panel (black or dark grey preferred — beading is most visible on dark paint). No text overlay — the hydrophobic performance is self-explanatory to the audience this variant targets. Pair with the primary text as caption; the image does the technical proof, the copy does the reasoning.

- **Carousel (V3 — vehicle-specific):** Four-frame carousel — one frame per vehicle (BMW, Range Rover, Tesla, Porsche). Each frame: the car in a Melbourne suburb context (not a studio). Frame 1: BMW on a Hawthorn street, autumn leaves on the road. Frame 2: Range Rover outside a Brighton residence, grey winter sky. Frame 3: Tesla parked in South Yarra car park, overcast light. Frame 4: Porsche outside a Malvern Victorian terrace. No text overlay on any frame — the specificity of car + suburb in the image does the targeting work visually. The primary text naming "Your BMW is in Hawthorn" (etc.) appears above the carousel.

- **Reels/Stories (retargeting):** Single-shot 6-second vertical — a technician completing a detail and handing the owner their keys outside a residential property. No voiceover. Subtitle overlay: "Membership. We come. You show up." End card: "$99/mo → Book now." Frequency-capped at 3× per week.

**Format priority for Membership:**
1. Instagram Feed — V3 vehicle-specific carousels; high visual specificity in this placement; the ICP in Toorak and South Yarra are Instagram-heavy and the carousel format allows multiple vehicle hooks in a single ad
2. Facebook Feed — V1 (lapsed booker narrative text); slightly older Bayside demographic (35–55) reads longer copy in Facebook feed; V2 (ceramic holder) also works well here
3. Instagram Reels — retargeting 6-second clip; non-intrusive, completion-rate-optimised
4. Stories — retargeting only; frequency-capped; not for cold prospecting (membership requires more explanation than a single Stories frame)

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Cold audience: V1 (lapsed booker) vs V3a (BMW/Hawthorn vehicle-specific) across Inner East — measure CTR to membership page; do not mix into one ad set; the question is whether problem-narrative (V1) or identity-specificity (V3) produces stronger initial engagement in this geography
- V3 vehicle variants (V3a–d) run per interest group — measure CTR and cost-per-membership-page-visit; cut any variant at <1.5% CTR after 1,000 impressions per ad set; consolidate budget to the two best-performing vehicle-suburb combinations after 7 days
- V2 (ceramic holder) runs against custom ceramic coating audience only — small pool, expected low volume but high conversion rate; measure cost-per-join rather than CTR
- Retargeting budget: 15% of total membership campaign budget; measure against cost-per-rebook-of-non-converter

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** Hawthorn, Brighton, Toorak, South Yarra, Malvern, Kew, Inner East, Bayside named per ad variant or description; BMW, Range Rover, Tesla, Porsche named per interest targeting segment; "bird droppings" and "winter grime" are August-specific Melbourne pain points grounded in real seasonal conditions; the Eastern Freeway is referenced implicitly through the Inner East geography
- **Tone check:** "You meant to rebook your detail in March" is observational, not accusatory; "The membership handles this" is confident without a claim of superiority; "We come. You don't rebook." is direct without exclamation marks; no "Melbourne's best" or "our team is passionate"; "Toorak, Brighton, Hawthorn" in the description is a geography list, not a boast
- **Tier clarity:** V1 and V3 anchor at $99/mo (Essential — lowest barrier to join); V2 anchors at $149/mo (Signature — the right product for the ceramic holder audience). Retargeting anchors at $99/mo because the non-converter needs the lowest barrier back into the funnel.
- **Membership vs one-off:** None of the variants mention one-off detailing. This campaign is exclusively acquisition-focused on the membership model. Users who are not ready for membership are directed to one-off booking through the landing page, not through the ad creative. Mixing one-off and membership copy in the same ad creates indecision, not conversion.
- **No commitment** language: Critical for this campaign. The membership fear is lock-in. "No commitment" in the retargeting description must be surfaced. For prospecting creatives, the landing page resolves this — but the retargeting non-converter may not have read far enough to see the cancellation policy. Surface it in the ad.
