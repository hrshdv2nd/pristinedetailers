## Seasonal Context

Late July is the prime PPF window for two overlapping reasons:

1. **EOFY new-car buyers.** Australian businesses purchase luxury vehicles before 30 June for tax deductions. Those June deliveries are now 3–4 weeks old — the exact window where new car owners start feeling the anxiety of stone chips and car park scratches on a car they've spent $80K–$250K on. They are the highest-intent PPF prospect in the calendar year.

2. **Six months of winter damage.** Cars that haven't been protected have accumulated spring gravel, wet road debris, and freeway stone chips since January. Owners who kept saying "I'll get PPF soon" are now looking at their bonnet and regretting it. The stone chip count is at its annual peak in July.

These two audiences — the new EOFY owner and the regretful unprotected owner — need different copy angles. The ad groups below split them.

## Keyword Recommendations

### Exact Match — 5 Keywords

```
[PPF Melbourne]
[paint protection film Melbourne]
[mobile PPF Melbourne]
[PPF Toorak]
[PPF Mornington Peninsula]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[PPF Melbourne]` — primary short-form term; highest volume in category; buyer has already decided on the product type and is comparing installers; must own this term to compete in the Melbourne PPF market
- `[paint protection film Melbourne]` — long-form variant; buyers who type the full product name have done more research than the abbreviated searcher; higher intent, higher consideration; conversion rate typically exceeds short-form
- `[mobile PPF Melbourne]` — buyer has specifically decided they want mobile installation; this is Pristine's strongest segment; the qualifier "mobile" filters out buyers who are happy to drop off at a fixed studio, leaving only the buyers who value the come-to-you model
- `[PPF Toorak]` — suburb-specific queries signal a buyer who is localised and ready to book; Toorak is Pristine's anchor suburb and the Inner East suburb most associated with Porsche and high-end European ownership; higher conversion intent than city-level terms
- `[PPF Mornington Peninsula]` — the Peninsula is a distinct geography with its own pain point (gravel roads, coastal UV) and a buyer audience that cannot easily access a fixed studio in Dandenong or Clayton; "mobile" is not the selling point here — "comes to me on the Peninsula" is; this term captures that segment

---

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[PPF kit]
[DIY paint protection film]
[vinyl wrap]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[PPF kit]` — buyer is purchasing a self-install consumer product (Xpel DIY, STEK, etc.); no service intent; will click, read pricing, and bounce immediately; $0 revenue potential
- `[DIY paint protection film]` — explicit self-install intent; budget or enthusiast buyer who is willing to do their own installation; opposite of Pristine's certified professional application model; do not pay to educate them
- `[vinyl wrap]` — different product category (decorative colour change, not protective film); some crossover in searcher behaviour (both are "film on a car") but vinyl wrap buyers have completely different intent and price expectations; including this term inflates impressions with non-converting traffic and damages quality score

## Retargeting Note

For users who visited `/services/ppf` or the PPF section of `/booking` but did not convert:

> **Headline:** Your PPF Quote Is Waiting
> **Description:** Certified mobile paint protection film — Toorak, Brighton, Peninsula. From $3,000. No drop-off. Book a quote.

Window: 7-day hot retargeting.
Frequency cap: 2 impressions/week.

Message angle: PPF at $3,000 is a high-deliberation purchase. The buyer who reached the booking page and left is not against the product — they are calculating. The retargeting message does not fabricate urgency or discount; it restates the key qualifier (mobile, certified, local geography) and lowers the action barrier from "book now" to "book a quote." A quote is a softer commitment that reactivates the consideration process without requiring a final purchase decision. The ICP at this price point finds countdown timers and fake scarcity signals suspicious — they disqualify a brand, not motivate it.

## Context Notes

- **Seasonal timing:** EOFY (June 30) new car purchases mean a wave of owners with 3–4 week-old vehicles sitting in Toorak and South Yarra garages who have not yet got PPF installed. This is the highest-intent PPF window of the year. Ads should launch by Monday 27 July to capture this cohort before they accept the stone chips as inevitable.

The recommended upsell path (PPF first, then ceramic on top) belongs on the landing page and in the post-conversion email sequence — not in the ad, where mixing products dilutes intent and reduces quality score
