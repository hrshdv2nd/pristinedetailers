Being the authoritative voice on those threads is free SEO that compounds.

**Presence strategy:**

**Draft Post — Whirlpool Automotive forum:**

> **Thread title:** How to evaluate a paint correction or coating quote — what to ask before you commit
>
> I've been detailing cars professionally in Melbourne for a while and I see the same situation regularly: someone books a "ceramic coating" at a price that seems good, ends up with a result they're disappointed with, and then I'm doing a correction job 12 months later that was entirely preventable.
>
> The issue is almost never the coating. It's what happens before the coating is applied.
>
> Here's what to ask any detailer before you hand over the keys:
>
> **1. Are you doing a paint decontamination before the correction?**
> A clay bar or chemical decontamination step removes embedded iron particles, road grime, and industrial fallout from the paint surface. If the detailer skips this, you're polishing over contaminated paint. Ask specifically — don't assume it's included.
>
> **2. What's the correction process? Single-stage or two-stage?**
> A single-stage (one-step) polish removes minor swirls and light scratches. A two-stage uses a cutting compound followed by a finishing polish — it addresses deeper marks but takes significantly longer. If your car has visible swirl marks in direct sunlight, you need at least a single-stage correction before any coating. A coating applied over existing scratches will preserve and highlight them at higher gloss.
>
> **3. Are you using a paint depth gauge?**
> Clear coat thickness varies panel to panel, especially on cars that have had previous accident repairs. Cutting through thin clear coat permanently damages it. If a detailer isn't checking paint depth before polishing, they're guessing. This matters particularly on respray panels — if your car has had any panel work, a depth reading should be standard.
>
> **4. What coating product specifically, and what's the actual warranty?**
> "9H ceramic coating" is a marketing term, not a product specification. Ask the brand name of the coating, the model/product line, and what the warranty covers. A manufacturer-backed warranty requires the installer to be an authorised applicator. Ask to see their applicator certification. If they can't produce it, the warranty claim is probably worthless.
>
> **5. What environment are you applying it in?**
> Ceramic coatings need a controlled environment — free of dust, at the right ambient temperature and humidity. Applying in a driveway on a hot day, or in a dusty shed, produces a compromised result. Ask where the job happens.
>
> None of these are trick questions — any professional detailer will answer them without hesitation. If they can't, or get defensive, you have your answer.
>
> Happy to answer any questions on this. Ceramic coatings are genuinely excellent when applied correctly — the bad reputation some of them have comes from skipped prep, not the product.

### Community 2: BYD Owners Club Australia (Facebook Group)

**Why this community:**
BYD is now explicitly in our ICP car list — and for good reason. BYD sales in Melbourne have accelerated sharply; the Seal, Atto 3, Shark, and Sealion are now common in Bayside, St Kilda, and the inner east. The owners are our demographic: tech-aware, high-income, asset-conscious. And BYD paint has specific characteristics that aren't yet widely understood in the Australian market — the paint is relatively soft, and new BYD owners who've come from German cars are often surprised by how quickly it chips or swirls. Facebook groups like "BYD Owners Club Australia" and "BYD Australia Owners Group" have 5,000–20,000 members and active daily discussion. Paint and protection questions come up constantly and are currently answered poorly — mostly by people who don't know paint chemistry.

This is a first-mover opportunity: we can become the trusted voice for BYD-specific paint care in Melbourne before any competitor establishes that positioning.

**Presence strategy:**
- Join the group and spend one week observing — identify recurring paint questions
- Post EV-specific content that shows genuine understanding of BYD's paint system (not recycled advice from ICE car forums)
- Position as someone who works on BYDs regularly and understands the nuances
- Target the "new car owner" anxiety window — people who've just taken delivery and are deciding what to do about paint protection in the first 30–60 days

**Draft Post — BYD Owners Club Australia Facebook Group:**

> **What to actually do about your BYD's paint in the first 60 days — a straight answer**
>
> BYD paint questions come up here all the time and the answers are usually vague ("get it ceramic coated") or overcomplicated. Here's what I'd actually tell a friend who just picked up their Seal, Atto 3, or Sealion.
>
> **The short version:** BYD uses water-based paint that's softer than most German or Japanese cars. It's not fragile, but it marks more easily than owners coming from a BMW or Mazda expect. The good news: the clear coat is a good thickness from factory, which means you have more room for correction if needed.
>
> **What actually threatens BYD paint in Melbourne specifically:**
>
> *Stone chips from the freeway.* Front bumper and bonnet take the worst of it on the Eastern, Monash, and Peninsula Link. BYD's front fascia has a lot of exposed surface area. If you're doing freeway kays regularly, the bonnet leading edge will chip within 12–18 months without protection.
>
> *Automated car washes.* Soft paint + rotating brushes = swirl marks. You'll see them immediately on dark-coloured BYDs in direct sunlight. If you've put the car through a brush wash even once, worth getting it checked under a paint inspection light before committing to a coating — you don't want to lock in existing scratches.
>
> *Bird droppings sitting too long.* Same issue as any car, but soft paint means the etch sets faster. Wipe within a couple of hours in warm weather, longer is fine in winter.
>
> **What to do:**
>
> In the first 60 days: get a genuine hand wash, have the paint inspected under a light, then decide whether you want ceramic, PPF, or both based on your actual driving situation — not what the dealer tried to upsell you at handover.
>
> If you're freeway driving regularly: consider PPF on the front bumper and bonnet at a minimum. The film stops chips before they reach the paint.
>
> If it's mostly city driving and you want ease of maintenance: a quality ceramic coating makes washing much faster and adds UV protection.
>
> If you want both: PPF first, ceramic on top. That's the full setup.
>
> I work on a lot of BYDs in Melbourne lately and happy to answer any specific questions — what to do with a specific model, whether a particular product is worth the price, anything. Ask away.

### Community 3: Mornington Peninsula Lifestyle & Locals Groups (Facebook)

**Why this community:**
The Mornington Peninsula is explicitly in our target geography, but it's underserved by our current community presence. Peninsula residents are a specific ICP sub-segment: high-income households (Mt Eliza, Sorrento, Blairgowrie, Portsea), weekend homes with high-end cars, gravel roads to wineries and farms, plus the Peninsula Link stone chip exposure and the bay-side UV and salt air combination. Large Facebook groups like "Mornington Peninsula Locals", "Mornington Peninsula Community Noticeboard" (30,000+ members), and "Life on the Mornington Peninsula" aren't car-focused — they're general community groups — which is exactly the point. Showing up with helpful local knowledge in a non-car context reaches people before they're in buying mode, which is where the strongest trust is built.

The Peninsula resident isn't searching Reddit for ceramic coating advice. They're asking their neighbour or the Facebook group they already use every day.

**Presence strategy:**
- Join 2–3 Peninsula community groups as a local business presence
- Don't post car content into general threads — wait for relevant context (someone asks "does anyone know a good detailer on the Peninsula?")
- When those threads appear, reply briefly and helpfully — "We cover the Peninsula, happy to answer questions" — not a pitch
- Post one original piece of Peninsula-specific car care content every 4–6 weeks into car-relevant community threads
- The goal is to become the name that comes to mind when a local business recommendation is needed

**Draft Post — Mornington Peninsula Community Facebook Group:**

> **The Peninsula is genuinely hard on car paint — specifically what I see coming off the Nepean Highway and the back roads**
>
> We do a lot of work on cars down on the Peninsula and the damage pattern is different from inner Melbourne. Thought it was worth writing up since I see the same issues repeatedly.
>
> **Peninsula Link and the Frankston Freeway stone chips**
> The aggregate on Peninsula Link has a reputation and it's earned. Front bonnet, bumper, and A-pillar chips are significantly higher on cars that commute via the Peninsula Link compared to inner Melbourne routes. If you're doing this commute 3–4 days a week, the front of your car is taking a sustained hit. This is the strongest case for paint protection film on the front section — the film absorbs the chip before it reaches your paint.
>
> **Gravel roads to farms and wineries**
> Mt Eliza, Red Hill, Main Ridge — if you're driving unsealed roads regularly, the rocker panels and lower door sections collect stone strike marks. Usually not deep, but a lot of small impacts over time.
>
> **Bay-side UV and salt air**
> Portsea to Frankston — anything parked outdoors near the bay is getting hit by both UV and salt air more intensely than a car in Hawthorn or Carlton. Dark metallics oxidise noticeably faster. Unprotected clear coat is more vulnerable here than most people realise. A ceramic coating's UV resistance is particularly useful in this environment — it holds up much better than wax or synthetic sealants over time.
>
> **Parking under eucalypts**
> Gum sap and leaf stains are acidic and bond to paint faster than bird droppings. A lot of Peninsula properties have mature gum trees and cars sitting under them for hours. The staining is harder to remove the longer it sits — a quality ceramic coating makes it significantly easier to clean off before it etches.
>
> We cover the whole Peninsula and do mobile work at homes, offices, and sheds — no need to drive anywhere. Happy to answer any questions about what's worth doing for your specific situation.

## Partnership Recommendation: Prestige Crash Repairers

**The opportunity and why it's different from dealerships:**
Previous reports covered dealership referral programs. This is a different buying moment — and arguably higher intent. When a Ferrari owner, Range Rover driver, or Tesla Model S owner brings their car to a prestige crash repairer after an insurance event, two things are true: (1) the car is already being repaired to a high standard, and (2) the owner is emotionally engaged in the car's condition in a way they haven't been since they bought it. This is the window where the question "should I get the paint protected now?" is easiest to answer.

Most prestige crash repairers don't offer a post-repair detailing or paint protection service. They repair and return. The customer drives away with fresh paint and no ongoing protection — and no idea who to trust for the next step.

**The pitch:**
Pristine becomes the "post-repair paint protection partner" for 2–3 prestige crash repairers in Melbourne. After a repair is completed, the shop includes a Pristine information card in the car: *"Your repaired panel has fresh paint. For long-term protection, here's who the team recommends."* A certificate for a complimentary paint inspection (valued at $120) is attached. The customer calls us pre-warmed.

**Why prestige crash repairers say yes:**
- They don't lose revenue — they're not in the paint protection business
- They can offer customers a complete post-repair solution without managing it
- The referral card makes them look more thorough and professional
- Happy customers who trust the repairer's recommendation build loyalty back to the repairer, not just to us

**Target partners (Melbourne):**
- **Prestige Crash Repair Melbourne** (Port Melbourne) — known for Ferrari, Lamborghini, Porsche work
- **Elite Panel & Paint** or equivalent shops in Richmond, Hawthorn, Armadale — inner east prestige focus
- **Accident repair centres approved by** BMW, Mercedes, Audi, Porsche franchises — these handle the highest-value vehicles and have the most relevant customer base
- **Zagame Group's accident repair arm** — high volume of prestige European work

**Referral structure to propose:**
- Complimentary paint inspection card included in every handover pack (no cost to the repairer)
- Pristine pays $100–$150 referral fee per converted ceramic or PPF job
- For crash repairer's own vehicles (courtesy cars, demonstrators): preferred rate, quick turnaround, top of booking queue
- Introduce quarterly: a quick email update on how many referrals converted, with anonymised before/after photos they can use in their own marketing

**Why this compounds:**
```
Customer picks up repaired car (fresh paint)
    ↓
Sees Pristine card from a repairer they already trust
    ↓
Books paint inspection → upgrades to ceramic coating
    ↓
Joins Signature membership ($149/mo) for ongoing maintenance
    ↓
Tells their car club / Facebook group who handled their post-repair protection
    ↓
2–3 new referrals from a community where the crash repairer just vouched for us
```

The moment of a crash repair completion is the single highest-intent window for paint protection outside of buying a new car. We're not competing with anyone in that window right now.
