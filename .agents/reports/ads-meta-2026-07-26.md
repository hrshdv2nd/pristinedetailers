# Meta Ads — Paint Protection Film
**Date:** 2026-07-26 (Sunday prep run — ready to launch Thursday 30 July)
**Week:** 30 → Rotation Week 6 → **Paint Protection Film**
**Platforms:** Facebook Feed + Instagram Feed
**Campaign objective:** Lead generation / landing page conversions
**Landing page:** `/services/ppf` → `/booking`
**Target location:** Melbourne, VIC — Toorak, South Yarra, Brighton, Hawthorn, Mornington Peninsula

---

## Seasonal Context

Two audiences are at peak PPF intent in late July:

**The EOFY new-car owner.** Australia's tax year ends 30 June. Businesses and high-income individuals buy prestige vehicles in June to claim depreciation. Those cars — BMW 5 Series, Porsche Cayenne, Mercedes-AMG, Tesla Model S — are now 3–4 weeks old. The owner has driven them enough to feel the anxiety of stone chips and car park scratches, but not so long that they've accepted the damage as normal. This is the PPF sweet spot: buyer intent is high, the car is still pristine, and the emotional stakes are at their peak.

**The regretful unprotected owner.** Six months of autumn and winter driving on the Eastern and Monash has put stone chips on bonnets, bumpers, and door edges across Toorak, South Yarra, and Hawthorn. These owners looked at their car on the weekend and thought "I should have got PPF when it was new." That regret is the targeting signal. The ad that names their specific pain — their car, their suburb, their freeway — converts.

Run both audiences simultaneously in separate ad sets. They require different copy angles: new-car owners respond to prevention framing; regretful owners respond to "it's not too late" and restoration of pride.

## Ad Variants

### Variant 1 — The Stone Chip Problem (Angle: Physics, not bad luck)

**Primary text (106 chars):**
> Stone chips on the Eastern aren't bad luck — they're physics. PPF stops them before they reach your paint.

**Headline (27 chars):**
> PPF. Come To Your Driveway.

**Description (22 chars):**
> From $3K. No Workshop.

**Why this works:** Opens with a verbatim-style line from Pristine's brand voice — "stone chips on the freeway aren't bad luck — they're physics." This reframes a frustrating, passive experience (bad luck) as an engineering problem with a known solution (PPF). The buyer who drives the Eastern from Hawthorn every morning has already emotionally accepted that stone chips are inevitable; this headline disrupts that acceptance by telling them it doesn't have to be. "PPF stops them before they reach your paint" is the resolution — no discount, no urgency, just a direct statement of what the product does and why it matters. Headline names the service model (mobile) in three words. Description gives price and removes the main friction objection (no workshop) in four words. Total copy is 135 chars across the three fields — no wasted character.

**Audience:** Broad Inner East cold audience — BMW, Audi, Mercedes, Tesla interests overlaid on 3101–3148 postcode cluster. This is the freeway-commuter buyer.

Headline (25 chars):

**Why V2a works:** "Your 911 parks in Toorak" is the most specific car-suburb hook possible for this audience. "Every morning on the Eastern is another stone chip waiting to happen" is not an abstract threat; it names the specific daily drive from Toorak/South Yarra toward the CBD that puts 911 bonnets at risk. The Porsche 911 in its GT3 or Carrera form has a low, exposed front splitter that is disproportionately vulnerable to stone chips at freeway speed. An owner who knows this (and they do) reads this copy as coming from someone who also knows it — which is the first trust signal. "PPF changes that" is the resolution: short, confident, no superlative.

**Headline (24 chars):**

**Description (25 chars):**

**Targeting window:** 7 days post-visit
**Frequency cap:** 2× per week — PPF at $3,000 is a deliberate decision, not an impulse; over-serving this audience triggers doubt rather than urgency

**Why this works:** The buyer who reached the PPF booking page and left is in the consideration-to-decision transition. They know what PPF is, they know the price, and they haven't said no — they've paused. "No Drop-Off" in the description restates the differentiator that made them consider Pristine in the first place.

**Exclusions:** Anyone who completed a booking after visiting; existing PPF customers (last 24 months); active membership subscribers (route to a membership upgrade path, not acquisition flow).

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**
- Hero static: Certified technician applying PPF to the bonnet of a white or silver Porsche 911 in a Toorak double-brick garage — morning light, professional equipment, full protective setup visible. The residential driveway and established garden establish suburb recognition. Not a studio; this is their home. The film application process should be mid-installation: squeegee visible, film edge being pressed, technician in branded workwear with full kit around them. The visual communicates expertise without words.
- Before/after close-up (Instagram Feed): Split frame — left side shows a raw bonnet with 3–4 stone chips circled in red; right side shows the same bonnet angle with PPF applied (gloss visible, chips absent). No graphic design treatment — raw photography, consistent angle. Caption: "This is what the Eastern does to your bonnet. This is what PPF does about it."
- PPF application timelapse (Reels/Stories 10–15 sec): Real-time application of film to a bonnet and front bumper — panel by panel, squeegee, heat gun, edge wrap. No music. Just the physical process. The competence displayed in a timelapse of proper PPF installation is more persuasive than any copy for a buyer who is worried about who they're trusting with a $200K car.
- "Still perfect" static (V2d EOFY variant): A new luxury car — Porsche Cayenne or BMW 5 Series — fresh off delivery, no chips, showroom gloss. Caption overlay (clean sans-serif, white): "4 weeks old. Still perfect. Keep it that way." CTA: "Get a PPF quote." Simple, emotional, no copy-heavy treatment.
- Avoid: stock photography of PPF on any car; stock photography of any American or European driveway or streetscape; stylised 3D renders; "before" images that look like a stock image rather than a real chip on a real Melbourne car.

**Format priority for PPF:**
1. Instagram Feed — V2 vehicle-specific variants; visual-first format with car + suburb specificity; the ICP in Toorak and South Yarra are Instagram-heavy and respond to high-quality creative
2. Facebook Feed — V1 (stone chip physics) and V3 (regret); broader age demographic reach (35–55 Bayside segment); wider text format works for V3's narrative opening
3. Instagram Reels — PPF application timelapse; native format, high organic engagement; no voiceover needed — the craft sells itself visually
4. Stories — retargeting only; single-image or minimal-text format with quote-request CTA; frequency-capped at 2× per week

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Cold audience: V1 (physics/freeway) vs V3 (regret/restoration) vs V2d (EOFY new-car) across Bayside and Inner East — measure CTR and cost-per-click-to-PPF-page; do not mix into one ad set
- V2 vehicle variants (V2a, V2b, V2c) run per interest group as challenger sets — determine which vehicle/suburb combo produces the lowest CPA within 7 days
- Retargeting budget: 10–15% of total PPF budget; exclude from prospecting measurement pool
- Kill any cold variant at <1.2% CTR after 1,000 impressions per ad set (PPF is lower CTR than ceramic given $3,000 price; adjust benchmark accordingly); consolidate budget to winner

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** Eastern Freeway, Monash Freeway, Toorak, South Yarra, Mornington Peninsula, double-brick garages, school-run, EOFY named or embedded; Porsche 911, Ferrari, McLaren, BMW all named per audience segment; "stone chips" is verbatim customer language from product marketing context
- **Tone check:** Confident and direct — "stone chips aren't bad luck, they're physics" is confident; "I should have got PPF the day I drove off the lot" is vulnerable and true; no exclamation marks; no claim of being Melbourne's best; no "passionate about cars"
- **PPF vs ceramic:** No mention of ceramic coating in any variant. A buyer who is researching PPF is making a separate $3,000 decision and should not be distracted by a separate $750 product in the same ad. The upsell to ceramic (PPF first, ceramic on top) belongs in the post-booking follow-up and on the landing page — not in the acquisition ad where it creates decision paralysis
- **EOFY timing window:** This is a time-specific angle and will lose relevance after mid-August. V2d should be paused by 18 August when the "4 weeks old" framing stops being accurate for June deliveries. Replace with a standard new-car-owner variant ("brand new car? protect it before the first chip") at that point
- **Price transparency:** $3,000 is stated in all variants. Pristine's PPF buyer is not price-sensitive at this level — they own a $100K+ car and have already made the largest financial decision. But naming the price upfront disqualifies price-shoppers before the click, reducing CPA by preventing bounce on the landing page from people expecting a $200 service
