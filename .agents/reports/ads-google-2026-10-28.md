## Seasonal Context

Week 44 lands in the single most compelling paint correction window of the Melbourne calendar year. Three conditions are simultaneously true for the Inner East and Bayside ICP right now that create concentrated, high-intent search behaviour.

**The post-winter reveal.** Melbourne's winter (June–September) runs exactly four months of cold mornings, wet roads, and elevated bird activity in the canopy suburbs — Toorak, Hawthorn, Malvern, Camberwell. A Toorak car owner who drove the Eastern Freeway or South Eastern daily through winter has accumulated: swirl marks from winter car washes (automated brushes and poorly trained hand-wash operators are the primary cause of swirl marks on prestige cars in Melbourne), bird dropping etch that had weeks to bake into clear coat before it was noticed, and iron fallout from brake dust embedded in the paint from slow commuter traffic on the Eastern and Monash. October is the month the owner looks at their car in actual sunlight — not the grey winter light that hides swirl marks — and sees the damage for the first time. The search that follows ("paint correction melbourne", "swirl mark removal") is not exploratory. It is reactive. The buyer already knows something is wrong with their paint and wants someone to fix it.

**The Melbourne Cup window (Tuesday 4 November 2026).** Melbourne Cup week — seven days from today — is the single highest-visibility car event in Melbourne's social calendar for the ICP. A Toorak, South Yarra, or Hawthorn car owner with a $100K+ European driving to Flemington or a post-Cup lunch on Punt Road is acutely aware that their car's paint condition is visible. Valet parking at Flemington, the hotel valet at Crown, the casual glance from the passenger door — these are all moments where swirl marks and paint oxidation are visible, and this buyer knows it. The urgency is not manufactured. It is the actual social calendar. A buyer searching "paint correction melbourne" in the last week of October is doing so because Melbourne Cup is one week away, not because they stumbled across an ad.

**The pre-ceramic correction window.** October through November is Melbourne's peak ceramic and graphene coating booking period — the buyer who deferred protection work through winter ("I'll do it when the weather warms up") is now booking. Paint correction is the mandatory prerequisite for any high-quality ceramic or graphene coating: applying a coating over uncorrected paint locks in the swirl marks permanently. A buyer who is about to spend $750–$999 on ceramic or graphene needs to know that skipping paint correction first is a mistake they cannot undo. This ad campaign should speak to both the standalone correction buyer and the correction-as-preparation buyer — the latter has a higher total job value (correction + ceramic) and is the highest-priority conversion in the funnel right now.

Toorak is the highest-LTV postcode in the paint correction ICP: high density of Porsche, BMW, Ferrari, and Range Rover owners, established canopy that generates bird etch year-round, and private garages that enable collection and return services. Bid +25% on this term. "Paint restoration" is an alternative term used by buyers who associate correction with the broader goal of restoring their car's finish rather than the specific technique. This keyword captures the buyer who has already decided to get ceramic or graphene coating and is searching to understand whether paint correction is required first. A buyer who lands on this term and converts has the highest average order value in the paint correction campaign: correction + ceramic (from $750 + correction fee) or correction + graphene (from $999 + correction fee). This term should be treated as a high-value cross-sell entry point, not a standalone correction lead. D2 ("Stage prep before ceramic or graphene") is the description to surface for this keyword.

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[diy paint correction]
[cheap paint correction]
[touch up paint melbourne]
```

**Rationale:**

- `[diy paint correction]` — wrong intent entirely. The buyer searching "DIY paint correction" is looking for a product (a dual-action polisher, a correction compound, a foam pad kit) that they will use themselves. They are not in the market for a professional service. Showing Pristine's studio service ad to this buyer wastes the click budget and generates zero bookings. The term also implies a buyer who is not in the ICP: the Toorak or Bayside car owner who protects a $100K+ car does not correct the paint themselves — the risk of making it worse is too high and their time is too valuable.

- `[cheap paint correction]` — direct anti-persona signal. The "cheap" modifier replicates the same exclusion logic used in every Pristine campaign: a buyer optimising for cheapest price is not a Pristine buyer. Paint correction quality is also directly tied to labour time — a "cheap" paint correction is a fast paint correction, which means insufficient compound stages, incorrect pad selection, and a risk of burning through the clear coat. Excluding "cheap" suppresses a buyer who is price-sensitive in a service where cutting corners has permanent consequences.

- `[touch up paint melbourne]` — different service, different product category. A buyer searching "touch up paint" is looking for a chip filler or a colour-matched touch-up pen for a specific stone chip or keying scratch — a product, not a professional service. Touch-up paint is a DIY fix for a specific damage point; paint correction is a multi-stage machine process for the full panel or full car. These are unrelated purchase intents. Showing a paint correction ad to this buyer does not convert and wastes budget.
