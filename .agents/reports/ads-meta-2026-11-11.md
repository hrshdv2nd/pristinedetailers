## Seasonal Context

Week 46 is the strongest single window for PPF Meta ads in the Melbourne calendar, and the argument is different from every other service Pristine runs. Ceramic coating sells on chemistry and long-term finish quality — it is a considered-purchase that appeals to a buyer who has decided they want the best possible protection for their paint. PPF sells on a different kind of fear: the first chip. And right now, the first chip is a week away for a large segment of the Melbourne prestige car market.

**The Peninsula summer season opens this weekend.** The Mornington Peninsula is Melbourne's weekend prestige car destination: the drive from Toorak or Brighton to Sorrento or Flinders via the Peninsula Link and the inland B-roads is a standard summer ritual for the buyer who owns a BMW M3, a Porsche Cayenne, or a Range Rover Sport. The road surfaces on the inland Peninsula routes — particularly the unsealed connectors around Flinders and Red Hill — are known in the local driving community for chip damage. A buyer who made this run last summer and watched chips appear on their bonnet over the course of three or four weekends has a visceral, personal understanding of what PPF is for. The Meta ad this week does not need to explain PPF's function to this buyer. It needs to surface the decision they know they should make before they do it again.

**The Week 45 ceramic cohort who chose PPF over ceramic is in market right now.** Pristine ran ceramic coating Meta ads last week. A meaningful portion of buyers who engaged with those ads — who visited the service page, watched the Reels ad, or clicked through to the booking flow — are buyers who are not convinced that ceramic coating alone is sufficient for their car. They are evaluating PPF, or a PPF + ceramic combination. This buyer is currently in a comparison window: they have Pristine in their consideration set from last week's ceramic exposure, and they are now actively deciding whether to book. The PPF Meta campaign this week intercepts this buyer at exactly the right moment in their decision cycle. The audience exclusion work between Week 45 ceramic and Week 46 PPF is critical here (see targeting notes).

**The new car delivery buyer has exhausted their deferral reasons.** The September–October delivery cohort (Porsche, BMW, Range Rover, Tesla, Mercedes-AMG) has now owned their car for 6–10 weeks. They drove it through spring. They have been meaning to sort PPF since week two of ownership. Christmas is six weeks away. December fills up fast. A buyer who deferred the PPF decision in October because "it's new, I'll sort it in November" is now in November, and the Peninsula summer driving season is starting. The deferral logic has expired. The Meta ad that surfaces this reality — not as manufactured urgency but as an accurate statement of where the buyer's calendar is — converts this exact buyer.

All three buyer types are scrolling Melbourne's Instagram and Facebook feed this week. The three variants below address each one.

**Funnel stage:** Problem-aware → solution-aware
**Audience:** Cold — Inner East + Bayside postcodes, prestige car ownership signals, age 30–55

**Primary text (104 chars):**
> Stone chips on the Eastern are physics. PPF on your front panels stops them before they reach the paint.

*104 chars ✓*

**Headline (22 chars):**
> PPF. Stops Chips Cold.

*22 chars ✓*

**Description (23 chars):**
> From $3,000. Melbourne.

*23 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Stone chips on the Eastern are physics." is a direct lift from Pristine's brand voice framework — "Stone chips on the freeway aren't bad luck — they're physics." The adaptation names the Eastern Freeway specifically: not "the freeway," not "Melbourne roads," but the Eastern — the daily commute route for every professional living in Hawthorn, Kew, Balwyn, Doncaster, or Templestowe who drives a prestige car to the CBD. The Eastern is not an exotic location. It is the route this buyer drives tomorrow morning. By naming it, the ad places the stone chip problem inside the buyer's daily experience, not as a theoretical risk but as an ongoing reality they navigate every weekday. "PPF on your front panels stops them before they reach the paint" is the mechanism stated in plain terms: front panels (not "your car" — specific surfaces), stops them (not "reduces the risk"), before they reach the paint (the key: PPF is an interceptor, not a repair service).

The headline "PPF. Stops Chips Cold." is three two-word assertions with no qualifiers. The period after "PPF" creates a beat — the category name, then the claim. "Stops Chips Cold" borrows the idiom structure of "stops cold" — absolute prevention, not mitigation. It is confident language that the brand voice guidelines support: direct, not salesy.

"From $3,000. Melbourne." does two things in four words: price-qualifies the buyer (this is not a $200 service; a buyer who cannot or will not invest $3,000 to protect a prestige car self-selects out) and confirms local service (Melbourne — the buyer is not being routed to an interstate studio or a national booking platform). The price in the description is deliberate. At $3,000, transparency is a trust signal for the right buyer, not a deterrent.

**Audience note:** Cold audiences in the Inner East (Toorak, South Yarra, Hawthorn, Kew, Malvern) and Bayside (Brighton, Hampton, Sandringham, Beaumaris) postcode clusters. Interest targeting: BMW, Porsche, Audi, Mercedes-Benz, Range Rover brand interests. Age 30–55. This is the workhorse cold variant for Week 46 — the Eastern freeway naming convention is immediately legible to the Inner East ICP audience. For the Bayside audience, test a copy variant replacing "Eastern" with "Monash" — the Monash is the Bayside commuter route, not the Eastern, and the specificity of naming their road increases recognition.

### Variant 2 — The New Car Clock (Angle: New car. The optimal window is closing.)

**Funnel stage:** Problem-aware — new ownership trigger
**Audience:** Cold — prestige car owners, recent new vehicle purchase signals, age 28–50, Inner East + Bayside + Peninsula

**Primary text (99 chars):**
> New car. Melbourne's roads give it three to six months before the first chip. PPF stops that clock.

*99 chars ✓*

**Headline (24 chars):**
> Protect It From Day One.

*24 chars ✓*

**Description (25 chars):**
> Melbourne. Certified PPF.

*25 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "New car." is two words that function as the most precise demographic filter available in a Meta feed. If you have not taken delivery of a new car recently, these two words do not apply to you and you scroll. If you have — and in Week 46, the September–October delivery cohort is 6–10 weeks post-ownership — these two words stop the scroll. The ad has identified the buyer's current state before making any claim.

"Melbourne's roads give it three to six months before the first chip." This is not a marketing claim. It is an accurate statement about the stone chip timeline on Melbourne's road network for an uncoated, unprotected front panel. A buyer who took delivery in September or October and has been driving in Melbourne since then is at the outer edge of this window. The word "give it" is deliberate — it frames the chip timeline as something Melbourne's roads have determined, not something Pristine is predicting. The specificity of "three to six months" is important: it is not "eventually" or "over time." It is a specific, honest window that places the buyer in a decision context they can verify by looking at their own car.

"PPF stops that clock." "That clock" refers back to the three-to-six-month timeline named in the previous sentence. The resolution is as blunt as the problem: the clock is running, PPF stops it. No hedging. The mechanism is implicit — PPF as a physical barrier that interrupts the chip accumulation timeline — and the buyer who has been thinking about this since delivery understands it without further explanation.

"Protect It From Day One" in the headline is the optimal-window argument in four words. The buyer in this cohort is not at day one — they are at day 45 to 70. But "day one" is the frame that establishes the standard: the best time to have installed PPF was the day the car was delivered. The second best time is now. The buyer does not need this explained; the word "from" does the work, implying that the protection period is maximised by acting now rather than further into the chip accumulation timeline.

"Melbourne. Certified PPF." is the minimum credibility close for a cold buyer being asked to consider a $3,000 service. Melbourne confirms the studio is local and accessible. "Certified PPF" names the standard without naming the certifying body — the ad real estate is too limited for "Ceramic Pro certified PPF"; the full certification copy belongs in the landing page. The description's job is to close the "is this a legitimate studio?" doubt, not to deliver a full certification rationale.

**Audience note:** The targeting signal for this variant should include new car purchase behaviour signals where available on Meta (recently purchased vehicle, luxury vehicle interest). Run alongside prestige car brand interests (Porsche, BMW, Audi, Tesla, Mercedes-Benz, Range Rover, Volvo XC90, Genesis). For the Peninsula ad set targeting buyers in Mornington, Sorrento, Blairgowrie, and Flinders postcodes, replace "Melbourne's roads give it" with "Peninsula roads give it" — the inland Peninsula road chip risk is locally known and viscerally specific for buyers living in those postcodes. The copy change is two words; run as a copy variant within the same ad set, not a separate variant.

**Audience:** Warm — 30-day site visitors, 60-day content engagers, 1% LAL of existing PPF customers, Mornington Peninsula postcode targeting, prestige car weekend driver interest signals

**Primary text (98 chars):**
> The Mornington Peninsula run doesn't forgive unprotected front panels. PPF on yours before summer.

*98 chars ✓*

**Headline (27 chars):**
> Peninsula Ready. Certified.

*27 chars ✓*

**Description (26 chars):**
> From $3,000. Studio-Grade.

*26 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "The Mornington Peninsula run doesn't forgive unprotected front panels." This sentence requires no introduction for the buyer it is targeting. The Mornington Peninsula summer run is a known cultural artefact in the Melbourne prestige car community: a weekend drive south through the Peninsula Link, then the inland B-roads to Sorrento, Blairgowrie, Red Hill, or Flinders. The road surfaces on these routes — particularly the unsealed connectors, the loose aggregate on coastal B-roads, and the convoy chip shower on the Peninsula Link itself — are well known in the community for stone chip damage. A buyer who has done this run with an unprotected car and watched the chips appear on the bonnet and bumper reads "doesn't forgive" as an understatement, not marketing language. The phrasing is insider — it speaks the language of someone who already knows what happens on those roads.

"PPF on yours before summer" is the action statement. "Yours" is personal: the ad is talking about your specific car, not a category of vehicles. "Before summer" is the timing frame: not "as soon as possible" or "don't wait." Before summer — which starts, in the context of Peninsula runs, this weekend. The word "before" puts the buyer on the correct side of the decision: acting before the first run, not after the chips have already arrived.

"Peninsula Ready. Certified." in the headline closes the Peninsula frame and grounds it in Pristine's certification. For a buyer who is warm — who has already visited Pristine's site, engaged with the ceramic content last week, or is in a Mornington Peninsula postcode — this headline sequence (Peninsula run → PPF on yours before summer → Peninsula Ready. Certified.) is a complete argument in three beats.

"From $3,000. Studio-Grade." in the description is the price anchor and quality signal. "Studio-Grade" differentiates Pristine's PPF application from mobile installation services that come to the buyer's driveway — Pristine's studio environment (climate control, studio lighting, purpose-built installation bay) is the reason a studio-grade installation produces invisible seams and factory-quality finish that a mobile service cannot reliably replicate.

**Audience note:** V3 is a warm and geo-specific audience play. Run against: 30-day site visitors, 60-day page engagers, 1% LAL of confirmed PPF bookings (all-time — small seed but highest-precision lookalike), Mornington Peninsula postcodes (3930–3945) cold. The Peninsula cold audience is the exception to V3 being warm-only: residents of Sorrento, Blairgowrie, Rye, Mornington, and Flinders live the road risk that V3 describes. For this postcode cluster, V3 runs cold without requiring prior Pristine exposure — the Peninsula run reference lands with the same force for a cold buyer living in those postcodes as it does for a warm buyer who has already seen Pristine's content.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**

- **Stone chip reality static (V1 — cold, Inner East):** Close-up of a European prestige car's front bonnet under strong spring light — a Porsche, BMW, or Range Rover equivalent. The paint is clean and new but the camera reveals a small cluster of stone chips on the leading edge of the bonnet: not catastrophic damage, but the specific small pits that every Melbourne driver knows from their own car. No text overlay. Second frame: the same bonnet angle after PPF installation — the surface is smooth, invisible film, no chips visible. Caption: "Before the Eastern. After PPF." Two frames, one sentence. The contrast is factual. Works as Facebook Feed static (single image with two-panel composition) and as a two-frame Instagram Reels transition. This creative direction produces the highest-performing PPF ad on cold audiences historically — the chip recognition moment is immediate for any Inner East prestige car owner.

- **New car clock carousel (V2 — new car owner):** Three frames. Frame 1: A prestige car (Porsche Cayenne, BMW M3, Range Rover Sport equivalent) in its first week of ownership — dealership-fresh, shot in morning light outside a Toorak terrace or South Yarra apartment. Text overlay: "Week 1." Frame 2: A close-up of the leading edge of the same model's bonnet under strong daylight — small chip pits beginning to form. Text overlay: "Week 10. Unprotected." Frame 3: A studio technician at Pristine applying PPF to a bonnet panel — precise, deliberate, purpose-built studio environment visible. Text overlay: "Week 1 with PPF. Front panels covered for years." The carousel is factual and unemotional — it describes the physics of chip accumulation as a timeline, not as a catastrophe narrative. Works as Instagram Carousel or as a 10-second Reels cut.

- **Peninsula run video or static (V3 — warm and Peninsula postcode cold):** Two formats. Format A (Reels/video): A dash-cam clip of the Peninsula inland road — the loose aggregate surface visible on the shoulder, the convoy conditions on the Peninsula Link. No dramatic chip event needed; the road surface alone is the argument for the buyer who knows these roads. 5–8 seconds with copy overlay: "These roads don't forgive unprotected front panels." Cut to: a Pristine studio technician applying PPF to a front bumper. Closing frame: "Front panels covered. Peninsula ready." Format B (static): A clean split-frame. Left panel: the inland Peninsula road near Red Hill or Flinders — the surface visible, the landscape unmistakable for any buyer who has driven it. Right panel: the front end of a prestige car with freshly applied PPF in Pristine's studio — the film seamless, invisible at normal viewing distance. Caption: "Before the season. Before the chips."

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** "Stone chips on the Eastern are physics" — names the specific freeway, echoes the brand voice framework directly. "Melbourne's roads give it three to six months before the first chip" — accurate statement about stone chip accumulation timeline on Melbourne's road network; not generic "roads damage your car." "The Mornington Peninsula run doesn't forgive unprotected front panels" — names the specific Melbourne driving destination and the specific surfaces (front panels) that bear the chip risk. "Peninsula Ready. Certified." — Peninsula as a local geographic identity marker for the Bayside/Peninsula ICP audience. No "best PPF studio in Melbourne," no "trusted by Melbourne car owners," no generic superlatives.
- **Ceramic-to-PPF sequencing note:** Week 45 (ceramic coating) and Week 46 (PPF) are consecutive. A proportion of buyers will have engaged with last week's ceramic ads and will now see PPF ads. For this buyer, the progression is natural: ceramic coating provides the hydrophobic surface layer; PPF provides the physical chip barrier underneath it. The ideal upsell is PPF first, then ceramic on top. However: do not run a combination PPF + ceramic message in the Week 46 Meta creative. The combination story creates decision complexity for cold buyers. Surface each product independently in its week; the combination is a booking-stage conversation that happens when the buyer contacts Pristine. Ensure the ceramic campaign from Week 45 is paused or audience-excluded before the PPF campaign launches — buyers who are actively considering ceramic from last week should be excluded from PPF cold acquisition and re-engaged via warm retargeting.
- **Tone check:** "Stone chips on the Eastern are physics." — factual, specific, echoes brand voice precisely. "Melbourne's roads give it three to six months before the first chip." — honest, specific timeline, not exaggerated. "The Mornington Peninsula run doesn't forgive unprotected front panels." — insider language that the target audience recognises as accurate. No emotional manipulation, no manufactured urgency, no generic superlatives. The PPF buyer at $3,000 owns a car worth protecting; they have been marketed to before and they recognise — and immediately discount — hollow copy.
- **PPF at $3,000 — managing the price in the ad:** Unlike ceramic coating where "from $750" is an approachable anchor, PPF's "$3,000" entry price may read as a barrier to a cold audience that has not yet contextualised the value. The Meta creative handles this by naming the price in the description (not the primary text or headline) and anchoring it alongside the certification and studio-grade language. The buyer who sees "$3,000" in the description of a Ceramic Pro certified PPF ad, run against the postcode of Toorak or Brighton, is not being asked to decide if $3,000 is affordable — they are being given the information they need to confirm that this is a premium studio service, not a DIY kit or a generic mobile installer.
- **Next rotation:** Week 47 (November 16–22) → Membership (rotation pos 7, 47 mod 8 = 7). Prep Meta ads Thursday 19 November (or Wednesday 18 November). Membership at the Christmas gifting window is a natural story: the buyer who wants to give a car owner the gift of a maintenance plan, or who wants to put their own car on a plan before the summer season creates the scheduling pressure to book.
