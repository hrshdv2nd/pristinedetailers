Hi [First Name],

Your car's done. The work is finished and it looks the way it should.

But there's one thing worth knowing before you head out: the ceramic coating is still bonding to your clear coat. That process takes about 48–72 hours to complete.

During that window, a few things to avoid:

- **Don't wash it** — not even a rinse. The coating chemistry needs time to cure undisturbed.
- **Avoid rain if you can** — not the end of the world if you can't, but a dry cure is cleaner.
- **Park in the shade where possible** — direct UV on uncured ceramic can affect the finish.

After 72 hours, you'll start to notice what the coating actually does. Water will bead and sheet off instead of sitting on the paint. Pollen, dust, and light grime won't bond the way they did before. The gloss depth will hold.

You don't need to do anything right now. Just drive it and let the chemistry do its work.

If anything looks off in the next few days — a smear, a watermark, anything at all — let us know. We want to hear about it.

## Email 2 — Day 7

**Purpose:** Deliver practical care tips, build credibility as a trusted advisor, introduce the idea of maintenance (plant the seed, don't pitch yet)  
**Send:** 7 days after booking  

**Subject:** One week on — a few things to watch for now that spring's here  
**Preview:** September in Melbourne is one of the hardest months on a car's paint. Here's what's worth knowing.

---

Hi [First Name],

A week since we were at your car. The coating should be fully cured by now and doing what it's supposed to do.

Spring in Melbourne is actually worth paying attention to when it comes to paint care. A few things that matter right now:

**Bird droppings**  
Still the most damaging thing that can land on a car, coating or not. The uric acid in bird excrement is aggressive — in direct sunlight it can etch through a ceramic coating in a few hours. If you see one, rinse it off as soon as you can. Don't wipe it dry.

**Tree sap and pollen**  
The jacarandas and wattles are dropping right now. If sap lands on your car, rinse first — always. A dry wipe drags it across the surface. Even with a ceramic barrier underneath, sap can haze if it bakes on for a day or two.

**Washing**  
You can wash the car now. The coating is fully cured. Two-bucket method if you're doing it yourself. Avoid automatic brush washes — the bristles introduce micro-scratches over time, and it compounds every time.

If you'd rather not have to think about any of this, that's what our maintenance detail is for. We'll cover that in a few weeks.

For now — just keep an eye on those droppings.

— Casey
