## Article Body (Markdown)

The best time to get a ceramic coating in Melbourne is August through September — ambient temperatures of 10–22°C create ideal curing conditions, and treating winter grime before it bonds permanently saves paint correction costs. If you've been putting this off, August is the window.

Melbourne winters are rough on paint. Not dramatically — this isn't rust country — but the accumulation is real. Six months of wet roads, Bayside salt air, and bird droppings sitting on cold clear coat build up to a surface that needs more than a wash to reset. By late August, most cars have earned a proper decontamination.

Here's what winter actually does to Melbourne paint:

| Threat | Why it accumulates in winter | Suburb risk |
|--------|------------------------------|-------------|
| Road grime and mineral deposits | Wet roads throw mineral-rich water onto lower panels, bonding to clear coat within 48 hours | All suburbs, worst in CBD and inner ring |
| Bird droppings | Cold slows evaporation — droppings sit longer and etch deeper into unprotected clear coat | Inner East: Hawthorn, Toorak, Richmond |
| Salt air corrosion | Sea-spray from Port Phillip Bay hits Bayside cars throughout the cooler months | Brighton, St Kilda, Elwood, Mordialloc |
| Brake dust and tar spots | More peak-hour traffic in shorter days means more brake events and more contamination per km | Everywhere, worst on freeway commuters |

## Why the August window matters for ceramic coating

Ceramic coatings need temperature to cure properly. The nano-ceramic bond forms as solvents flash off and the product hardens against the clear coat — a process that's optimal between 10°C and 22°C. That's exactly where Melbourne sits from mid-August through September.

Get coated in February and you're fighting 35°C days that flash solvents too fast, compromising adhesion. Wait until November and you're competing with half of Melbourne who had the same idea — technician availability tightens, and your car goes into peak UV season without protection. August gives you ideal curing conditions, pre-season availability, and a fresh hydrophobic barrier in place before the hottest, most bird-dropping-heavy months of the year.

There's a secondary benefit worth naming: cars treated now also avoid the spring UV rush. The UV index in Melbourne starts climbing sharply from October. A coating applied in August has a full cure cycle behind it before that load arrives.

## The right order: decontaminate, correct, coat, protect

Throwing a ceramic coating over winter contamination wastes your investment. The correct sequence is:

1. **Decontamination** — iron fallout remover and clay bar to strip bonded contamination. This is not optional before any coating application.
2. **Paint correction** — winter washes (especially drive-throughs) leave swirl marks. A one- or two-stage machine polish before coating locks in clarity, not damage.
3. **Ceramic coating** — applied panel by panel to a clean, corrected surface for maximum bond strength and longevity.
4. **PPF on high-impact zones** — if stone chips are a concern (they should be for any Melbourne freeway commuter), polyurethane film on the front bumper, bonnet, and mirrors goes on before ceramic on those panels.

The order matters: PPF first on zones you're filming, ceramic on top and across the rest of the car. Reversing this wastes product and adhesion.

## Spring decontamination for Mornington Peninsula and Bayside cars

If you're in Brighton, Hampton, Mordialloc, or anywhere along Port Phillip Bay, winter has been harder on your paint than it has been on an inner-city car. Persistent salt air from the bay doesn't just sit on the surface — it works into panel edges and attacks unprotected clear coat over months. Before UV season kicks in, a full decontamination and fresh sealant is the minimum your car needs.

For Mornington Peninsula drivers, add road grime from unsealed Peninsula Link surrounds and longer commute chip exposure — the case for PPF on the front end becomes straightforward before spring road trip season begins.
