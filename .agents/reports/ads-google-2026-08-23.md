## Seasonal Context

Late August sits at the hinge point between Melbourne's winter and spring — and it is the single most productive window to sell an ongoing membership to a car owner.

**The "winter accumulation" buyer.** Winter in Melbourne runs from June through August. For car owners without a maintenance plan, this means five months of accumulated grime, water spots from road spray, contamination from wet car parks, and the gradual deterioration of any existing sealant layer applied before the cold weather set in. By the last week of August, the observable difference between a maintained car and an unmaintained one is at its annual peak. The owner who looks at their car on a Sunday and feels vaguely embarrassed by it is exactly the buyer the Membership ad should reach.

**The "spring setup" buyer.** Melbourne's social calendar shifts in September: AFL Grand Final season, spring racing (Caulfield Cup, Cox Plate, Melbourne Cup prep), the return of weekend drives to the Peninsula. A car owner in Toorak or Brighton who wants their Range Rover or Porsche looking right for the Birdcage or a Saturday run to Sorrento starts thinking about maintenance in the last week of August. The Membership is the product for this buyer — not because of a one-off clean, but because a recurring plan gets the car in continuous condition heading into the events season.

**The "inertia breaker" buyer.** The most common prospect for Membership is the owner who has been meaning to book a detail for months and hasn't. They've said "I'll get around to it" since June. The Membership proposition breaks the inertia loop: instead of requiring them to act every time, they set it up once and it runs. The search ad for this buyer doesn't need to sell the detail — it needs to sell the "never think about it again" outcome.

These three audiences each require a slightly different copy angle. The ad groups below address them.

## Keyword Recommendations

### Exact Match — 5 Keywords

```
[car detailing membership Melbourne]
[monthly car detailing Melbourne]
[mobile car detailing subscription Melbourne]
[regular car detailing Melbourne]
[car care plan Melbourne]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[car detailing membership Melbourne]` — primary intent term; buyer has decided the format (subscription/recurring) and is comparing providers; must own this term as the anchor keyword for the entire campaign
- `[monthly car detailing Melbourne]` — searches this term without the word "membership" — likely a buyer who wants ongoing care but has not yet formed a mental model around "membership"; this term catches the pre-membership prospect who is still framing the desire as "book monthly" rather than "sign up"
- `[mobile car detailing subscription Melbourne]` — mobile qualifier + subscription framing; the most precise match for Pristine's core product; lower search volume but highest intent-to-product fit; the buyer who types all three words is already at decision stage
- `[regular car detailing Melbourne]` — broad intent term for the inertia buyer who has not formed a specific product desire; they want their car detailed regularly but haven't connected that to a membership model; this term captures early-funnel membership intent before the buyer knows the word "membership"
- `[car care plan Melbourne]` — catches the "plan" framing common among prestige car owners who think in maintenance plans (they already have service plans with their dealer); "car care plan" mirrors the language of a buyer who equates our membership to a dealer's maintenance contract

---

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[car wash near me]
[DIY car detailing]
[one off car detail]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[car wash near me]` — low-intent commodity searcher looking for a drive-through wash; incompatible with the Membership's $99/mo price point and professional technician model; will click, see the price, and bounce
- `[DIY car detailing]` — explicit self-service intent; buyer is not looking for someone to come to them; opposite of Pristine's proposition; spending against this term is a direct subsidy of a non-customer
- `[one off car detail]` — transactional intent without subscription interest; this buyer wants a single job completed, not an ongoing plan; they are a candidate for a one-off service booking but not for the Membership landing page conversion; route them to the main services page if that intent appears organically, not via this campaign

## Retargeting Note

For users who visited `/membership` or `/booking` (with membership plan selected) but did not complete:

> **Headline:** Your Membership Quote Is Waiting
> **Description:** Essential from $99/mo. Signature from $149/mo. We come to your home or office. Set up today.

Window: 7-day hot retargeting.
Frequency cap: 3 impressions/week.

Message angle: The Membership prospect who reached the booking page is not undecided on the concept — they've already evaluated the product. The barrier is friction in commitment. The retargeting message does not fabricate urgency; it reduces the perceived commitment barrier ("quote" instead of "sign up") and re-anchors the price tier split ($99/$149) to help the buyer pick a lane rather than abandon the choice entirely. The phrase "set up today" is chosen deliberately: "set up" implies a one-time action after which the membership runs itself — it reinforces the core product benefit in the retargeting step.

## Context Notes

- **Seasonal timing:** Late August is the spring transition window. Cars that weren't maintained over winter are visibly neglected. Melbourne's social calendar picks up in September (AFL Grand Final, spring racing, Peninsula drives). The Membership buyer in Toorak or Brighton who wants their car in continuous condition for the spring season starts looking in the last two weeks of August — this campaign is timed to that window.
- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** Toorak, Brighton, Mornington Peninsula in geographic targeting; AFL Grand Final / spring racing are implicit seasonal context (not in ad copy — too specific to be broadly relevant — but informing the timing and budget deployment); "we come to you" is the core mobile differentiator relevant to Inner East professionals who cannot drop off at a workshop
- **Tone check:** No superlatives; "set-and-forget" not "hassle-free"; "never miss one" not "consistent service"; "save 35% annually" not "great value"; "we come to you monthly" not "convenient recurring service"; technical pricing named directly ($99/$149) because the Membership buyer needs price clarity to commit, not aspirational copy
- **Membership vs single services:** This ad set does not promote ceramic coating or PPF. The Membership buyer is at a different decision stage than the high-ticket service buyer — they want ongoing maintenance, not a $750–$3,000 one-off. Mixing product messages in a Membership campaign dilutes intent matching and reduces Quality Score for membership-specific terms.
- **Tier strategy note:** D2 mentions "ceramic upkeep included" — this refers to the Signature plan's ceramic maintenance service. This phrase should only appear in ads targeting warm audiences already familiar with ceramic coating, not in cold broad-match contexts. For cold Membership ad groups, D1 is the safer primary description.
