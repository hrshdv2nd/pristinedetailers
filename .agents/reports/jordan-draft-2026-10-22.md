## Melbourne Summer Car Care Guide 2026: Protecting Your Paint from October UV, Heat, and Bird Drops

Melbourne's summer UV — particularly for bayside suburbs like Brighton, Sandringham, and Mordialloc — is among the most damaging in Australia. A ceramic or graphene coating significantly reduces UV-induced paint oxidation but requires a specific maintenance routine from October through February: monthly wash, bi-annual spray sealant, and immediate attention to bird dropping acidity.

## What Melbourne Summer Does to Unprotected Paint

A UV index of 11+ is classified as extreme — and Melbourne hits it routinely between November and February. On a dark-coloured panel parked outdoors in direct sun, surface temperature reaches 70–80°C. That's the environment your clear coat is sitting in, every day from October through to March.

Here's what accumulates across Melbourne suburbs from October to February:

| Threat | Why It's Worse in Summer | Suburb Risk |

| UV intensity | UV index 11+ from Nov–Feb; bay reflection amplifies load | Brighton, Sandringham, Mordialloc — highest |

## The 10-Minute Rule for Bird Drops

Bird droppings carry a pH of 3–4 — roughly the acidity of vinegar. In winter, you might have 24 hours before that acid starts etching your clear coat. In Melbourne summer, on a panel heated to 50–60°C by direct sun, the same dropping bakes in under 10 minutes.

The result is a frosted etch mark in the clear coat that doesn't buff out. Fixing it requires machine polishing — a stage-one or stage-two paint correction job. That's a half-day of skilled labour on top of whatever coating work you were already planning.

A ceramic or graphene coating doesn't make your car immune to bird drops. What it does is slow the acid's penetration and make the surface easier to clean. But "slower" in Melbourne summer still means act within the hour — not the next morning.

A spray bottle of quick detailer in the glovebox costs $15 and takes 30 seconds to use. Spray, blot with a microfibre, done. It's the cheapest form of summer paint protection and the most underused.

## Summer Maintenance Schedule by Coating Type

How you maintain your car from October through February depends on what protection is on it:

| Protection | Monthly | Every 3 Months | Every 6 Months |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ceramic coating (5yr+) | Hand wash only | Check water beading performance | Spray sealant top-up |
| Graphene coating (9yr) | Hand wash only | Check water beading performance | Spray sealant top-up |
| PPF only (no coating) | Hand wash | Inspect film edges for lifting | Check for UV yellowing on exposed film |
| No protection | Careful hand wash | Paint inspection | Book a coating before damage compounds |

For coated cars, the summer check is quick: spray water on a panel in the shade. If it beads into tight droplets and rolls off, the coating is performing. If it spreads flat, the hydrophobic layer has thinned and needs a spray sealant before UV season peaks in January.

PPF without a coating on top does its job stopping stone chips, but without a ceramic or graphene layer on top, the film can yellow under extended extreme UV. The full summer setup is PPF on high-impact zones — front bumper, bonnet leading edge, mirrors — with graphene coating over the top.

For unprotected cars: UV oxidation doesn't happen in one afternoon. It accumulates month by month. By the time it's visible as chalking or colour fade, you're looking at paint correction before any coating will bond properly.
