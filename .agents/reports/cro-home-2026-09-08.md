## Issue 1 — `FAQSection`: `[SHOP ADDRESS]` placeholder is live in production AND contradicts the page's core premise

**Element:** `FAQSection` → first FAQ answer, line 818  
**Current copy:**
> "Detailing (full detail, ceramic coating, PPF, interior care) is completed at our studio - [SHOP ADDRESS]. The one exception is window tinting, which our mobile team installs at your home or office."

**Problem (two separate failures):**

**A — The placeholder is unfilled.** `[SHOP ADDRESS]` renders literally in the browser. A visitor who expands this FAQ sees a template variable. On a premium page targeting owners of $50K–$200K cars, this signals an unfinished website and erodes the trust you've been building since the hero.

**B — The message directly contradicts the site's positioning.** The homepage headline, the `MarqueeStrip` brand logos, the `MelbourneSection` ("we serve car owners across East and South East Melbourne"), and the product-marketing-context all lead with mobile as the core differentiator. The FAQ's first answer tells visitors the opposite: every high-ticket service (ceramic, PPF, full detail) is done at a fixed studio. A visitor who landed from "mobile car detailing Melbourne" will read this and either feel misled or confused about what they're actually booking.

This isn't just a copy problem — it's a positioning problem. If ceramic and PPF require a dust-controlled studio environment (which is legitimate), the page cannot continue to open with "mobile" as its hero value proposition without qualifying it. Right now the page promises mobile, buries studio in FAQ answer 1, and the FAQ answer has a placeholder. All three are conversion killers.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Immediate (10 min):** Fill in `[SHOP ADDRESS]` with the actual studio address.

2. **This week (1 hr):** Resolve the mobile/studio contradiction proactively — don't leave it to FAQ. Two options:

   - **Option A (honest positioning):** Add a brief explainer near the hero or in the `ServicesPreview` section: "Mobile to your driveway: maintenance details, window tinting. Studio-applied for best results: ceramic coating, PPF, full detail." This sets expectation before the visitor builds false intent.

   - **Option B (if truly mobile):** Remove the FAQ answer's studio framing entirely and clarify how ceramic and PPF are applied mobile. If this requires a dust-controlled environment that only exists at a studio, Option A is correct.

## Issue 2 — Hero subtext claims "5,000+ car owners" — contradicts every other proof point on the site

**Element:** `HeroSection` → `<p>` at line 90–92  
**Current copy:**
> "Precision grade detailing and paint protection helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."

**Problem:**

The product marketing context states "2,400+ cars detailed." The Melbourne section (`MelbourneSection`) and testimonials use language consistent with a 6-year, 2,400-car operation. The hero now claims 5,000+ — more than double. A visitor who is already sceptical ("I don't know who to trust with a car like this") and who reads two different customer count claims across a single page has a reasonable basis to distrust both.

Additionally, "Precision grade" is exactly the kind of generic superlative the brand voice guidelines call out for removal: no proof attached, no differentiation from any other detailer who'd also call themselves "precision grade."

**Recommendation:**

Fix the number first. Change "5,000+ car owners" to "2,400+ Melbourne cars" (or update the product context to reflect the correct current number — either is fine, inconsistency is not).

Then apply the quick-win rewrite below, which removes "precision grade" and fixes the number in one edit.

## Quick Win — Hero subtext rewrite (flagged June 16 and July 14, still unshipped)

**Element:** `HeroSection` → `<p>` at lines 90–92

**Before:**
> "Precision grade detailing and paint protection helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."

**After:**

**Why this converts better:**
- "Mobile" appears in the first three words — confirms message match for traffic landing from "mobile car detailing Melbourne"

- "Certified Melbourne technicians" directly addresses the objection "How can mobile be as good as a fixed studio?"
- Removes "Precision grade" self-congratulation with no proof, and corrects the 5,000+ number

This rewrite has now been recommended across three audits. It is a 15-minute implementation and the highest single-line ROI change on the page. If there's a reason it's not shipping, I need to know what it is.

## Bigger Bet — Test hero that resolves the mobile/studio tension rather than hiding it

**Background:**  
Issue 1 surfaces a structural problem: the page leads with mobile but delivers a studio experience for the two highest-ticket services (ceramic, PPF). This isn't caught in the hero — it's caught in FAQ answer 1. Visitors ready to book a $999–$7,900 service shouldn't discover the delivery mode in the FAQ.

**Hypothesis:**  
A hero section that proactively names both delivery modes — mobile for maintenance and tinting, studio for ceramic and PPF — will increase booking intent for high-ticket services by setting correct expectations at the top of the funnel, rather than filtering them out mid-consideration in the FAQ.

**Control (current):**  
Hero positions entirely around mobile. No mention of studio. Visitor discovers studio requirement in FAQ.

**Variant:**  
Hero subtext (or an added trust chip below the headline) reads:
> "We come to you for maintenance and tinting. Ceramic and PPF are applied in our dust-controlled studio — same result, without the drop-off."

**What to measure:**
- Primary: Booking clicks on the hero CTA (segmented: ceramic/PPF enquiries vs. maintenance bookings)
- Secondary: FAQ expansion rate for "Do you come to my home or office?" (lower = expectation set earlier = variant working)
- Tertiary: Bounce rate from visitors who expand the FAQ and then exit

**Why this is a bet:**  
It could reduce the perceived mobile convenience that draws initial interest. It may also increase conversion quality — visitors who book after seeing the studio requirement are more qualified leads for high-ticket services.

Test duration: minimum 4 weeks, minimum 500 sessions per variant.
