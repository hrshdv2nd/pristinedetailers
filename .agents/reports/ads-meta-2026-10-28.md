## Seasonal Context

Late October in Melbourne is the optimal window to run paint correction ads for two reasons that compound each other.

**The sunlight reveal effect.** Melbourne's winter (June–September) hides swirl marks. The combination of low sun angle, overcast skies, and pre-dawn commutes means a car owner can drive a vehicle with significant paint damage for months without seeing it clearly. October brings longer days, higher sun angle, and the first extended periods of clear weather. The car that "looked fine" in July is the same car that, parked in a Toorak or Hawthorn driveway in October morning light, shows the spider-web swirl marks from six months of winter car washing. The buyer is not discovering a new problem — they are seeing a problem that existed all winter for the first time. The Meta ad that names this moment ("you can see it now because the sun's back") lands because it describes what the buyer observed this morning, not a hypothetical.

**The Melbourne Cup social deadline (Tuesday 4 November 2026).** The Melbourne Cup is seven days from today. It is the highest-visibility driving event in the Melbourne social calendar for the Toorak, South Yarra, and Brighton ICP. Flemington valet, Swan Street lunch, Crown Hotel arrival — these are moments where the car's paint is visible, and this buyer knows it. The emotional weight of Melbourne Cup for this ICP is not just about the races; it is about being seen in the right way, and a car with swirl marks and water spot etching from six months of winter is not the car this buyer wants to be seen in. The urgency is the buyer's own social calendar. The ad does not need to manufacture a deadline.

The copy across all three variants names different versions of the same underlying state: the car owner who cares about their car's appearance has spent October looking at paint that no longer reflects light the way it did when they bought it, and they want it right before summer begins.

**Why this works:** "October light doesn't lie" is a Melbourne-specific hook that names the exact mechanism of the buyer's discovery — they saw their paint in better light and realised the damage. "Those swirl marks weren't there all winter — they just weren't visible" does two things simultaneously: it normalises the discovery (this is what happens in Melbourne, not because you neglected your car) and it implies the damage is not new, which creates an implicit sense that it should have been addressed sooner. This is not a fear-based close — it is a recognition. The buyer who has swirl marks from a winter car wash feels a moment of recognition, not alarm. That recognition is the moment to convert.

"Paint Correction Melbourne" as the headline is the simplest possible bridge from the problem (swirl marks visible) to the solution (correction). No adjective, no urgency, no offer. The buyer who recognised themselves in the first two sentences clicks because they already know what they need — the headline just confirms Pristine provides it.

**Audience note:** Run cold in Inner East postcodes (3142, 3141, 3122, 3144, 3101) with BMW, Audi, Mercedes-Benz, and Porsche interest signals. This is the workhorse cold variant. The "October light" hook works for any prestige car owner in a suburb with established tree canopy — Toorak and Hawthorn are primary targets. Do not run this variant against buyers who recently enquired about ceramic or graphene coating — route those buyers to Variant 3.

**Funnel stage:** Solution-aware (already considering ceramic coating)
**Audience:** Cold — Bayside cluster (Brighton, Hampton, Sandringham), age 32–55, Tesla/Range Rover/BMW interest targeting; also retarget: ceramic coating page visitors who haven't booked

**Primary text (under 125 chars):**
> Most cars aren't ready for ceramic. Paint correction first — swirl marks locked in under a coating last years, not months.

*122 chars ✓*

**Headline (27 chars):**
> Paint Correct. Then Coat.

*25 chars ✓*

**Description (27 chars):**
> Certified Techs. Book Now.

*26 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Most cars aren't ready for ceramic" is a technically accurate statement that positions Pristine as the provider who tells the buyer what they need to know, not what is easiest to sell. A car with swirl marks and bird etch that receives a ceramic coating without prior correction does not have those defects removed — they are sealed in under the coating, visible for the full warranty period (up to 8 years for ceramic). This is not a manufactured upsell; it is the correct sequence, and a buyer who has been researching ceramic coating and encounters this ad will recognise the claim as legitimate.

"Swirl marks locked in under a coating last years, not months" adds a consequence that is accurate and specific: the buyer who has spent $750 on ceramic over uncorrected paint has committed to seeing those swirl marks for the duration of the coating's warranty. The cost of skipping correction is not just a worse-looking result — it is a multi-year worse-looking result. This is the close that converts a buyer who was planning to book ceramic without correction into a buyer who books both.

"Paint Correct. Then Coat." in the headline is the most technically direct of the three variants. It is written for the buyer who has enough knowledge to know what both terms mean. This is a solution-aware buyer, not a problem-aware one — the headline respects that prior knowledge and delivers the correct sequence in four words.

**Audience note:** Run on Bayside postcodes (3186, 3187, 3188, 3191) and on ceramic coating page visitor retargeting. Bayside buyers skew slightly older (35–55) and more deliberate — they respond to technical arguments over emotional hooks. The prerequisite framing ("correct first, coat second") is the argument that closes for this audience. Also run against any Melbourne-wide ceramic coating intent audience (people who visited `/services/ceramic-coating` or `/services/graphene-coating` in the last 60 days) — this is the highest-value conversion opportunity in this campaign.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**

- **V1 — Sunlight Reveal static:** Split-frame. Left side: the hood of a dark-coloured car (charcoal or deep navy — colours that show swirl marks most visibly) in flat grey winter light, swirl marks visible as a spider-web pattern in the clear coat. Caption: "Winter." Right side: the same hood panel after a two-stage paint correction, in morning sunlight. The light reveals depth and gloss, not swirl marks. Caption: "October." No branding in the image itself — the before/after tells the story. The two captions are the copy; the visual is the evidence. Dark cars only (darker paint amplifies swirl mark visibility and amplifies the correction result). Never use a white or silver car for a correction before/after — the damage is not visible.

- **V2 — Melbourne Cup static:** Clean typographic layout. A dark field — charcoal or black. In the centre, in large brand-weight typeface: "Melbourne Cup is Tuesday." Below, smaller: "Your paint is either ready or it isn't." Bottom third: Pristine branding and "Book paint correction this week." No car photography needed — the tension is in the deadline, not in a visual. This is a copy-led creative that works on Facebook Feed (where text reads on mobile) and Stories (where fast cuts reward bold text). Colour note: spring racing's traditional palette (greens, pinks, florals) should be absent — this creative is for the car, not the carnival.

- **V3 — Prerequisite infographic (Reels/Carousel):** Frame 1: "Step 1: Paint Correction. Remove swirl marks, bird etch, water spots." — with a close-up of machine polisher on a panel. Frame 2: "Step 2: Ceramic or Graphene Coating. Applied to corrected paint only." — with the coating application step. Frame 3: "Skip Step 1. These are sealed in for 8 years." — a close-up of swirl marks as they would appear under a cured coating in direct light. Frame 4: "Do both. Book at our Melbourne studio." — clean studio shot, booking CTA. The four-frame sequential logic (process → consequence of skipping → correct choice → CTA) is the argument that the V3 buyer needs to see in order to justify booking both services. Carousel format on Facebook; Reels vertical cut on Instagram.

- **Avoid:** Generic stock photography of car washes or polishing rigs not at Pristine's studio; "best paint correction in Melbourne" in any frame; price anchoring in the creative itself (prices belong in the headline and description, not overlaid on an image); any Melbourne Cup branding or official imagery (AFL/racing IP risk — name the event, don't reproduce its mark); any car outside the ICP range in the creative (no hatchbacks, no workhorse utes — only European prestige, Japanese performance, or luxury SUVs)

**Format priority for Paint Correction:**
1. Instagram Feed — V1 (sunlight reveal) as static before/after; the correction result visual is strongest on the Instagram feed grid where detail is visible
2. Facebook Feed — V3 (prerequisite carousel); the older Bayside demographic who is the V3 audience skews Facebook-primary
3. Instagram Reels — V3 as 15-second sequence (three cuts: swirl marks visible → correction → ceramic applied on clean paint); awareness play for buyers not yet in the consideration funnel
4. Stories — V2 retargeting only (Melbourne Cup deadline — the full-screen Stories format amplifies the urgency text treatment)

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Cold Inner East: V1 (sunlight reveal) vs V2 (Cup hook) on same postcode clusters — measure CTR and cost-per-click-to-correction-page; win-in-5-days criterion: >1.8% CTR and <$2.00 CPC (paint correction intent is more specific than membership; a higher CTR threshold is achievable)
- Cold Bayside: V3 (prerequisite) as sole variant — the technical argument needs clean measurement; do not run it against a V1 control in the same postcode cluster or the Cup urgency will dominate spend
- Retargeting: Single variant (retargeting message); rotate to V3 after 2 impressions
- Kill any cold variant at <1.2% CTR after 800 impressions per ad set; consolidate budget to winner within 5 days

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne-specific accuracy:** Flemington valet named explicitly (a real, recognisable context for the ICP); Toorak canopy bird etch is a real and documented problem in 3142; Eastern Freeway commuter swirl marks reference Inner East buyer behaviour accurately; Brighton–Sandringham coastal UV oxidation is factual for the Bayside postcode cluster
- **Tone check:** "October light doesn't lie" — observational, not alarmist. "Most cars aren't ready for ceramic" — factual, expert positioning. "Melbourne Cup's next Tuesday. The swirl marks will be visible in the Flemington valet." — specific and true, not manufactured urgency. No "best," no "passionate," no countdown timer
- **Melbourne Cup seasonal shelf life:** V2 and its retargeting headline retire on Wednesday 5 November. Summer equivalent: "Mornington Peninsula season starts November. Paint correction now." This sustains the same urgency structure (specific upcoming context → visible consequence → book now) through December
- **Correction → coating funnel logic:** This campaign is the entry point to Pristine's highest-value conversion sequence. A paint correction booking at, say, $450 that converts to correction + ceramic ($450 + $750 = $1,200) is the ideal outcome. The V3 copy is designed to trigger this conversion in one booking decision — the buyer sees "correct first, then coat" in the ad, books both, and arrives at the studio having already committed to the full process. This should be tracked as a campaign-level metric: what percentage of paint correction bookings from this campaign also include a ceramic or graphene service?
- **Next rotation:** Week 45 (2–8 November 2026) → Ceramic Coating (week 45 mod 8 = 5). Prep Meta ads for Thursday 5 November. The Ceramic Coating campaign should reference paint correction as the preparatory step (buyers who came through this week's correction ads are now the warm audience for ceramic) — the two campaigns should function as a two-stage funnel, not independent campaigns.
