# Spring Car Care in Melbourne: What to Do in September Before Summer UV Arrives

The best time to protect your car's paint in Melbourne is September — ambient temperatures of 10–22°C are ideal for ceramic coating cure, and treating winter grime before spring UV arrives prevents it bonding permanently to your clear coat.

Melbourne's spring transition looks gentle from the outside. The days lengthen, temperatures climb past 15°C, and suddenly your car is bathed in real sunlight for the first time since April. That's exactly the problem.

Six months of winter grime — road mineral deposits, bird droppings, salt air from Port Phillip Bay — has been building on your clear coat. Low UV and wet roads meant it accumulated slowly without obvious damage. Now, rising UV and heat will bake it in. What was removable in August becomes bonded in November.

September is the window. Here's what to do with it.

![Ceramic coating being applied to a car](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1619767886558-efdc259cde1a?w=1200&q=80)

## What Winter Does to Your Paint

Winter in Melbourne isn't just cold — it's chemically aggressive on paintwork. The threats aren't dramatic; they're slow and cumulative.

| Threat | Why it accumulates in winter | Melbourne suburb risk |
|---|---|---|
| Road mineral deposits | Wet roads kick up calcium and magnesium salts that bond to clear coat within 48 hours | All suburbs — worst for Eastern Freeway, Monash, Eastlink commuters |
| Bird droppings | Slower evaporation in cold means droppings sit longer and etch deeper | Inner East: Hawthorn, Kew, Toorak — dense tree canopy |
| Salt air corrosion | Coastal salt mist carried inland by southerly winds | Bayside: Brighton, Hampton, Black Rock, Sandringham |
| Underground car park abrasion | Concrete dust and narrow bays cause micro-scratches year-round | CBD, South Yarra, Richmond apartment residents |

The damage from any one of these is minor. The damage from six months of all four, concentrated on an unprotected clear coat, is visible on most Melbourne cars by October.

## The Spring Service Sequence

Don't book a polish before a decontamination. Don't apply a coating before a correction. Order matters.

**1. Chemical decontamination**

This removes what a wash can't — bonded mineral deposits, iron particles from brake dust, and organic contamination. Iron fallout from Melbourne's train network settles on paint in the Inner East suburbs year-round. A clay bar or chemical decontamination spray lifts it from the surface before it gets heat-baked in.

**2. Swirl and scratch assessment**

Under spring light, your paint will show what winter hid. Swirl marks from automatic car washes, fine scratches from brush contact, and oxidation patches on horizontal surfaces — bonnet, roof — are all easier to catch in September than after months of UV exposure have worsened them.

**3. Paint correction (if required)**

If the assessment shows moderate to heavy swirling, a one- or two-stage machine polish removes the defects from the clear coat rather than filling them temporarily. Correcting before coating locks in a flawless base — not just a shinier surface.

**4. Ceramic coating**

September temperatures of 10–22°C are close to ideal for professional ceramic coating application. The product bonds to the clear coat during the cure phase — too hot and it flashes off unevenly, too cold and it doesn't cure fully. Melbourne's December is too hot and too busy. September is the window.

**5. PPF on high-impact zones**

For cars that commute on the Eastern Freeway, Eastlink, or Monash, paint protection film on the front bumper, bonnet leading edge, and mirror caps stops stone chips before they reach the paint. Applied after correction, before the summer chip season starts.

## Why September Beats December

Most Melbourne car owners think about paint protection in December, when they're washing the car before a summer road trip and notice the oxidation they missed all year.

Two problems with December timing.

**Booking lead times blow out.** Our calendar typically runs 5–6 weeks ahead by late November. A ceramic coating booked in December often gets installed in February — by which point the car has been through the hottest UV months unprotected.

**Temperature is wrong for coatings.** Ceramic application in 35°C heat on a car that's been sitting in direct sun doesn't cure correctly. We reschedule these jobs in summer, which extends lead times further.

September avoids both problems. Ambient temperatures are right, our calendar has more availability, and your car goes into summer with protection in place — not scheduled for after summer.

## Mornington Peninsula and Bayside: Act Before October

If you park near Port Phillip Bay or drive the Mornington Peninsula regularly, decontamination before UV season isn't optional — it's urgent.

Salt air from the bay deposits microscopic chloride particles on paintwork that look like nothing until UV-driven oxidation sets in. Cars in Brighton, Sandringham, Black Rock, and along the Peninsula need a full decontamination and protection application before October, not after.
