# CRO Audit — Homepage
**Analyst:** Riley (CRO)  
**Date:** 2026-08-11  
**File:** `components/pages/home.tsx`  
**Cycle week:** 33 (Week 1 → Homepage)

---

## Context

The homepage receives cold traffic from local organic search ("mobile car detailing Melbourne", "ceramic coating Melbourne", "PPF Melbourne"). Primary conversion goal is booking (Setmore link), secondary is membership sign-up. Visitors are time-poor professionals and car enthusiasts who value quality + convenience and are price-insensitive if trust is established.

---

## Issue 1 — Hero headline fails the 5-second clarity test

**Element:** `HeroSection` → `<h1>` (lines 82–89)  
**Current copy:** *"Where your car meets its best self."*

**Problem:** A cold visitor from Google lands here and can't determine within 5 seconds that this is a *mobile* service (the primary differentiator), that it operates in *Melbourne*, or that it handles anything beyond a basic wash. The eyebrow pill at line 53 says "Melbourne · Car Detailing Service" but at 12px it's decorative, not load-bearing. The subtext at line 91 doesn't mention mobile either — it reads "Precision grade detailing and paint protection helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."

Two compounding problems with the subtext: (1) "precision grade" is the exact kind of generic superlative the brand voice guidelines flag as to-be-avoided; (2) the product-marketing-context.md states 2,400+ cars detailed, but the page claims 5,000+ car owners — a 2× inflation that will erode trust with any visitor who cross-references a review site.

**Recommendation:** Rewrite the H1 to lead with the mobile/come-to-you differentiator — the thing no fixed studio can match. Kill "best self" entirely. Correct the 5,000+ claim to 2,400+.

---

## Issue 2 — Live `[SHOP ADDRESS]` placeholder in FAQ actively destroys trust

**Element:** `FAQSection` → first FAQ item (line 818)  
**Current answer text:**  
> *"Detailing (full detail, ceramic coating, PPF, interior care) is completed at our studio - [SHOP ADDRESS]. The one exception is window tinting, which our mobile team installs at your home or office."*

**Problem:** This is the most damaging issue on the page. Two separate failures:

1. `[SHOP ADDRESS]` is an unfilled template placeholder rendering live on the site. Any visitor who opens this FAQ sees it. This signals the site is unfinished and untrustworthy — exactly the anxiety ("is this legit or will they disappear after one job?") the brand needs to eliminate, not amplify.

2. The content itself directly contradicts the homepage's entire value proposition. The hero, Melbourne section, and product-marketing-context.md all position Pristine Detailers as a mobile, come-to-you service. This FAQ answer says most services require a studio drop-off — which is the problem Pristine Detailers exists to solve for customers.

**Recommendation:** This is an emergency fix, not a CRO consideration. The FAQ answer needs to be rewritten immediately to reflect the actual service model per the product marketing context. If the business truly requires studio visits for ceramic/PPF (which the MelbourneSection copy contradicts — "studio-grade detailing brought to your driveway"), the entire value proposition needs an urgent audit. Either way: kill the placeholder today.

## Issue 3 — "Get a Free Quote" CTA routes to /services, not a quote form

**Element:** `CTASection` → secondary CTA button (lines 921–936)  
**Current:** Button labelled "Get a Free Quote" → `href="/services"`

**Problem:** A visitor clicking "Get a Free Quote" expects a form or at minimum a contact page. Instead they land on the services page — a browse experience, not a quote flow. This is a classic friction mismatch: the CTA raises an expectation the destination doesn't fulfil. High-value buyers (ceramic coating, PPF — $750–$7,900 jobs) who want a quote are the most important segment to capture and they're being dropped into general browsing.

Secondary issue: the Gallery "View gallery" link in `GallerySection` line 776 is `href="#"` — a dead link. Visitors who click it to see more work go nowhere.

**Recommendation:** Either (a) create a `/quote` or `/contact` page with a short enquiry form (name, car make, service interested in, suburb) and update the href; or (b) change the CTA copy to "Explore services →" to match what the link actually does. Option (a) is strongly preferred — enquiry capture for high-ticket jobs is the tertiary conversion goal per the business context.

## Quick Win — Rewrite the hero subtext (exact copy change)

**Component:** `HeroSection` → `<p>` at line 91

**Before:**
> *"Precision grade detailing and paint protection helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."*

**After:**
> *"We come to you — mobile detailing and paint protection, certified technicians, no drop-off required. Serving Melbourne's Inner East and Bayside suburbs."*

**Why it wins:** Leads with the #1 differentiator (mobile/come-to-you), removes the inflated social proof number, drops the generic "precision grade" qualifier, and adds the geographic anchor that local search traffic is scanning for. Directly mirrors the customer language in the product context: "I hate having to drop it off somewhere."

---

## Bigger Bet — A/B test the H1 against a specificity-led alternative

**Hypothesis:** Replacing the brand-clever headline with a specificity-led one that names the mobile differentiator will increase booking CTA clicks from cold organic traffic.

**Control:** *"Where your car meets its best self."* (current)

**Variant:** *"Melbourne's mobile detail service. We come to you — studio results, no workshop drop-off."*

**Rationale:** The current H1 could work for warm/referral traffic who already know the brand. Cold organic visitors need explicit category confirmation first. The variant passes the "billboard test" (a stranger reading it for 3 seconds understands what you do and why it's different), the control does not.

**Measurement:** Track primary CTA clicks ("Book a Detail") per session, segmented by organic search traffic only. Run for minimum 2 weeks / 200 sessions per variant.
