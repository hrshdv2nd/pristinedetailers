**If your car has ceramic or graphene coating:**

Winter is a coating stress test. It also leaves something behind.

Brake dust and iron particles from Melbourne's roads bond to paintwork during the cold months. They show up as reddish-brown speckling in low-angle light — you might have noticed it and assumed it was dirt. It's not. It's metallic contamination embedded in the coating surface, and it doesn't come off with a wash.

Left untreated going into a Melbourne spring, it starts to work against the hydrophobic layer — the part of your coating that sheds water and contaminants. The coating itself is still there. But the surface on top of it is compromised.

A spring decontamination service removes the iron deposits, restores the surface, and confirms the coating is still performing properly before summer UV hits hard. If there's any edge-seal lift on PPF, minor surface oxidation, or sections where the hydrophobic layer has thinned, we'll find it now — while it's easy to address — not in February when the damage has set in.

## Copy Notes

**Why this works:**

- **The UV insight earns trust.** "Melbourne's UV index roughly doubles between August and November" is specific, true, and something most people haven't thought about. It's not a scare tactic — it's a fact that reframes the season. A reader who learns something new trusts the sender more. That's the first job of a retention email.

- **Iron contamination is the right spring hook.** It's visible (the reddish speckling in low-angle light is something coated car owners have seen and not known what to do about), it's specific to Melbourne roads, and it creates a logical reason to book — even for people who feel like their coating is "fine." The point isn't that their coating has failed. It's that the surface on top of it needs maintenance. That's a meaningful distinction.

- **The chemistry argument is why October beats January.** Telling customers that January is bad for coating application isn't positioning — it's proof that Pristine actually cares about the result. A shop that turns bookings away because conditions are wrong is a shop you can trust with your car. The framing reinforces the premium positioning without ever using the word "premium."

- **The "window" frame creates real urgency.** It's not a manufactured deadline. October fills up every year. Coating in January is genuinely worse. The urgency is structural, not invented — and readers sense the difference.

- **Two segments, one email.** The middle section addresses coated cars and uncoated cars separately. Each reader skips to the paragraph relevant to them. The other half still reinforces the brand's expertise and authority.

- **Reply CTA is doing real work.** "Reply with what you're driving and when you last had it treated" removes the pressure of committing to a booking. It converts undecided readers into warm conversations — and those conversations close at a much higher rate than cold clicks.

- **Pricing is included deliberately.** Customers who've been considering ceramic or graphene often stall because they can't remember the price and don't want to start a formal enquiry just to find out. Listing it removes that friction without making the email feel like a price list.

**What this email is NOT doing:**
- Not repeating the salt/condensation/mould angles from June and July sends
- Not using fear about damage that has already happened (that was the winter sends)
- Not pitching to the win-back segment (they received a separate sequence last week — suppress from this broadcast)
