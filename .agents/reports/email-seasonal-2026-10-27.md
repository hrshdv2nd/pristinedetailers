## Campaign Brief

**Segment:** All customers — members + past clients
**Goal:** Book a post-winter inspection/maintenance service, or a new ceramic/graphene coating before the summer UV season peaks
**Insight:** Late October is Melbourne's hinge point. Winter contamination (salt residue, iron deposits, bonded road grime) is baked onto paint from four months of wet roads. At the same time, UV index climbs sharply through November and peaks in December–February. Customers who get a coating applied or maintained now arrive at summer fully protected. Customers who wait get a ceramic job in the middle of the harshest UV window — or don't get one at all.

The jacaranda angle is specific and underused: Melbourne's jacarandas drop purple flowers and sap from late October into November. The sap is mildly acidic and stains uncoated paintwork. It's not dramatic damage — it's the kind of thing that shows up at resale time, or when you go to polish the roof and find the clear coat is patchy.

**Body:**

---

Hi [First name],

The car got through winter looking fine. That's partly because Melbourne winter is mostly grey and overcast — not dramatic. Your paint doesn't look worse. It just looks the same as it did in May.

Here's the thing: "looks fine" and "is fine" are different at this time of year.

**What four months of Melbourne winter leaves behind:**

Iron deposits from brake dust and road grime bond slowly to clear coat. They're invisible to the eye but rough to the touch — drag your fingernails across a panel you haven't cleaned recently and you'll feel what I mean. On an uncoated car, those deposits are now embedded. On a ceramic-coated car, they're sitting on the coating surface. Either way, they need to come off before the UV season starts baking them in further.

Salt residue from July and August is still in the panel gaps, wheel arches, and door sills. Rain dilutes it but doesn't remove it. A decontamination wash does.

If your ceramic or graphene coating was applied more than 12–18 months ago, the SiO2 booster layer — the part that makes water bead off in sheets — has likely thinned out from winter rainfall and road contamination. The base bond is still there. But the surface performance has dropped. You'd notice it if you looked for it: water sitting instead of sheeting, paint feeling less smooth.

---

**What spring brings next:**

If you're in the Inner East or anywhere with jacarandas overhead, you already know what the next four weeks look like. Purple flowers on the roof, the bonnet, the boot. They're not just messy — jacaranda sap is mildly acidic. On unprotected paint it etches slowly, especially if the flowers sit wet overnight and dry in the morning sun. It's not the kind of damage you see immediately. It's the kind that shows up at trade-in time when the appraiser looks at the roof under direct light.

And UV is climbing. Melbourne's UV index in October is already hitting 8–9 on clear days. By December it's 12–13. If you're thinking about ceramic or graphene coating, the window to get it applied before the heat peak is October–November. Not because it can't be done in summer — it can — but because a coating applied in mild spring conditions has ideal cure time, and you go into the highest-UV months with a fully hardened, fresh bond on your paintwork.

---

If you're an active member: Your post-winter service is worth booking in the next two weeks. We'll do a full decontamination clay, iron fallout removal, inspect the coating performance, and do a SiO2 top-up if the hydrophobics need it. You'll notice the difference the first time it rains.

If you don't have a coating yet: October is genuinely the best time to do it. Here's why it matters: the car goes straight from a professionally corrected base into protection, cures through a mild spring fortnight, and arrives at summer fully hardened. You're not playing catch-up in the middle of February UV. You're already protected.

## Copy Notes

**Why this works:**

- **"Looks fine and is fine are different things"** — this is the conceptual hook. It validates the customer's experience (the car does look fine) while opening the door to the nuance. It doesn't scare them. It reframes what they're looking at.

- **The fingernail test is tactile and concrete.** "Drag your fingernails across a panel" is a specific instruction that turns an invisible problem (iron deposits) into something the reader can verify for themselves. When they feel the roughness, they remember this email. A reader who tests something and confirms it is already sold.

- **The jacaranda angle is hyperlocal.** It's specific to Melbourne, specific to October–November, specific to suburbs like Toorak, Armadale, Fitzroy, and Hawthorn where the trees are dense. Nobody else in this category is writing about jacaranda sap on clear coat. That's the point. Specificity earns the kind of trust that "protect your paint this spring" never does.

- **The UV curve is a genuine insight.** UV index 8–9 in October vs 12–13 in December is verifiable. It reframes the booking timing from "whenever you're ready" to "there is actually an optimal window, and you're in it." The spring booking isn't just about preference — there's a technical reason to act now.

- **Three segments, one email, clean.** Members, existing coating customers, and uncoated customers each get a paragraph that speaks directly to their situation. The rest of the email applies to everyone. No one reads three paragraphs that don't apply to them — there's a clear "if you're X" signal before each one.

- **Tone: informed friend, not salesperson.** "Not a sales pitch, just a straight answer" and "Let's get the car ready for what's coming" are the closing moves. Both are warmer than a CTA button and carry more trust weight. The reply ask is designed to generate conversations that close.

- **No urgency theatre.** The seasonal window is real. The jacaranda timing is real. The UV curve is real. No artificial countdown, no "spots are filling up", no FOMO language. The content creates urgency because the reader understands the mechanism — not because they're being pushed.

**What this email is NOT doing:**
- Not repeating the mould/condensation angle (covered in winter sends)
- Not using the Peninsula road trip frame (used in June)
- Not pushing a hard offer/discount (wrong for this segment — price-sensitive customers aren't the ICP)
