## Seasonal Context

Week 47 is the natural membership entry point in the Melbourne car owner's calendar.

**Christmas is six weeks away.** Prestige car owners in Melbourne's Inner East and Bayside suburbs are entering a gifting consideration window for their own cars and for car enthusiasts in their circle. A Pristine Detailers membership at $99–149/mo is a gift category that Melbourne's ICP understands immediately — it is not a voucher, not a detailing session, but a structured care plan for a car that matters. The buyer who considers gifting a Pristine membership is the same buyer who already owns a coated car and knows the value of regular maintenance. This is a word-of-mouth-adjacent product: existing customers are likely to recommend it.

**The PPF + ceramic cohort from Week 46 is primed for upsell.** Buyers who engaged with Pristine's PPF campaign last week have the highest probability of converting to a Signature membership. They have already committed $3,000+ to a studio-grade installation. They understand that a ceramic coating on top of PPF needs periodic top-ups to maintain the hydrophobic layer. The Signature plan — which includes ceramic maintenance, priority booking, and full interior + exterior detail — is the natural next product in their car care journey.

**Summer detail anxiety is peaking.** The Melbourne car owner heading into summer wants their car maintained through Peninsula runs, Christmas events, and New Year driving. A membership removes the scheduling friction: priority slots mean no waitlist, no "sorry we're full until January." The buyer who had trouble booking a detail last December will recognise this benefit immediately.

### Variant 1 — The Summer Plan (Angle: Melbourne prestige cars need a plan heading into summer)

**Audience:** Cold — Inner East + Bayside postcodes, prestige car ownership signals, age 30–55

**Primary text (107 chars):**
> Summer's starting. Melbourne prestige cars need a plan, not a panic detail before every drive. From $99/mo.

*107 chars ✓*

**Headline (27 chars):**
> Monthly Care. Always Ready.

*27 chars ✓*

**Description (30 chars):**
> Essential $99. Signature $149.

*30 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Summer's starting." is a two-word scene-setting sentence. No hook slop, no "POV:", no "stop scrolling." The buyer who owns a prestige car and is thinking about their summer driving schedule reads this as an accurate context statement — summer is starting, Peninsula runs are imminent, Christmas events are coming. It lands without preamble.

"Melbourne prestige cars need a plan, not a panic detail before every drive." Names the buyer's category (Melbourne prestige car owner), names the problem (reactive, panic-booking maintenance), and implies the solution (a plan) in one sentence. "Panic detail before every drive" is a phrase any car owner who has tried to book a detail heading into Christmas will recognise — the waitlist, the "earliest available is February" response, the stress of showing up to an event in an unmaintained car. The membership solves this by giving members first access to calendar slots.

"From $99/mo" closes with the price. At $99/mo it is accessible as a first impression — the buyer who might bounce at $3,000 (PPF) will read "$99/mo" and calculate the annual investment as proportionate to their car's value.

"Monthly Care. Always Ready." in the headline is a compact benefit statement — the outcome of being on a plan, not a description of the plan. "Always ready" answers the driving motivation of the target audience: a car that is presentation-ready at any point in the summer season.

**Audience note:** Cold audiences in the Inner East (Toorak, South Yarra, Hawthorn, Kew, Malvern) and Bayside (Brighton, Hampton, Sandringham, Beaumaris) postcode clusters. Interest targeting: BMW, Porsche, Audi, Mercedes-Benz, Range Rover, Tesla brand interests. Age 30–55. This is the workhorse cold variant for Week 47.

### Variant 3 — The Coating Owner Upsell (Angle: Your ceramic coating needs maintenance. That's what the Signature plan is for.)

**Funnel stage:** Solution-aware / product-aware — existing coating customer
**Audience:** Warm — custom audience of Pristine ceramic/graphene customers (last 24 months), 30-day site visitors (ceramic or PPF service pages), 1% LAL of ceramic customers

**Primary text (123 chars):**
> You invested in a ceramic coating. The coating needs maintenance to keep performing. That's what the Signature plan is for.

*123 chars ✓*

**Headline (24 chars):**
> Your Coating Needs This.

*24 chars ✓*

**Description (24 chars):**
> Signature plan. $149/mo.

*24 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "You invested in a ceramic coating." opens with the buyer's existing purchase decision — not a hypothetical scenario, but their own history. This is the most direct possible audience relevance signal in a Meta ad. The buyer who has had a Pristine ceramic coating knows what this sentence means and reads on.

"The coating needs maintenance to keep performing." This is the technical truth that every ceramic coating owner should understand but rarely acts on. Without periodic ceramic top-ups (which the Signature plan includes), the hydrophobic layer degrades gradually — water stops beading the same way, contaminants sit longer, the self-cleaning effect diminishes. The sentence does not exaggerate ("your coating is ruined") or manufacture urgency ("it's already failing"). It states a maintenance reality the buyer can verify by looking at their own car's water behaviour.

"That's what the Signature plan is for." is the product reveal. Three words followed by the product name. The sentence does not say "which is why you should consider our Signature membership" — it states the product's function directly. This is the brand voice the product context calls for: direct, expert, never salesy.

"Your Coating Needs This." in the headline mirrors the primary text exactly — no restatement, just a sharper frame on the same claim. "Needs This" is confident language; "This" refers back to the Signature plan named in the primary text.

**Audience note:** V3 runs exclusively against warm and custom audiences. Run against:
- Pristine customers who received a ceramic or graphene coating in the last 18 months and are not currently on a membership plan
- 30-day visitors to the ceramic coating service page
- 60-day visitors to the graphene coating service page (graphene at $999+ is a Signature plan audience — the investment is proportionate)
- Week 46 PPF campaign engagers who visited the service page but did not book (these buyers may be evaluating PPF + ceramic combination — Signature plan includes both ceramic maintenance and priority booking)
- 1% LAL of confirmed ceramic customers (Melbourne-scoped)

Do not run V3 against cold audiences — without the ceramic coating ownership context, "Your Coating Needs This" reads as an unclear directive. This variant converts precisely because it speaks to a buyer who has already made the initial investment.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**

- **V1 — Summer Plan (static, cold):** A single-image static ad. A clean prestige car (Range Rover Sport, BMW 5 Series, or Tesla Model 3 equivalent — Bayside/Inner East positioning) photographed in morning light outside a Toorak terrace or Brighton coastal home. The car is immaculate — ceramic-finish sheen, spotless wheels. No text overlay. The simplicity of a perfectly maintained car communicates the membership's outcome without explanation. Second frame option: the same car under Melbourne summer conditions (outdoor Christmas event setting, suburban street in summer). Caption: "Before every drive. All summer." Avoid: studio shots that look like advertising (remove branded backdrops); avoid any car with visible neglect or damage (the membership sells the maintained outcome).

- **V2 — Christmas Gift (static or carousel):** A gift-framing visual. Option A: a simple static — a prestige car bow (the kind placed on a car given as a gift), photographed on a Porsche Cayenne or BMW M3 in a Toorak driveway. No copy overlay needed — the bow does the work. Option B (carousel): Frame 1 — the bow on the car. Frame 2 — a plan card: "Essential. $99/mo. Monthly care, priority booking." Frame 3 — "Signature. $149/mo. Full detail, ceramic top-ups, first priority." This is an intentionally warm, non-corporate creative direction — it communicates the product as a considered gift, not a commercial service pitch.

- **V3 — Coating Upsell (video or static, warm audience only):** A short Reels-format video (8–12 seconds). Opening frame: extreme close-up of water beading off a ceramic-coated panel — the classic hydrophobic effect. Text overlay: "This is what a maintained coating does." Cut to: 2 seconds of the same surface without beading — water sitting flat, no repulsion. Text overlay: "This is what happens without top-ups." Final frame: Pristine studio technician applying a ceramic top-up treatment. "Signature plan. From $149/mo." The comparison is factual and immediately recognisable to any coating owner who has watched their water beading change over time. Avoid: theatrical "ruined paint" imagery — the point is maintenance, not damage prevention.
