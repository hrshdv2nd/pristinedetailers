## Creative Context

Membership is the hardest sell on Meta — because the person scrolling their feed does not wake up thinking "I need a car detailing subscription." Unlike Google Search (where the buyer is already looking), Meta must create the desire, interrupt a passive audience, and make a $99–$149/month commitment feel natural by the end of a 125-character primary text.

The three variants below each attack a different psychological barrier:

1. **The Inertia Hook** — targets the buyer who has been meaning to book for months
2. **The Maths Hook** — targets the deliberate buyer who needs to see the value calculation
3. **The Workshop Escape Hook** — targets the buyer whose real pain is logistics, not cost

Each variant is grounded in verbatim customer language from the product marketing context: "I just never get around to booking a detail," "I hate having to drop it off somewhere," "Members save up to 35% annually."

## Ad Variant 1 — The Inertia Hook

**Angle:** Naming the pattern of delay; membership as the fix

**Primary text (125 chars max):**
You meant to book it in winter. Then spring. It's July. Membership fixes that. $99/mo, we come to you.

*Char count: 102 ✓*

**Headline (27 chars max):**
Never Miss A Detail Again

*Char count: 25 ✓*

**Description (27 chars max):**
From $99/mo. Melbourne.

*Char count: 23 ✓*

**Why this works:**
The hook mirrors the buyer's exact internal monologue — they *have* been postponing since winter. Seeing their own pattern of inaction reflected back at them is a pattern interrupt that beats any benefit claim. "Membership fixes that" is the pivot: not "here's a great offer" but "here's the solution to the specific problem you just recognised in yourself." "$99/mo, we come to you" closes the curiosity loop — price and delivery model in nine words. The full promise (price, mobile, monthly) is contained inside 102 characters.

**Visual direction:**
Split-image or carousel: Left side — car with a slightly dull, unmaintained look (not filthy, just noticeably not-quite-right; visible bird dropping mark or minor dust). Right side — same car angle, paint obviously refreshed, wet-look gloss, water beading. Minimal text overlay. The visual makes the before/after argument without words; the copy handles the conversion.

**Copy-to-visual alignment:**
The "you meant to book it" copy assumes the viewer's car looks more like the left side than the right. The before/after visual confirms that assumption without accusation — it normalises the situation (everyone's car looks like this eventually) and makes the right side feel achievable rather than aspirational.

## Ad Variant 2 — The Maths Hook

**Angle:** Concrete financial benefit; membership vs. one-off pricing

**Primary text (125 chars max):**
Members save 35% a year on premium mobile detailing. We come to your home monthly. Start from $99/mo.

*Char count: 101 ✓*

**Headline (27 chars max):**
Save 35% vs. One-Off Prices

*Char count: 27 ✓*

**Description (27 chars max):**
Join. We come to you.

*Char count: 21 ✓*

**Why this works:**
The car-owning ICP in Toorak or Brighton is not price-sensitive in absolute terms — but they are *value-sensitive*. Framing membership as 35% cheaper than one-off bookings reframes the conversation from "is $99/month a lot?" to "am I already spending more than this?" Someone doing three one-off details a year at $300 each is spending $900 — the Signature plan at $149/month ($1,788/year) gets them twelve services plus ceramic maintenance. Even the Essential plan at $99/month ($1,188/year) gets them twelve washes + seal versus three or four unmanaged bookings at higher per-job cost. The maths closes itself; the ad just has to surface it.

**Visual direction:**
Clean stat callout creative. Dark background (matches prestige car aesthetic). Large white text: "MEMBERS SAVE 35% PER YEAR." Below, in smaller text: "Monthly mobile detailing — from $99/mo." Pristine Detailers logo mark, bottom right. A high-gloss car panel in the background — close crop, paintwork detail visible, no full car. This format performs well for deliberate buyers who are already in consideration mode and respond to numbers-as-hero creative.

**Copy-to-visual alignment:**
The headline is a number; the visual reinforces the number by making it the hero. No distraction from the core value argument. The buyer who is comparing options pauses on a "35%" stat because it gives them the shortcut they're looking for.

## Ad Variant 3 — The Workshop Escape Hook

**Angle:** Removing logistics friction; membership as the end of drop-offs forever

**Primary text (125 chars max):**
No workshop drop-off. No logistics. Monthly detail at your home in Toorak, Brighton, or Bayside. From $99.

*Char count: 106 ✓*

**Headline (27 chars max):**
No Workshop. Never Again.

*Char count: 25 ✓*

**Description (27 chars max):**
Monthly plan, Melb suburbs.

*Char count: 27 ✓*

**Why this works:**
"No workshop drop-off" in the first four words is a hard stop for the buyer who has mentally filed "getting a detail" in the same category as "getting a service" — something that requires half a day, arranging a lift, and logistics they don't have time for. The three-part "No X, No X, No X" structure builds rhythmic relief before the solution lands. Naming suburbs (Toorak, Brighton, Bayside) is not just location targeting — it's confirmation that Pristine actually services their street, not just "Melbourne." "$99" at the end is the price anchor that confirms the commitment is not $500/month for this level of convenience.

**Visual direction:**
Video (15 sec) or strong static. Static: Phone screen showing a Pristine booking confirmation — "Your next detail: Tuesday 4 August, 9:00am — Home, [Toorak suburb]." In the background, slightly blurred, is a beautiful garage with a dark European car. The message: booking looked like this, not like a workshop reception. Mobile receipt = membership identity. Video variant: 15-second voiceover. Opens on a standard workshop reception — waiting room, fluorescent lights, car key drop-off. Cut immediately to: front door of a South Yarra terrace house, Pristine technician arrives with equipment. Voiceover: "No drop-off. No waiting. Monthly detail. We come to you. From $99." CTA card: "Join Pristine Membership."

**Copy-to-visual alignment:**
The contrast between workshop logistics and home delivery is the entire creative idea. The static makes that contrast in one frame; the video makes it in motion. The copy pre-sells the contrast so the visual is confirmation, not explanation.

## Targeting Recommendation

### Cold Audience — Primary (Prospecting)

**Melbourne postcodes (location-specific targeting):**
- Inner East: 3142 (Toorak), 3141 (South Yarra), 3122 (Hawthorn), 3101 (Kew), 3124 (Camberwell)
- Bayside: 3186 (Brighton), 3187 (Brighton East), 3188 (Hampton), 3185 (Elwood), 3183 (St Kilda East)
- Boroondara: 3123 (Hawthorn East), 3125 (Balwyn), 3126 (Canterbury)

**Interest targeting:**
- Luxury vehicles: BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Audi, Porsche, Tesla, Range Rover (owned vehicle associations, not just "interested in")
- Automotive: car detailing, vehicle maintenance, paint protection
- Demographic overlays: Household income top 25% Melbourne, age 28–55
- Life event: New vehicle purchase (last 6 months) — captures the EOFY new-car cohort still deciding on protection

**Lookalike audience:**
- 1% lookalike of existing membership customers (CRM email list uploaded as Custom Audience)
- 2% expansion for budget scale if 1% saturates
- Base: members only, not all customers — the membership buyer profile is distinct from one-off service buyers and the lookalike will perform significantly better if built from the right seed

**Exclude:** Active membership holders (CRM exclusion), existing customers who joined in the last 30 days

### Warm Audience — Retargeting

**Segment:** Website visitors who reached `/membership` or `/booking` (with Membership service selected) but did not complete booking — 30-day window

**Retargeting ad:**

**Primary text (125 chars max):**
You've seen our membership. $99/mo. Your car. Your home. Still thinking? Book a quote — no commitment.

*Char count: 101 ✓*

**Headline (27 chars max):**
Your Quote Is Waiting

*Char count: 21 ✓*

**Description (27 chars max):**
No obligation. Book now.

*Char count: 24 ✓*

**Why this message:** The visitor who reached `/membership` or the booking page is not a cold prospect — they found Pristine, understood the offer, and almost converted. The retargeting message does not restart the pitch; it picks up exactly where they left off. "You've seen our membership" is a direct acknowledgement that they know the product. "$99/mo. Your car. Your home." restates the core offer in the fewest words. "Still thinking?" names the state without judgment. "Book a quote — no commitment" lowers the barrier from "join a plan" to "get a quote" — a softer action that reactivates deliberation without requiring a purchase decision. This ICP finds countdown timers and discount codes patronising; honest re-engagement converts better.

**Frequency cap:** 3 impressions per week, 7-day window. At the $99–$149/month price point, there is a deliberation window of 1–2 weeks; beyond that, the buyer has either converted or moved on. Do not extend to 30-day retargeting for unconverted booking-page visitors — it signals desperation, not reliability.

## Platform Placement Notes

- **Feed (Facebook + Instagram):** Primary placement for Variant 1 and Variant 2. Static creative performs better in feed than Stories for this ICP — they are more likely to stop and read copy than to swipe through ephemeral content.
- **Stories/Reels:** Variant 3 video is native to Stories format (15 sec, vertical). The workshop-to-home-delivery narrative works well in autoplay mobile video. Ensure captions are baked in — 85% of Stories autoplay muted.
- **Reels (Instagram):** If a video version of Variant 3 is produced, test in Reels with a tight 10-second edit. Hook must be in the first 2 seconds: open on the workshop reception scene, cut immediately.
