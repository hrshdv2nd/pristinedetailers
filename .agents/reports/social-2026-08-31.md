## Platform: LinkedIn

**Caption:**

Most detailing jobs fail before the first polish machine touches the paint. The reason isn't technique or product — it's bonded contamination that a standard wash doesn't address and a visual inspection doesn't reveal.

Clear coat accumulates three categories of contamination over time: iron fallout (brake dust and rail dust from Melbourne's tram network), organic compounds (tree sap, tannin, and bird etch — particularly prevalent in inner suburbs lined with plane trees), and road surface bitumen and tar. Each requires a distinct chemical process to safely remove: iron remover breaks down ferrous particles, tar solvent lifts bituminous deposits, and clay bar mechanically pulls residual bonded material. Applying a ceramic coating over unresolved contamination traps those particles beneath the chemical bond. The coating cures. The car looks clean. The contamination continues etching through the clear coat underneath it.

For a prestige vehicle or fleet car, the consequence is practical: a ceramic coating applied over properly decontaminated paint performs closer to its warranted lifespan — 5 to 8 years on a Pristine Detailers installation. One applied over contaminated paint underperforms, requires earlier correction, and reduces the resale value it was installed to protect. The ROI of doing this correctly the first time is straightforward.

Every Pristine service begins with a full decontamination stage before any polish or coating application. If you're evaluating ceramic coating for a prestige vehicle or company car, the first question to ask any detailer is simple: what's your decontamination process?
