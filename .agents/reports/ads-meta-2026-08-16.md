## Seasonal Context

Late August is the sharpest Meta opportunity for ceramic coating of the calendar year — two distinct buyer pools are simultaneously active:

**The winter damage owner.**
From May through August, Melbourne's leafy suburbs put unprotected paint through consistent punishment. Moreton Bay fig trees in Toorak and Hawthorn drop acidic sap and attract birds year-round. A car parked under one all winter — or on the street in South Yarra, or in a St Kilda car park with no cover — has accumulated real paint damage: bird-drop etching, oxidation from moisture, fine scratches from wet grit. These owners are now looking at their car in the returning August light and seeing it differently than they did in June. The Facebook and Instagram content they engage with about car care is no longer aspirational — it's diagnostic. They are identifying a problem they need to fix.

**The pre-summer protector.**
October to March is Melbourne's UV window. A ceramic coating installed in August cures fully before spring UV arrives, providing protection for the entire summer. The buyer who plans ahead — the professional in their 30s or 40s who drives a $120K BMW or Tesla and thinks about total cost of ownership — is already in market. They know the product; they've seen it on a friend's car or watched a YouTube explainer. What they're missing is a trusted, local operator who comes to them. That's the Meta ad's one job: be that operator, in their feed, in their suburb.

Run both audiences simultaneously in separate ad sets. Winter-damage owners respond to consequence framing ("this is what's happening to your paint right now"). Pre-summer protectors respond to forward-looking efficiency ("before summer UV, not after").

**Primary text (108 chars):**
> Your paint has had a Melbourne winter. Bird drops, wet roads, frost. Ceramic seals it before summer. We come to you.

**Headline (25 chars):**
> Ceramic Coating Melbourne

**Description (26 chars):**
> We come to you. From $750.

**Why this works:** Opens with a statement of fact, not a question or a threat. "Your paint has had a Melbourne winter" lands as something the buyer already knows is true — it triggers recognition, not alarm. The list (bird drops, wet roads, frost) is the specific sequence of Melbourne winter damage rather than a generic "harsh conditions" abstraction. Toorak and Hawthorn buyers have all experienced a bird drop on their bonnet that they couldn't remove in time; saying "bird drops" names that specific moment. "Ceramic seals it before summer" reframes the ad as preparation rather than reaction — the buyer who responds to this line is planning ahead, not panicking. "We come to you" in the final position is the differentiator that removes the last friction: they don't have to do anything except book.

Headline is the primary keyword — clean, direct, confirms the product and the city.

Description names the price point and the model. $750 is stated because it qualifies the buyer before the click: someone expecting a $120 detail will not click. Someone considering a $750 investment is exactly the buyer the landing page is built for.

**Audience:** Broad Inner East cold audience — BMW, Audi, Tesla, Porsche interests overlaid on 3122, 3142, 3141, 3101 postcode cluster. This is the winter-damage owner who has not yet searched for ceramic coating — Meta surfaces the problem before Google captures the intent.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**
- Hero static: Certified technician applying nano-ceramic coating to the bonnet of a dark-coloured prestige vehicle (black, midnight blue, or deep grey — these show hydrophobic water behaviour most dramatically) in a residential Toorak garage — morning light, professional equipment visible, branded workwear. The residential setting is critical: not a workshop, not a car park. A double-brick garage with timber rafters or a white-painted heritage garage door signals Inner East Melbourne immediately. The ceramic application stage — swipe-and-buff with a suede applicator pad — should be visible mid-process. Competence displayed at the moment of application is more persuasive than a finished result for a buyer considering a $750 investment in someone they've never met.
- Water-bead demonstration (Instagram Feed + Reels): Close-up video — 8–12 seconds — of water poured across a freshly coated dark bonnet. The beading and sheeting should be unmistakable: water gathers into large, tight spheres and rolls off the edge of the panel cleanly. No narration. Overlay: "After ceramic coating. This is what water does to a coated surface." This is the most share-worthy ceramic content in the detailing vertical and should be produced as a native Reel. The visual demonstration converts objectors who doubt whether ceramic "really works" more effectively than any testimonial or specification list.
- Before/after close-up (Instagram Feed): Split frame — left shows a bonnet or door panel with visible bird drop etching, oxidation, or swirl marks (raw photography, real damage, no graphic treatment); right shows the same panel angle after ceramic coating with deep gloss and no visible defects. Caption: "Melbourne winter does this to clear coat. Ceramic coating does this to Melbourne winter."
- Avoid: stock photography; any car that doesn't match Melbourne's prestige demographic (sedans under $50K, high-wear vehicles); American or European driveways or street scenes; studio photography on a white background with no residential context; CGI ceramic coating "nano" graphics — they read as low-quality and undermine the premium positioning.

**Format priority for ceramic:**
1. Instagram Feed — V2 vehicle-specific variants; visual-first format with car specificity; the Toorak and South Yarra ICP is Instagram-heavy; V2a (Porsche/Toorak) and V2b (Tesla/Richmond) are the priority here
2. Facebook Feed — V1 (winter damage) and V3 (testimonial); broader age demographic reach (35–55 Bayside segment); narrative opening of V3 performs well in the longer-scroll Facebook feed format
3. Instagram Reels — water-bead demonstration video; native format; no copy-heavy treatment; the visual does the persuasion
4. Stories — retargeting only; single-image with "Still Available This Week" headline; frequency-capped at 2× per week

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Cold audience: V1 (winter damage) vs V3 (testimonial) across Inner East and Bayside — measure CTR and cost-per-click-to-ceramic-page; do not pool into one ad set
- V2 vehicle variants run per interest group as challenger sets — determine which vehicle/suburb combination produces the lowest CPA within 7 days of launch
- Retargeting budget: 10–15% of total ceramic budget; exclude from prospecting measurement
- Kill any cold variant at <1.5% CTR after 1,000 impressions per ad set; consolidate budget to winner
