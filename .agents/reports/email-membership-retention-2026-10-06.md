# Email Sequence: Membership Retention
**Focus:** Re-engage members who haven't booked in 45+ days  
**Date written:** 2026-10-06  
**Melbourne season:** Spring (October)  
**Skill used:** churn-prevention  

---

## Context

These three emails target existing Pristine Detailers customers who have had a coating or PPF service done and haven't booked a follow-up in 45+ days. They're in the CRM. Their car is protected. They're just not maintaining the relationship.

October is the right moment to bring them back. Melbourne spring is genuinely hard on coated cars in ways winter isn't — the birds are different, the UV is different, the tree sap is different. This isn't manufactured urgency. It's real. These emails use that reality accurately and personally.

The sequence moves from low-pressure check-in → value education → concrete offer. Each email supposes the previous went unread. The tone across all three is the same person writing, not a campaign.

Suppress if a booking is made at any point. If Email 3 triggers a reply about wanting out, route to a human immediately.

---

## Email 2 — Day 52: Value Reminder

**Subject:** What your coating's up against right now  
**Preview text:** It's been 7–8 weeks. Here's what's sitting in your account.  
**Send time:** Tuesday or Wednesday, 8:30am AEST  
**Tone:** Direct and informative — making the invisible visible, no alarm

---

Hey [First Name],

Seven weeks since your last booking. I want to be straight with you.

The coating on your [car] has been doing its job without you checking in on it. That's what it's designed to do. But there are a few things happening in Melbourne right now that it wasn't designed to handle indefinitely.

**Bird droppings.** October is peak season for rainbow lorikeets, magpies, and wattlebirds across the inner suburbs and bayside. Their droppings are acidic and they hit warm paint in a way that etches faster than in winter — the chemistry changes with surface temperature. A coated car is more resistant, but not immune if it sits for days without being addressed.

**Pollen and sap.** The flowering gums, wattles, and jacarandas are going right now. What lands on the car in the morning can be baked on by midday. It's subtle, but it dulls the hydrophobic layer over time.

**UV loading.** The Melbourne UV index from October through February runs well above the European and US winter levels most coatings are tested against. Your coating's UV resistance is rated for it — but it's most effective when it's clean and not contaminated by the first two things above.

Here's what's sitting in your account unused right now:

- Annual maintenance visit (included with any coating service, valid 12 months from install date)
- Priority booking access — we hold times for existing customers before they open to new bookings
- Add-on pricing — existing customers get 20–30% off leather, glass, and wheel coating add-ons

The maintenance visit alone is worth coming back for. It takes about 90 minutes and includes a condition check of the coating, a maintenance wash, and any light surface correction needed.

If you'd like to get on the calendar this month, reply here or book directly.

[See available times →]

Tom  
Pristine Detailers

**Notes:**
- "[car]" = pulled from service record in CRM (e.g. "your GT3", "your Model S") — if unavailable, remove the tag and leave as "the car"
- The three technical points (bird acid, pollen/sap, UV) are accurate and Melbourne-specific — this is education, not scare copy
- "Annual maintenance visit" assumes standard post-service offer — confirm with ops that this is still included and that it's tracked per customer in CRM

## Seasonal Note — Spring Frame (October)

October is one of the trickiest months to write retention copy for because the weather and city feel optimistic. The risk is that customers feel fine and assume the car is fine. These emails work against that assumption specifically: the spring threats to a coated car (lorikeet/wattlebird acid droppings on warm paint, wattle/gum sap and pollen, rapidly increasing UV) are real and Melbourne-specific, not manufactured urgency. Customers who care about their cars — the ICP for Pristine — will recognise the accuracy. That recognition is what makes the email feel like it came from someone who knows their car, not from a CRM campaign.

The save offer (wheel coating) was chosen because:
1. Wheels are the most neglected protected surface on a car that's had ceramic/graphene applied to the paint
2. Winter brake dust accumulation makes October the natural time to address them
3. The $200 value is meaningful without being so high it looks unbelievable
4. It hasn't been used as a previous offer in this sequence cycle (June = interior, July = iron decontamination)
