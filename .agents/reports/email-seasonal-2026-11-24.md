## Copy Notes

**Why this works:**

- **"Summer's here" vs "summer's coming"** — the October email was forward-looking. This one opens from arrival: UV is hitting 11–12 today. That shift from future to present tense is the most important thing in this send. Customers who read October's email and filed it away get a new trigger: not "prepare," but "it's happening now."

- **The thermal camera line is verifiable and specific.** "85–90°C on a dark roof in Toorak on a still summer afternoon" is not marketing language — it reads like something a technician would say. Specificity earns credibility. A reader who wonders "is that true?" and googles "black car paint temperature summer" will find data that confirms it. That confirmation moment transfers trust.

- **December 12–15 is a hard deadline, not a sales device.** The studio actually closes. The booking window actually closes. Using a real constraint rather than a manufactured one ("limited spots!") is consistent with the brand voice and respects the customer's intelligence. If a reader pushes back — "but I could book in January" — the response is honest: yes, you can, but here's what changes.

- **Four segments in four paragraphs, clean exits.** Members, maintenance-due customers, uncoated customers, and holiday/coastal drivers each get a paragraph with a clear entry signal ("If you..."). The rest of the email is universal. No wasted copy, no one reads through three paragraphs that don't apply to them.

- **The October callback is deliberate.** "I know the October email covered the pre-summer ceramic booking window. If you read it and thought 'I should do this,' here's the actual last chance." This acknowledges the previous touchpoint, validates the indecision, and reframes this send as a natural follow-up rather than a repetition. It also signals self-awareness — Pristine Detailers doesn't blanket customers with identical urgency every month. When Casey says "this is the actual last chance," it carries weight because the brand earned it.

- **The coastal/Peninsula angle is hyperlocal and timely.** Melbourne's holiday migration to the Peninsula and coast is a real, predictable behaviour. Naming it ("Bayside drive-ins, gravel car parks in Portsea and Sorrento, the Great Ocean Road") creates the same in-group recognition the jacaranda line created in October. These are places the ICP actually goes.

- **No discount.** The ICP pays a premium because they care about their car. A discount offer here would pull the wrong audience and signal that price, not protection, is the primary value. The urgency is real and specific — no manufactured offer needed.

**What this email is NOT doing:**
- Not repeating the jacaranda or iron deposit angle (October's territory)
- Not repeating the winter contamination theme (June's territory)
- Not pushing a discount or promotional pricing
- Not vague about the booking window — the December 12–15 date is named
