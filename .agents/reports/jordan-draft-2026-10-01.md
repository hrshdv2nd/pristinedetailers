## What Is Mobile Window Tinting?

Window tinting is a polyurethane-based film applied to the interior surface of your car's glass. It blocks UV radiation, reduces cabin heat, and improves privacy. The mobile part means the work comes to you — your driveway, office car park, or the basement level of your apartment building.

For most Melbourne drivers, that distinction matters. Dropping a car at a specialist shop in Moorabbin for a full day isn't practical if you work in the CBD or live in South Yarra, Richmond, or Prahran. Mobile installation removes that friction entirely.

## What's Legal in Victoria?

Victoria's road rules set minimum Visible Light Transmission (VLT) percentages — the percentage of light the tint allows through. Go below the legal limit and you're looking at a defect notice.

| Window | Minimum VLT (Victoria) |
|--------|------------------------|
| Windscreen | Tinting not permitted (factory UV band only) |
| Front side windows (driver + passenger) | 35% VLT minimum |
| Rear side windows | No restriction |
| Rear window | No restriction |

The critical number is 35% for front windows. Most factory glass sits around 70–80% VLT, so a legal front tint is a noticeable change — but still clearly visible to police. Anything darker than 35% on front side windows is defectable and will fail a vehicle inspection.

For maximum privacy or heat rejection at the rear, rear windows in Victoria can be taken as dark as the film allows — 5% limo tint is legal on rear glass.

## How Mobile Window Tinting Works

1. **Booking** — select your vehicle type and preferred location online. You'll receive a confirmation with a 2-hour arrival window.
2. **Arrival** — the technician arrives with film pre-cut to your vehicle's template and a full portable setup kit.
3. **Preparation** — each window is cleaned with a decontamination solution to remove dust, oil, and any residue that would cause adhesion failure.
4. **Installation** — film is applied to the interior surface of each window. A heat gun and squeegee remove air pockets and set the film to the glass.
5. **Cure time** — film requires 3–5 days to fully cure. During that window, avoid rolling windows down or applying anything to the interior glass surface.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does window tinting take?**
Sedans typically run 1.5–2 hours. SUVs and wagons take 2.5–3 hours. A full vehicle including the rear windscreen can take up to 3 hours.

**Can you tint in an underground car park?**
Yes. Most apartment car parks in South Yarra, Richmond, and Prahran work well — covered from wind, adequate lighting, flat surface. Your technician confirms when booking.

**Does window tinting void my car warranty?**
No. Window tinting is applied to the glass interior surface only. It doesn't interact with any mechanical system, and new car warranties are unaffected.

**What VLT is legal in Victoria for front windows?**
35% VLT minimum on front side windows (driver and passenger). The windscreen cannot be tinted beyond the factory UV band. Rear windows have no legal VLT minimum in Victoria.

**Can I roll my windows down straight after installation?**
Not for 3–5 days. The adhesive needs time to cure fully. Rolling windows down too early can lift the film at the edges. Your technician leaves a care card at handover with the full post-installation instructions.
