## Article Body

PPF (paint protection film) is a physical barrier that stops stone chips, road debris, and car park impacts. Ceramic coating is a chemical bond that repels water, UV, and bird drops. They solve different problems — which is why most Pristine customers in Melbourne's inner east and bayside suburbs use both: PPF on high-impact zones, ceramic coating on top.

## What Does Each One Actually Protect Against?

That's the question every Melbourne driver should ask before spending $750–$7,900 on paint protection. The honest answer: PPF and ceramic coating are not competing products. They're complementary systems. But understanding which one solves which problem tells you where to start.

PPF is a self-healing polyurethane film bonded to the most vulnerable panels of your car. It's physical armour — the only protection that stops a stone chip from the Eastern Freeway or a car park scratch from a Westfield Doncaster pillar before it reaches your clear coat.

Ceramic coating is a chemical bond with your clear coat that creates a hydrophobic, UV-resistant, self-cleaning surface. It won't stop a stone chip, but it repels bird droppings before they etch, blocks Melbourne's UV before it fades your paint, and makes every wash easier for years.

## Melbourne's Specific Threats Change the Equation

Not all cities are the same. Melbourne's combination of high-speed freeways, urban parking, and coastal UV creates a specific set of risks that changes which protection to prioritise.

**PPF is the priority if you drive the Eastern Freeway, Eastlink, or the Monash.** Melbourne's freeway gravel is relentless. A car without PPF on the front bumper, bonnet, and mirrors will show stone chip damage within 12–18 months regardless of how carefully you drive. PPF is the only product that absorbs those impacts before they reach your paint.

**Ceramic coating is the priority if you park outdoors in Toorak, Brighton, or Bayside suburbs.** Bird droppings from Moreton Bay figs — a staple of Melbourne's inner-east and bayside tree canopies — have a pH of 3–4. On a 30°C+ surface, that acid etches permanently into unprotected clear coat in under 10 minutes. A ceramic coating gives you time to react.

Melbourne's UV index near Port Phillip Bay is among the highest in Australia from November through March. Without UV protection, light-coloured paint oxidises and dark paint loses depth within a few years of outdoor parking.

## Why Most Pristine Customers Use Both

Here's how the combination works in practice: PPF goes on first over your high-risk panels — bonnet, front bumper, mirrors, A-pillars, door sills. It takes the physical punishment from roads and car parks. Ceramic coating then goes on top of everything, including the PPF. The coating adds the hydrophobic layer that makes the film easier to maintain and significantly increases bird drop and UV resistance.

The result is the highest level of protection available — not because it's more expensive, but because the two systems solve genuinely different problems and compound each other's performance.

Pristine's most popular packages pair PPF on the front end with a full ceramic or graphene coating on top. If you're protecting a BMW, Porsche, or Range Rover in Melbourne's inner east, this is the standard to work toward.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does ceramic coating stick to PPF?**
Yes. Ceramic coating bonds to PPF surface just as it bonds to clear coat. This is exactly why the combination works — the coating on top of the film gives you hydrophobic performance across the entire car.

**Can I apply ceramic on top of PPF?**
Yes, and it's the recommended sequence. PPF goes on first over vulnerable panels, then ceramic coating is applied on top of the PPF and across all other painted surfaces.

**Which is more expensive — PPF or ceramic coating in Melbourne?**
PPF starts at $3,000 for a partial front end (bonnet, bumper, mirrors) and can reach $7,900 for full-vehicle coverage. Ceramic coating starts at $750 for a full car. For a full-protection build — PPF on high-impact zones plus ceramic coating on top — expect to invest from $3,750 upward depending on vehicle size.

**Does PPF prevent bird drop damage?**
Partially. The film absorbs the impact and can be replaced without repainting the panel. But PPF without ceramic coating on top will still show bird drop etching over time. The combination of both is the most effective defence against Melbourne's bird drop acidity.
