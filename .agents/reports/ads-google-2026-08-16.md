## Seasonal Context

Mid-August is the strongest Google Search window for ceramic coating in Melbourne, for three overlapping reasons:

**1. Winter has done its work on unprotected paint.**
From May to August, Melbourne's leafy Inner East and Bayside suburbs expose cars to three months of bird droppings, morning dew, frost on bonnets, wet road spray, and park overhang contamination. A Toorak or Hawthorn driveway under a Moreton Bay fig tree is a bird-drop factory — and bird excretions are acidic enough to etch clear coat within 24–48 hours in warmer conditions. By late August, the buyer who parked under one all winter has already Googled their paint damage. They're now also looking at spring on the horizon and thinking about resale prep, a pre-summer refresh, or the detail they meant to book in January.

**2. The pre-spring conversion window.**
October is when Melbourne UV starts to be genuinely damaging. Ceramic coating takes several days to cure — the August window allows booking, installation, and full cure before spring UV arrives. Buyers who research "ceramic coating Melbourne" in August are making a forward-looking protection decision, not a reactive one. They are at the highest intent stage of the consideration cycle: they've decided to act, they just need to decide who.

**3. Cars purchased in the March–May new-model window.**
Many Melbourne prestige car owners receive new vehicles in the first half of the year (European model-year launches, March plate changes). A BMW 5 Series or Porsche Macan purchased in April has now had four months of Melbourne roads, school runs, and open-air parking. The owners who intended to "get ceramic once it's run in" are now at that threshold. This search audience is highly specific — they will type the product name, not "car detailing" — and they convert at higher rates than generic detailing traffic.

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[ceramic coating kit]
[ceramic coating cost]
[car wax Melbourne]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[ceramic coating kit]` — buyer is shopping for a consumer self-application product (Gyeon, Meguiar's Hybrid Ceramic Wax, etc.); no professional service intent; will click, bounce from the pricing page, and waste spend with $0 revenue probability
- `[ceramic coating cost]` — pure price research stage; this buyer is not ready to book and is calculating budget — they will visit the landing page, note the $750 entry price, and leave without converting; exclude to protect quality score and CPA; retarget them via display or Meta after they've had time to consider
- `[car wax Melbourne]` — a fundamentally different product category; the wax buyer is price-sensitive (expecting $30–$80 for a product) and has no intent for a professional $750 ceramic application; showing Pristine's ad to this audience inflates impressions, depresses CTR, and degrades quality score for the entire campaign
