## Seasonal Context

Mid-September in Melbourne is the highest-intent fortnight for PPF acquisition in the calendar year. Three conditions are present simultaneously that do not co-occur at any other point:

**The new car delivery window.** Prestige vehicle registrations peak in September. BMW, Porsche, Range Rover, and Tesla deliveries accelerate as dealers push Q3 numbers. A buyer who has just taken delivery of a new $80K–$200K car is in a state of acute protectiveness — every morning commute on the Eastern or Monash makes the physics of stone chips personal. The Meta buyer in this category is typically not running a Google search yet; they are encountering the PPF decision for the first time from their feed. The ad that intercepts this buyer needs to make the problem feel immediately real ("you've just spent $100K — the Monash doesn't know that") and the solution feel immediately available ("certified studio, book this week").

**The AFL Grand Final social activation.** The Grand Final is approximately two weeks away — last Saturday of September. A Toorak or South Yarra car owner whose social calendar includes Grand Final week plans, spring racing at Flemington, or the first Peninsula weekend drive of the year is currently in a state of elevated car-presentation awareness. They're not shopping in a detached, analytical way — they are looking at their car in the driveway and having the thought: "I should have sorted this before now." The Meta ad that meets this moment does not manufacture urgency; it names the thought the buyer is already having.

**The spring carry-over buyer.** Car owners who deferred PPF through winter — reasoning that the car already had chips, so what was the point — revisit this logic when daylight extends and summer driving begins. The deferred damage that was acceptable in July becomes unacceptable in September when the car is coming out more often and being seen. This buyer has typically done enough research to know what PPF is; the ad does not need to explain the product. It needs to re-trigger the intention they formed and put aside.

All three buyer types are scrolling Melbourne's Instagram and Facebook right now. The three ad variants below address each one.

## Ad Variants

### Variant 1 — The Physics Argument (Angle: Stone chips are not bad luck — they're physics)

**Funnel stage:** Problem-aware → solution-aware
**Audience:** Cold — Inner East + Bayside, prestige car ownership signals (BMW, Porsche, Range Rover, Audi, Tesla), age 30–55

**Primary text (105 chars):**
> Stone chips on the Monash aren't bad luck — they're physics. PPF stops them before they reach your paint.

*105 chars ✓*

**Headline (25 chars):**
> Stop Stone Chips for Good

*25 chars ✓*

**Description (25 chars):**
> From $3,000. Book Online.

*25 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Stone chips on the Monash aren't bad luck — they're physics" is a reframe that does something specific: it removes shame (the buyer who drives the Monash every day does not feel like they've failed to protect their car — they feel like they've been subject to an unavoidable physical law) and introduces agency (if stone chips are physics, then there is an engineering solution, and PPF is it). This copy mirrors Pristine's own brand voice guidance verbatim — "Stone chips on the freeway aren't bad luck — they're physics. PPF stops them before they reach your paint" — which means it is grounded in the established, proven language the brand already uses for this problem.

"PPF stops them before they reach your paint" is the product promise distilled to its mechanism: the film takes the impact, not the paint. No jargon, no qualifier, no "up to" hedging. The outcome is stated as a fact because it is a fact — polyurethane film of sufficient specification stops stone chip impact from penetrating to the paint surface. The brevity of this statement is its strength: a buyer on Instagram sees this in 2 seconds and understands exactly what the product does.

The headline ("Stop Stone Chips for Good") delivers the resolution in five words. "For good" carries double meaning — permanently and completely — which mirrors what the PPF buyer actually wants: not "reduce chips," but stop them. The description anchors price ($3,000) and provides the conversion path (Book Online).

**Audience note:** Run cold on Inner East and Bayside postcodes (3142, 3141, 3122, 3186, 3188, 3191) with BMW, Porsche, Audi, Range Rover, and Tesla interest targeting. Age 30–55. This is the workhorse cold variant — the stone chip argument works across all three buyer types and is the most universally resonant PPF hook in Melbourne. Do not run against existing PPF customers (they've had the film installed; the message reads as redundant and wastes spend).

**Primary text (96 chars):**
> You spent $100K on that Porsche. The Monash doesn't care. PPF protects it before the first chip.

*96 chars ✓*

**Headline (23 chars):**
> Protect It From Day One

*23 chars ✓*

**Description (25 chars):**
> Melbourne PPF. Certified.

*25 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "You spent $100K on that Porsche" is an address — it places the buyer in their own specific situation. The pronoun "that Porsche" implies the ad is talking to someone who owns a Porsche specifically, even though the targeting runs across all prestige car ownership signals. This is the standard precision move in copy for premium automotive services: name one car and every prestige car owner hears their own vehicle. A BMW M3 owner reads "that Porsche" and applies it mentally to their own car without conscious effort.

"The Monash doesn't care" is a Melbourne-specific shorthand that the ICP buyer understands immediately. It doesn't need to be expanded. The Monash Freeway from the Dandenong ranges into the CBD is the primary stone chip vector for the Inner East and South East demographic — it's a road with heavy gravel truck traffic, construction access, and inconsistent surface quality. Every prestige car owner who drives it has a stone chip story. The three-word sentence confirms Pristine understands their specific context without needing to over-explain it.

"PPF protects it before the first chip" is the timing argument that separates this ad from the generic "protect your car" message. "Before the first chip" names the specific window the new car buyer is in — they haven't had a chip yet, and they want to keep it that way. This is the emotional logic of the spring-delivery buyer: not repair, not recovery — prevention from the first kilometre.

"Protect It From Day One" in the headline closes the message with a time frame that converts the abstract protection argument into an actionable now decision. The description ("Melbourne PPF. Certified.") names the city, confirms professional installation, and takes up no space that the primary text hasn't already earned.

**Audience note:** The targeting signal for this variant should include recent car purchase behaviour where available (Meta's "likely to purchase a vehicle" behaviour signal or new vehicle registration targeting). Run alongside prestige car brand interests. The Peninsula ad set for this variant should reference Peninsula roads specifically — "before the first run to Sorrento" — as a 4th-frame carousel extension, not as a separate primary text that limits inventory.

**Why this works:** This is a verbatim translation of Marcus T.'s testimonial from the product marketing context: "The finish on my GT3 is better than factory. Water rolls off in sheets. Car enthusiast interest groups (Porsche Club, track day, automotive photography) respond well to variant 3 even cold if the creative includes a GT3 image — the car is a trust signal in itself for this segment. ## Retargeting — /booking Page Abandoners (PPF Focus)

For users who visited `/services` (PPF section) or `/booking` with PPF selected but did not complete (window: 7 days):

**Primary text (77 chars):**
> Still thinking? Stone chips don't wait. Book your PPF slot before we're full.

*77 chars ✓*

**Headline (24 chars):**
> Your PPF Spot Is Waiting

*24 chars ✓*

**Description (24 chars):**
> From $3,000. Book Today.

*24 chars ✓*

**Targeting window:** 7 days post-visit
**Frequency cap:** 2× per week — PPF is a $3,000+ decision; the buyer who reached the booking page is not suffering from a lack of information or insufficient exposure. Over-retargeting a considered purchase creates friction rather than resolving it. Two impressions in seven days maintains top-of-mind presence without becoming intrusive.

**Why this works:** A buyer who navigated to the PPF booking page has completed a substantial consideration journey: they discovered Pristine, evaluated the service, reviewed the pricing, and started the booking process. Their departure from the booking page is not a rejection — it is a pause. The most common pause reasons at $3,000+ are scheduling friction (can't find a day that works), final decision hesitation (one more comparison to do), or simple interruption (they got a call and forgot to come back). "Still thinking?" opens without assumption — it acknowledges the pause without labelling it as doubt. "Stone chips don't wait" is the light urgency trigger that is true and specific: the car is exposed to stone chip hazard every day that the PPF booking is not confirmed. "Book your PPF slot before we're full" introduces mild scarcity — Pristine's studio has finite booking capacity, and PPF installations require a full day of studio time. This is not manufactured urgency; studio bookings for PPF do fill. The framing is honest.

**Do not offer a discount.** The buyer who paused on a $3,000 PPF booking is not pausing because $3,000 is too much — they chose to evaluate Pristine, not the cheapest installer in Melbourne. A discount at this stage would erode confidence in the film quality and the installer's pricing structure. The retargeting message should reinforce the value of booking now, not the price reduction of booking now.

**Message sequence note:** If the buyer sees this retargeting ad twice without converting, rotate to Variant 3 (GT3 testimonial) on the third impression. A social proof close after a scarcity message is the natural arc for a high-consideration purchase: first the urgency prompt (book your slot), then the quality confirmation (2,400+ cars, GT3 result). After three impressions without conversion, suppress for 14 days — a buyer who has seen PPF retargeting three times without responding needs more deliberation time, not more frequency.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**

- **Stone chip physics static (V1 — cold, Inner East):** Split frame. Left panel: close-up of a stone chip at paint level — visible chip, small but undeniable, on what is clearly a European car's bonnet (dark paint, sharp focus on the chip, residential Melbourne driveway context — not a car park, not a workshop). No text on the left except a caption: "What hits every Porsche on the Monash." Right panel: the same bonnet surface covered in clear self-healing PPF, struck by a small stone in a freeze-frame shot — the surface taking the impact, the paint untouched. Caption: "What happens when you have PPF." No brand name on the image. The contrast is visceral and truthful. Colour: desaturated on the left (cooler tone, the damage feels neutral), normal colour on the right (the protected finish looks clean and warm). Works as Facebook Feed static and as a two-frame Reels swipe.

- **Day one protection carousel (V2 — new car owner):** Three frames. Frame 1: A Porsche or BMW in its first week of Melbourne ownership — clean, dealership-fresh, shot in morning light in a Toorak or South Yarra residential street. Text overlay: "Day 1." Frame 2: The same car months later, without PPF, with visible stone chips on the bonnet and A-pillar. Text overlay: "Day 90. No PPF." Frame 3: A PPF installation in progress at Pristine's studio — close-up of a technician's hands applying film to a bonnet edge, precise and unhurried. Text overlay: "Day 1 with PPF. Your call." The three-frame sequence does not exaggerate the damage (realistic Melbourne chip accumulation, not extreme neglect) and does not dramatise the installation (it is craft work, shown as craft work). Works as a Carousel ad and as a 10-second Reels cut.

- **GT3 testimonial social proof (V3 — warm audience):** Clean dark background. The Marcus T. testimonial in brand typeface: *"The finish on my GT3 is better than factory. Water rolls off in sheets. Worth every dollar."* Attribution below: "— Marcus T., 2024 Porsche 911 GT3, Melbourne." Below the quote: "2,400+ cars protected. 4.9 stars." Brand logo in the lower right. Option B: the same copy over a gallery-quality shot of the GT3 after the PPF installation — water beading on the bonnet, the Melbourne skyline soft in the background behind the car. The testimonial on the image works as a Quote Card (an S-tier Meta creative format for warm audiences); the testimonial alone on dark background is cleaner for the warm retargeting context.

- **Avoid:** Stock images of generic non-Melbourne cars; extreme damage photography that looks theatrical rather than realistic; images of cars outside the ICP range (no hatches, no base sedans); any claim about being Melbourne's "best" or "most trusted" PPF installer; countdown timers or fake urgency elements; imagery that shows an untidy studio or a non-professional installation environment — PPF buyers are specifically choosing on installer quality and the studio imagery is part of that trust signal.

**Format priority for PPF:**
1. Instagram Feed — V2 (new car, day one) as carousel; visual storytelling format; Inner East demographic is Instagram-primary
2. Facebook Feed — V1 (physics) and V3 (testimonial); wider text format suits the physics argument; Bayside + warm audience skew Facebook
3. Instagram Reels — V2 as 10-second cut (dealership-fresh → chipped → protected); native format; reaches the new car buyer before they have crystallised their PPF decision
4. Stories — retargeting only; "Your PPF Spot Is Waiting" message; frequency-capped; single-frame format

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Cold Inner East: V1 (physics) vs V2 (day one) on same postcode clusters — measure CTR and cost-per-enquiry; win-in-7-days criterion: >1.2% CTR and <$4.50 CPC (PPF's higher price point allows higher CPC while maintaining ROAS)
- Cold Bayside: V1 (physics) vs V3 (testimonial) — Bayside demographic's response to testimonial vs. physics argument is the primary hypothesis to test here; V3 requires existing brand awareness to perform; track time-on-page post-click as a quality indicator
- Warm (LAL + retargeting): V3 (testimonial) as primary; rotate to retargeting message after 2 impressions without conversion
- Kill any cold variant at <1.0% CTR after 800 impressions per ad set; consolidate to winner within 7 days

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** Monash Freeway named in V1 and V2 primary text (the most recognised stone chip vector in Melbourne for Inner East commuters); Toorak named in targeting table; Sorrento and Peninsula referenced in targeting rationale for the Peninsula ad set; Grand Final and spring racing implicit in seasonal context, informing bid timing, not copy; Marcus T. Porsche GT3 testimonial grounded in verbatim customer language from product marketing context
- **Tone check:** "Stone chips on the Monash aren't bad luck — they're physics" — factual, not alarmist. "The Monash doesn't care" — three words that every Inner East driver understands. "Before the first chip" — precise, not vague. "Better than factory" — specific, attributable, backed by a real customer. No "premium experience," no "passionate team," no "Melbourne's leading PPF studio." The PPF buyer at $3,000 has read enough detailer copy to identify and dismiss generic marketing language immediately.
- **PPF vs. Membership segmentation:** Do not run Membership and PPF Meta ads to the same postcode at the same time without audience exclusion. The PPF buyer is in a high-commitment single-purchase mindset ($3,000 one-off); the Membership buyer is in a recurring-value mindset ($99–$149/mo). Mixing the messages in the same person's feed creates brand confusion and reduces conversion rate for both products. Keep campaigns siloed by audience and product stage.
- **Ceramic coating cross-sell note:** PPF buyers are the highest-priority cross-sell audience for ceramic coating as a combination booking. After the PPF booking is confirmed, the post-booking email sequence (not a Meta retargeting ad) should introduce the ceramic coating on top recommendation. Do not include ceramic coating in PPF ad copy — keep the PPF message focused on the PPF decision. The combination recommendation is a booking-stage conversation, not an awareness-stage message.
- **EOFY carry-over note:** Buyers who purchased new cars in EOFY season (June 2026) are now approximately 90 days into ownership. Their PPF window is not closed — cars in the 90–180 day range can still be protected effectively, and stone chip accumulation over that window often becomes the motivating factor to act. V1 (physics) is the right message for this audience: not "you should have done this sooner" but "the chips you've had so far are physics — PPF stops the rest."
