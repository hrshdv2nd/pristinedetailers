## Why Melbourne's Freeways Are Harder on Paint Than You Think

Stone chips aren't random. They happen where loose aggregate escapes road surfaces — and Melbourne's roads are particularly aggressive.

The Eastern Freeway and Eastlink carry high volumes of heavy vehicles, including trucks and construction traffic, which throw more debris at higher velocity than light vehicle traffic alone. The Monash Freeway near Dandenong and Cranbourne has sections of older surface aggregate that sheds regularly. The Western Ring Road and CityLink tunnels concentrate grit from multiple compressed lanes.

Most paint chips happen at 80–100 km/h — the speed at which gravel carries enough kinetic energy to fracture clear coat on contact. Factory clear coat on most European and Japanese vehicles is 35–50 microns thick. A sharp piece of aggregate at freeway speed doesn't scratch it; it craters it. That's why chips look different from swirl marks: they're impact fractures, not abrasion.

## What PPF Does That Ceramic Coating Can't

Ceramic coating is built for chemical resistance, hydrophobic performance, and UV protection. It does not stop stone chips. The nano-ceramic layer is hard, but thin — it improves resistance to light surface abrasion but cannot absorb the kinetic energy of a 5mm piece of gravel at 100 km/h.

Paint Protection Film is a 6–8 mil polyurethane film bonded directly to your paint. Self-healing PPF has a thermoplastic top coat that flows back over light surface marring when exposed to heat. More importantly, it absorbs the impact energy that would otherwise fracture your clear coat.

## FAQ

**Does PPF stop all stone chips?**
PPF stops the vast majority of stone chips on the panels it covers. A very sharp piece of aggregate at the right angle can still leave a mark through the film, but the reduction in chip frequency compared to unprotected paint is significant.

**How long does PPF last on high-impact zones?**
Self-healing PPF on high-impact zones typically lasts 8–12 years with correct maintenance. Melbourne's UV and heat don't degrade quality film significantly — the main wear factor is cumulative impact load on the most-hit zones, particularly the bonnet leading edge.

**Can PPF be applied in an underground car park?**
No. PPF installation requires a clean, temperature-controlled environment for proper film adhesion and a contamination-free finish. Pristine's Melbourne studio is set up specifically for this — the film is cut and applied in controlled conditions, not a car park or driveway.

**Does PPF need a coating applied on top?**
It doesn't require one, but a ceramic or graphene coating over PPF improves hydrophobic performance and makes the film easier to maintain. It also adds UV protection to the film itself, which extends its lifespan.
