## Seasonal Context

Week two of spring. The AFL Grand Final is three weeks out. Spring racing begins in October. The Melbourne social calendar is firing up exactly as daylight extends and weekend drives to Sorrento and the Peninsula return to the rotation. This is not a coincidence — it is the most reliable annual pattern in premium car care spend, and Ceramic Coating is the service that sits at its peak.

**The "spring investment" buyer.** A Toorak or Brighton car owner who has spent winter watching bird droppings etch through their clear coat and stone chips accumulate on their freeway routes has now reached the point where doing nothing has visible consequences. The sun is back, the car comes out of the garage, and the oxidation is obvious. This buyer is not price-shopping — they own a $100K+ car and they are looking for a reason to act. Ceramic coating at $750 is the decision that makes the next five years easy. The ad that meets them in this moment needs to confirm two things: that this is the right decision and that Pristine is the right provider.

**The "protect the new car" buyer.** Spring is peak delivery season for prestige vehicles. A buyer who picked up a new BMW, Porsche, or Tesla in August or September is searching "ceramic coating" within weeks of taking delivery — sometimes within days. Their concern is not repairing damage; it is preventing it from the first kilometre. "Protect your car before anything touches it" is the frame that converts this buyer. The search term is usually [ceramic coating new car Melbourne] or [ceramic coating Melbourne cost] — they are in evaluation mode, not emergency mode.

**The "paint correction buyer upgrading"** — the highest LTV acquisition in this campaign. A car owner who just booked or completed paint correction with Pristine has already crossed the quality threshold. The question is not whether they trust the service; it is whether they know ceramic coating is the natural next step. Ads targeting [paint correction before ceramic coating] — already live in the Paint Correction campaign — should now be supported by a matching ceramic coating campaign that meets this buyer at the decision stage, not the discovery stage. The Week 37 campaign owns the second half of that journey.

These three audiences converge in the second week of September. Budget should be front-loaded Mon–Wed when purchase intent is highest after weekend research.

Combining them in a single headline does what no generic "best ceramic coating" ad can: it differentiates on evidence, not superlatives.

## Keyword Recommendations

### Exact Match — 5 Keywords

```
[ceramic coating Melbourne]
[mobile ceramic coating Melbourne]
[ceramic coating cost Melbourne]
[ceramic coating Toorak]
[nano ceramic coating Melbourne]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[ceramic coating Melbourne]` — primary intent term; the anchor keyword for the entire campaign; any buyer who is in active evaluation mode for ceramic coating in Melbourne is typing some variation of this; bidding exact match prevents spend leakage into [ceramic tint Melbourne] or [ceramic coating kit] which carry completely different intent
- `[mobile ceramic coating Melbourne]` — captures the buyer who has already decided that mobile delivery is non-negotiable; this term has lower volume than the head term but higher conversion rate because the buyer's primary objection (workshop logistics) is already resolved before the click; ad copy can skip past the mobile explanation and go straight to coating specifics and warranty
- `[ceramic coating cost Melbourne]` — price-intent modifier; this buyer is evaluating the investment, not the logistics; they are comparing $750 for a mobile certified coating against $600 at a fixed studio 12 km from their home; the ad copy for this keyword should lead with the warranty and the long-term value case ("protects a $100K car for 5+ years — $750 is maintenance, not a luxury") rather than the mobile differentiator
- `[ceramic coating Toorak]` — suburb-specific search from the highest-LTV postcode in the Melbourne ICP; a buyer typing a suburb alongside the service name is almost certainly in the final comparison stage — they've decided on ceramic coating, they know they want local, and they're identifying who serves their specific street; lower volume, highest intent; the retargeting audience from this keyword should be treated as a top-priority conversion window
- `[nano ceramic coating Melbourne]` — technical term used by buyers who have researched the difference between SiO2-based ceramic coatings and older polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) or spray-on sealants; a buyer typing "nano ceramic" has ruled out generic mobile washes and spray sealants and is asking specifically about the chemical bond; H5 ("Nano Ceramic. Bonded. 5 Years.") mirrors this language exactly and will carry a strong relevance signal; this is the buyer who will ask technical questions at booking and will notice the difference in application quality — the highest-value, lowest-bounce segment in the campaign

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[ceramic tint Melbourne]
[DIY ceramic coating]
[ceramic coating kit]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[ceramic tint Melbourne]` — window tinting intent; buyers searching for ceramic tint are looking for UV-blocking window film, not paint coating; completely different service, different technician skill set, different product category; any overlap in the auction wastes spend and degrades Quality Score by pulling in traffic that will bounce immediately on reading the service description
- `[DIY ceramic coating]` — self-application intent; this buyer wants to apply the product themselves using a consumer-grade bottle from Supercheap Auto; they are the explicit anti-persona for a $750 professional certified service; they will not convert, and their click at CPC rates for a competitive Melbourne keyword is pure waste
- `[ceramic coating kit]` — product purchase intent, not service intent; buyer is looking to buy the raw product (Gyeon, Gtechniq, Ceramic Pro consumer kits) rather than hire a certified applicator; same anti-persona as the DIY searcher; negative-matching this term also implicitly excludes long-tail variants like [best ceramic coating kit] and [ceramic coating kit price] which carry the same non-converting intent

Window: 7-day hot retargeting.

"Mobile certified coating" in the description confirms the two key differentiators without re-explaining the service. "Up to 8yr warranty" reintroduces the trust signal that may have been the deciding factor before the buyer left. "4.9 stars, Toorak to Bayside" closes with local credibility. This retargeting message does not offer a discount — the ICP for ceramic coating is not price-sensitive in the way that justifies a promotional message, and a discount on a $750 service would undermine the premium positioning and erode the buyer's confidence in the coating quality.

## Upsell Pathway Note

Ceramic coating buyers who have not had paint correction are the highest-priority inbound prospect for a combined booking. The service page should present paint correction as the recommended prerequisite step (it is — applying ceramic to uncorrected paint locks in existing swirl marks permanently). The recommended framing: "For the best result, we correct the paint first. Ask about our paint correction + ceramic coating package." This is not an upsell — it is an honest technical recommendation that also represents $300+ in additional revenue per booking. Any buyer who accepts this recommendation should be tagged at conversion so the CRM can sequence a post-coating maintenance reminder 12 months out.
