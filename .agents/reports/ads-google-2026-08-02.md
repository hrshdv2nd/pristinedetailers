## Seasonal Context

August in Melbourne creates a specific and underexploited window for membership acquisition:

**1. Winter maintenance gap.** Car owners who care about their paint consistently fall behind on detailing between June and August. The dark evenings, rain, and cold make workshop visits feel genuinely inconvenient. For owners who haven't booked since April or May, August is the moment that gap becomes visible — the car looks dull, there's grime around the sills, water spots are sitting on the ceramic. The membership solves the problem structurally: the detail is already scheduled regardless of the owner's motivation level.

**2. Magpie and miner season.** August marks the start of nesting season for Australian magpies and noisy miners across Melbourne's Inner East and Bayside suburbs. Droppings from these birds are acidic and, left on a clear coat for more than 24–48 hours in sun, cause permanent etching. Owners of prestige vehicles parked on tree-lined streets in Toorak, Hawthorn, and South Yarra know this. A membership that includes monthly or bi-monthly contact with a certified technician means droppings are caught before they etch — but this hook works best on the landing page, not in the search ad.

**3. EOFY new-car owners, 8 weeks in.** Cars purchased in June for tax purposes are now 8 weeks old. The owner has cleared the "it's new, it doesn't need anything yet" mental barrier, and the first maintenance question is becoming real. Membership is the right framing for this buyer: one decision now, taken care of from here. This is the Membership equivalent of last week's PPF EOFY angle — the same cohort, a different service decision.

These three segments — the lapsed booker, the EOFY new-car owner, and the ceramic coating holder seeking structured maintenance — are all in the consideration phase simultaneously. The ad groups below address each.

**Pin guidance:**
- Pin H1 ("Car Detail Membership — $99/mo") to Position 1 — a buyer who types "car detailing membership Melbourne" is already product-aware and comparing tiers and prices; confirming the product category and the entry price in the headline wins the attention of exactly that buyer
- H2 and H4 rotate in Position 2 — H2 ("Never Rebook. Just Show Up.") is the friction-removal hook for the time-poor professional who has booked Pristine before and hasn't gotten around to rebooking; H4 ("Monthly Detail. Come To You.") is the product description for a buyer who is not yet familiar with how the membership works and needs confirmation that it includes the mobile model
- H3 and H5 rotate in Position 3 — H3 converts the savings percentage into a concrete annual value; H5 closes with aggregate proof (4.9 stars, 2,400+ cars) before the action prompt

**Why "Never Rebook. Just Show Up." matters in August:** The lapsed booker — an owner who last detailed in April or March — is the single largest pool of membership candidates in August. They haven't quit on car care; they just haven't had the activation energy to rebook. The headline names that exact experience and resolves it in four words. It is the most targeted headline in this set for the primary Membership audience.

### Descriptions (90 chars max)

| # | Description | Chars |
|---|-------------|-------|
| D1 | Signature: bi-monthly detail + ceramic care. From $149/mo. Mobile — we come to you. | 83 |
| D2 | Set-and-forget. Your next detail is already in the calendar. Join from $99/mo. | 78 |

**Why D1 works:** Leads with the Signature tier — the most popular tier and the highest-margin offer — before the Essential tier. The feature list (bi-monthly detail, ceramic care) answers "what does the membership actually include?" in one line; many buyers searching "car detailing membership" don't yet know that Pristine's membership includes ceramic maintenance, which is a genuine differentiator from a generic recurring wash service. The price anchor ($149/mo) sits at the end — after the value has been stated — not at the front. "Mobile — we come to you" is the last five words because it is the feature that closes the gap between "I want this" and "I'm willing to book this."

**Why D2 works:** "Set-and-forget" is a frame, not just a word. It positions the membership not as a service purchase but as a system that removes a recurring decision. "Your next detail is already in the calendar" is the outcome framed as a present-tense reality — the buyer reads this and imagines the relief of not having to remember. "Join from $99/mo" closes at the Essential tier price point to lower the perceived commitment barrier. The lapsed booker and the time-poor professional both respond to this framing because their problem is not that they don't want a detailed car — it is that they never get around to booking one.

## Keyword Recommendations

### Exact Match — 5 Keywords

```
[car detailing membership Melbourne]
[mobile car detailing subscription Melbourne]
[monthly car detail Melbourne]
[car detailing membership Toorak]
[mobile car detailing Brighton]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[car detailing membership Melbourne]` — primary exact-match term; buyer is already at the subscription stage of intent; has decided they want a recurring service and is comparing providers; this is the most valuable search in the campaign
- `[mobile car detailing subscription Melbourne]` — the word "subscription" signals a buyer who is thinking in recurring/predictable terms (often a professional who manages subscriptions for other services and wants to add car care to that stack); also picks up any buyer who has encountered a competitor's membership model and is now comparing
- `[monthly car detail Melbourne]` — a buyer who searches with the frequency ("monthly") is already aligned with the Essential membership model; this is not a one-off buyer; targeting this term with membership copy is a direct match of ad to intent
- `[car detailing membership Toorak]` — suburb-specific membership search; a buyer who types the postcode alongside the product type is at high conversion intent; Toorak is Pristine's anchor suburb with the highest density of prestige vehicles; these buyers have already narrowed to mobile membership services in their specific area
- `[mobile car detailing Brighton]` — Bayside-specific mobile term; Brighton is the highest-intent suburb for the Bayside audience; "mobile" confirms they've rejected fixed studios and want Pristine's model; does not include "membership" explicitly but captures intent-ready buyers who will respond to membership framing in the ad

---

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[car wash subscription]
[car detailing course]
[car detailing jobs]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[car wash subscription]` — automated tunnel wash subscription (Benny's, Geelong Car Wash, etc.); completely different product at $20-40/mo; no overlap with $99–$149/mo certified mobile detailing; these buyers are price-driven and will not convert on a premium membership
- `[car detailing course]` — buyer is seeking training or education (TAFE, vocational, YouTube content); zero service-purchase intent; showing membership ads to this segment produces irrelevant clicks and lowers quality score
- `[car detailing jobs]` — job seeker, not a service buyer; a high-volume irrelevant query that will surface on broad/phrase matches; adding this as an exact-match negative protects against spend on applicants and careers browsers

## Retargeting Note

For users who visited `/membership` or began the membership sign-up flow but did not complete:

> **Headline:** Your Membership Spot Is There
> **Description:** From $99/mo — detail scheduled, technician certified, we come to you. One click to join.

Window: 7-day hot retargeting.
Frequency cap: 3 impressions/week (membership is a lower deliberation purchase than PPF; slightly higher frequency is appropriate).

Message angle: The membership non-converter has already seen the offer and the price. They did not say no — they paused. The retargeting message does not restate all features; it restates the core offer ("your spot is there") as something being held rather than something to discover. This positions the conversion as completing a started journey, not starting a new one.

## Context Notes

- **Seasonal timing:** August is the winter maintenance gap window. Melbourne owners who last detailed in April are now 4 months behind. The membership framing converts this gap from a problem to a solved category. This is the strongest acquisition window for lapsed-detailing members across the year.
- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** Toorak and Brighton named in keywords; magpie/bird-dropping season is embedded in the landing page brief (not in the search ad where it would be too specific); winter grime and "never get around to rebooking" are verbatim customer-language patterns from product marketing context
- **Tone check:** "Never Rebook. Just Show Up." is confident, not pushy; "Set-and-forget" is framing, not a slogan; price is stated as fact ($99/mo, $149/mo), not as a sale; no superlatives; no "we offer"
- **Tier strategy in ads:** Essential ($99/mo) is the acquisition price in headlines; Signature ($149/mo) appears in D1 descriptions. The conversion goal is to get them to click and land — the landing page closes on Signature (the highest-margin, most-popular tier). The ad's job is to get the right buyer to the page, not to sell the tier.
