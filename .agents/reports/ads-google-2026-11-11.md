## Seasonal Context

Week 46 is the precise moment when PPF converts from a "good idea" to an urgent decision for Melbourne car owners. Three conditions converge this week that do not align at any other point in the calendar.

**The Peninsula summer season is opening now.** The Mornington Peninsula road network — the Peninsula Link, the Nepean Highway corridor, and the inland gravel connectors to Sorrento, Blairgowrie, Flinders, and Point Leo — carries its first meaningful volume of weekend prestige car traffic in the November long-weekend window and from this point forward through summer. These roads are categorically worse for stone chips than the Eastern or Monash: loose aggregate on the inland B-roads, gravel pulled onto the tarmac from residential driveways, and the tailgating pressure of a convoy of cars making the same summer run. A buyer who lives in Toorak or Brighton and owns a Porsche Cayenne, a BMW M3, or a Range Rover will drive that car to the Peninsula four to ten times between now and April. The front bonnet, bumper, and wing panels on an unprotected car will accumulate chips at a rate that is visible and permanent by mid-January. The buyer who searches for PPF the week of November 11 is acting on a concrete awareness that their car is about to start accumulating damage that cannot be reversed without repainting.

**The new-car delivery cohort is at peak PPF urgency.** September–October new prestige car registrations in Victoria peaked. The buyers who took delivery of a new Porsche, BMW, Range Rover, Tesla, or Mercedes-AMG 6–10 weeks ago have now driven their car through Melbourne spring traffic — the Eastern and Monash freeways, CBD car parks, and early Peninsula runs. The initial "it's too new to touch" hesitation has passed. These buyers know their front panels are unprotected. They know stone chips are accumulating in microscopic form even before the first visible chip appears. The optimal window for PPF application on a new car is before any significant chip accumulation — and at 6–10 weeks post-delivery, these buyers are at the edge of that window. A buyer in this cohort who searches `[paint protection film new car melbourne]` this week is not browsing — they are acting.

**The UV inflection is complete.** Melbourne's UV index is now firmly in the "very high" band (8–9) in November and climbs to "extreme" (11–12) in December. For PPF, UV matters differently than for ceramic coating: PPF is a physical film that blocks UV from reaching the paint underneath, but the film itself is UV-stabilised and the installation is best done before the UV load on uncoated panels has accumulated. More importantly, the November UV window drives searches by buyers who have a ceramic-or-PPF decision to make. Week 46 is the natural follow-on from Week 45's ceramic push: buyers who saw ceramic coating ads last week but were considering PPF (or a PPF + ceramic combination) will convert to PPF this week. The search terms overlap — `[car paint protection melbourne]` captures both audiences, and the PPF ad needs to intercept the buyer who has already decided on physical film protection.

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[ppf removal]
[ppf diy]
[ppf training course]
```

**Rationale:**

- `[ppf removal]` — existing PPF owner, non-converting intent. A buyer searching for PPF removal is a current PPF owner who wants the film removed — either to replace it or to sell the car. This buyer is not in the market for a new PPF installation; they are in the market for a removal service, which Pristine may or may not offer as a standalone service. Even if Pristine does offer removal, routing this buyer to an installation ad creates immediate message mismatch and bounce. Exclude entirely. This exclusion also suppresses `[remove paint protection film melbourne]` and `[ppf removal cost]` long-tail variants.

- `[ppf diy]` — DIY installation intent. A buyer searching for DIY PPF wants to self-install a kit. This is not a Pristine service and the buyer is an anti-ICP for PPF at $3,000+ studio rates. DIY PPF installation without studio equipment, training, or climate-controlled conditions produces visible seams, air bubbles, and lifting edges — outcomes that a buyer considering Pristine's $3,000+ service will never produce themselves without professional training. Exclude. This exclusion also suppresses `[diy ppf kit]`, `[ppf install yourself]`, and `[how to apply ppf at home]`.

- `[ppf training course]` — professional training intent. A buyer searching for PPF training is either entering the industry as a technician or researching the craft for professional development. Zero conversion probability as a Pristine service customer. Exclude explicitly. This exclusion also suppresses `[ppf installer training]`, `[paint protection film course]`, and `[ppf certification training]`.

**Retargeting logic:** A buyer who navigated to the PPF service page or the booking flow has committed significant consideration time. At $3,000+, PPF is a 1–3 week deliberation cycle; the visitor who left is on day 1–14 of that cycle. The most common pause points at this price level are: (1) waiting for a quote comparison with another studio, (2) scheduling uncertainty (can the studio fit them before Christmas?), and (3) one final validation of the certification and film brand. The retargeting message does not counter objections — it surfaces availability. "November still open" is accurate and time-bounded without being manufactured scarcity. No discount — a buyer in the PPF category at $3,000+ who reached the booking page is not price-sensitive; a discount at this stage erodes confidence in the certification quality being sold.
