## Seasonal Context

Mid-September 2026 is the single best PPF acquisition window of the calendar year. Three buyer types converge this fortnight and none of them are browsing — they are in active purchase mode.

**The spring-delivery new car buyer.** Prestige vehicle delivery peaks in September. A buyer who picked up a new Porsche Macan, BMW X5, or Range Rover Sport in August or early September is searching "paint protection film" within a fortnight of taking delivery. This is not a considered research process — it is an urgent protective impulse. They've just spent $80K–$200K on a car and the moment they drive it out of the dealership on a Melbourne road, the physics of stone chips become real. The conversion window for this buyer is narrow: search intent for "PPF new car" peaks in the two to four weeks post-delivery and then falls sharply as the car becomes familiar and the urgency fades. This ad campaign should be live before that window closes.

**The AFL Grand Final buyer.** The AFL Grand Final is approximately two weeks away (last Saturday of September). A Toorak or South Yarra car owner who plans to valet park at the MCG or drive through the city on Grand Final weekend is acutely aware of their car's condition in a way that does not exist in June. The car is going to be seen. Car park damage, door dings, and scratches are exactly the hazards PPF exists to prevent. The buyer thinking "I should protect this car properly before the social season" is not a casual browser — they are in a self-created time constraint. "Book now before the Grand Final" does not need to appear in ad copy. The buyer supplies their own urgency; the ad just needs to confirm that Pristine is the right place to book.

**The "I've been meaning to do this" carry-over buyer.** The Mornington Peninsula house opens up in October. The Porsche Club spring runs start in November. A car owner who has deferred PPF through winter — "the car's already got a few chips, no point doing it now" — reconsiders that logic when summer driving resumes. Stone chips accumulate on the Monash, the Peninsula Link, and the roads to Flinders and Sorrento. These buyers have researched PPF before. They know the product. They are not typing [what is PPF] — they are typing [PPF Melbourne price] or [PPF installer Melbourne]. The ad copy should speak to the outcome (protected paint, maintained resale value) rather than the product definition.

Budget front-loading: Monday–Thursday this week carries the highest intent density given AFL Grand Final timing. Weekend research (Friday–Sunday) should capture the buyer who is planning their spring car presentation. Run 7 days.

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[diy ppf]
[ppf vs ceramic coating]
[car wrap melbourne]
```

**Rationale:**
- `[diy ppf]` — self-installation intent. PPF is technically demanding to apply — any installer who has seen a DIY installation understands why. A buyer typing "DIY PPF" is looking to purchase the raw film and apply it themselves. They are not the target for a $3,000 professional installation service. Their click at Melbourne CPC rates is pure waste and will increase cost-per-acquisition without any corresponding conversion potential. This negative also implicitly suppresses [diy ppf kit] and [ppf install yourself] long-tail variants.

- `[ppf vs ceramic coating]` — comparative research phase. This buyer is not ready to purchase PPF. They are at the product-comparison stage, trying to understand the difference between physical film and nano-ceramic chemistry. A buyer in this phase clicking a PPF booking ad will bounce at the service page because their question has not been answered. Suppressing this term prevents spend on non-convertible research traffic and preserves Quality Score for the purchase-intent keywords that matter. The landing page's FAQ section should serve this audience organically rather than via paid acquisition.

- `[car wrap melbourne]` — different service intent. Vinyl car wrapping (colour change, livery, aesthetic modification) shares some search vocabulary with PPF but represents a completely separate buyer intent: aesthetics versus protection. A buyer searching for a car wrap is not in the market for a $3,000 clear paint protection film. Running ads against this term wastes spend, degrades CTR on the PPF campaign, and pulls in traffic that will not convert. Excluding it also implicitly suppresses [chrome wrap], [matte wrap], [colour change wrap], and similar terms.

## Upsell Pathway Note

PPF buyers are the highest-conversion audience for ceramic coating as an add-on. The correct sequence is PPF first (physical barrier on vulnerable panels) followed by ceramic or graphene coating on top (hydrophobic layer for the entire car's finish). A buyer who has committed to PPF at $3,000+ has already demonstrated willingness to invest in serious protection — the incremental ask for ceramic coating at $750 is proportionally small. The landing page and booking confirmation should both present the combination recommendation: "Most of our PPF customers combine their film with a ceramic coating on top for the complete finish. We can do both in one booking — ask us at the time of your PPF quote." This is not upsell pressure; it is an honest technical recommendation that represents the best outcome for the car and additional revenue per booking.
