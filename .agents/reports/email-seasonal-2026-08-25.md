Hi [First name],

September 1 is a week away, which means Melbourne is about to flip — longer days, higher UV, the first 20-degree afternoon in months, and everyone pulling their car out of the garage and looking at it properly for the first time since May.

What they find is usually more than they expected.

Three months of Melbourne winter does specific things to a car:

**The paint looks dull.** Not dirty — dull. Winter condensation cycles, road spray, and low UV mean oxidation creeps into the top layer of clear coat. On a coated car it's a maintenance issue. On an uncoated car it's the beginning of permanent paint degradation.

**The bird situation is about to get worse, not better.** Spring is peak season for lorikeets and magpies in the suburbs — Hawthorn, Kew, Camberwell, Toorak. Rainbow lorikeet droppings are highly acidic (pH 3.5–4.5) and sit hot on clear coat. On an unprotected surface, 48 hours in spring sun etches the paint. On a fresh ceramic coating, the same dropping wipes off clean. The difference between those two outcomes is real and visible.

**Your interior from winter.** Wet boots, damp jackets, fogged windows. If you haven't had a deep interior clean since before winter, there's a good chance the carpet backing and seat bolsters have the early stages of mould — the kind you won't smell until you turn the air-con on properly in October.

---

None of this is news if you've had us look after your car before. But this specific window — the last week of August — is the right time to act, for one reason:

**Ceramic coating applies better in late winter than any other time of year.**

Lower UV stress during application means longer initial cure time and a better bond. The heat and UV of summer, ironically, can rush the cure if a coating goes on in December. Late August / early September is the window our technicians prefer. After this, spring UV climbs fast.

If you've been thinking about a ceramic coating, or your existing one is more than 18 months old and hasn't had a maintenance check, this is the appointment to book.

## Copy Notes

- Subject line A ("Spring is 7 days away") works because it's a specific, accurate statement — not manufactured urgency. The count feels real because it is.
- Subject line B ("magpies are already back") is a Melbourne-specific observation that reads as insider knowledge, not marketing. Test with the enthusiast/suburban segments.
- The lorikeet pH fact (3.5–4.5) is accurate and specific — it earns trust. Don't soften it or make it sound approximate.
- The ceramic coating timing argument (late August cure advantage) is technically real and genuinely useful — leads with information, not offer.
- Do not add urgency countdown timers or "LAST CHANCE" language — it undercuts everything else in the email.
- "60% booked" — only use this if it's actually true. If not, remove it. Invented scarcity breaks trust the moment someone tries to book and sees empty slots.
- James reading replies should be real. If he can't do it personally, set up a filter so someone qualified responds — a canned reply kills the whole tone.
