## Seasonal Context

Week 47 is the natural window for Membership ads in the Melbourne calendar, and the timing works from three directions simultaneously. Each one maps to a distinct buyer type, and together they represent the highest concentration of Membership-consideration buyers Pristine will see before Christmas.

**The Christmas gifting window is live.** Five weeks to Christmas Day. The buyer who wants to give a gift to someone who owns a prestige car — a spouse, a partner, an adult child who took delivery this year — has a gift selection problem that Pristine's Membership solves cleanly. The usual gift logic breaks down for car owners who already have the car they want: accessories are rarely right, experiences feel generic, cash is awkward. A Pristine membership plan is specific, practical, and anchored in something the recipient cares about. It is also self-evidently appropriate for someone who owns a Porsche, a Range Rover, or a BMW M3 — it signals that the giver understands what matters. The gifting angle is unusual for a protection studio to run, which is exactly why it cuts through in the feed. Pristine's competitors are not running Christmas gift creative. The Toorak and Brighton audience that follows Pristine's Instagram is not being shown gift options this week that speak directly to their cars.

**The existing ceramic and graphene coating customer needs to act before December.** Pristine's ceramic and graphene coating customers carry manufacturer-backed warranties of eight and nine years respectively. Those warranties do not run automatically — they require periodic maintenance visits, correctly scheduled, by certified technicians. A buyer who had their car coated 12 to 18 months ago is due for maintenance. December is the worst month to discover this: studio slots fill with summer pre-treatment bookings, and a customer who lets maintenance slide through December and January risks a gap in their warranty record. A Membership plan removes the scheduling burden entirely — the customer is on a plan, the studio manages the cadence, the warranty record is maintained. The warm audience of existing coating customers — who have already committed $750–$999 to a certified coating service — is the highest-conversion audience for Membership by a wide margin. This week's ads hit them at the right moment in the maintenance cycle.

**The summer pre-treatment buyer is in the market right now.** Melbourne summer runs from December through February. The prestige car owner who cares about their paint knows that UV intensity, bird activity, insect residue, and the volume of outdoor events (Melbourne Cup aftermath, summer Peninsula weekends, Christmas social calendar) all increase between December and February. A buyer who has been meaning to put their car on a maintenance plan since they had it coated knows that summer is not the time to let it run without attention. The booking urgency is real and calendar-based: it is not manufactured. A Membership booking in November means the first scheduled visit falls before the peak summer surface stress period.

All three buyer types are in the feed this week. The three variants below address each one.

## Ad Variants

### Variant 1 — The Christmas Gift (Angle: Five weeks to Christmas. The gift for the car owner who already has the car.)

**Funnel stage:** Cold — gift buyer
**Audience:** Cold — Inner East + Bayside postcodes, prestige car household signals, age 30–55, spouses and partners of high-income professionals

**Primary text (122 chars):**
> Five weeks to Christmas. A Pristine membership is the gift for the Toorak or Brighton car owner who has the car they want.

*122 chars ✓*

**Headline (25 chars):**
> The Gift Already Decided.

*25 chars ✓*

**Description (26 chars):**
> Melbourne. Book By Dec 20.

*26 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Five weeks to Christmas." is a factual statement of calendar position that lands as useful information for a buyer who is actively in the gift selection window. It is not "Christmas is coming" — it is a specific, honest count that creates context without manufacturing urgency. For a buyer who has been thinking about a gift for the car owner in their life, five weeks is enough time to book without feeling pressured, and close enough to feel like now is the moment.

"A Pristine membership is the gift for the Toorak or Brighton car owner who has the car they want." does something specific: it qualifies the recipient, not the product. The buyer scrolling this ad is not a prestige car owner — they are someone who shops for one. By naming Toorak and Brighton explicitly, the ad functions as a geographic identity check: if the person you're buying for does not live in one of these suburbs, this gift is not for them. If they do, the ad has named them before they had to explain their situation. The specificity is the sell.

"The Gift Already Decided." in the headline closes the selection anxiety that every gift buyer carries in November. The buyer who lands on this ad has been trying to work out what to get for someone who already has everything they want in a car. The headline does not describe the gift — it resolves the decision. The phrasing implies that the right answer was always here; the buyer just hadn't seen it yet.

"Melbourne. Book By Dec 20." is a soft deadline that is accurate: studio capacity fills in December, and a Membership booking placed after December 20 may not schedule the first visit before Christmas Day. The deadline is real and functional. It does not use "limited slots" language because that is manufactured urgency; it names a date and implies the operational reason behind it.

**Audience note:** This variant is for cold audience targeting in gift-buyer mode. The targeting logic is non-standard — the buyer is not the car owner; they are the car owner's household. The postcode targeting (Toorak, South Yarra, Brighton, Hawthorn) reaches households where prestige car ownership is at scale. Interest signals should include premium home and lifestyle brands (not car-specific), luxury travel, and high-income professional demographics. Run V1 on Facebook Feed (wider age range, household purchase behaviour) rather than Instagram Feed (skews to individual car owner identity).

### Variant 2 — The Warranty Maintenance (Angle: Your ceramic warranty requires maintenance to stay valid.)

**Funnel stage:** Warm — existing ceramic or graphene coating customers
**Audience:** Warm — custom audience of Pristine's ceramic and graphene coating customers (last 18 months), 1% LAL

**Primary text (122 chars):**
> Your ceramic warranty requires maintenance to stay valid. A Pristine membership handles the schedule so you don't have to.

*122 chars ✓*

**Headline (25 chars):**
> Keep Your Warranty Valid.

*25 chars ✓*

**Description (21 chars):**
> Certified. Melbourne.

*21 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Your ceramic warranty requires maintenance to stay valid." is not a sales claim — it is a statement of fact that every Ceramic Pro and Gtechniq warranty holder is contractually bound by. The word "requires" is chosen deliberately over softer alternatives like "benefits from" or "recommends" — this is a condition of the warranty, not a suggestion. A buyer who had their car coated 12 to 18 months ago and has been meaning to sort out the maintenance schedule reads this sentence as a reminder they already knew was coming.

The sentence also creates a small anxiety that the rest of the ad immediately resolves. The buyer's concern, once surfaced, is scheduling: who do I call, when do I book, how often does it need to happen, what happens if I miss it? "A Pristine membership handles the schedule so you don't have to" answers all of those questions in nine words. The buyer does not need to research maintenance intervals, call the studio to check availability, or calendar a reminder. The plan manages itself. For the time-poor professional persona — the primary Pristine ICP — removing scheduling overhead is a significant value proposition in its own right, independent of the warranty maintenance function.

"Keep Your Warranty Valid." in the headline is the consequence stated plainly. The buyer who already has an 8-year or 9-year manufacturer warranty on their paint knows exactly what that means for their car's resale value. A warranty on a Porsche or BMW is not a small thing — it is a documented, transferable record of certified maintenance that affects the car's value at trade-in or private sale. The headline invites no further explanation because the buyer already understands the stakes.

"Certified. Melbourne." in the description is a two-word credibility close for a warm buyer who already has a relationship with Pristine. For this audience, the word "Certified" is a known signal — Ceramic Pro and Gtechniq certification is what differentiated Pristine from every other studio they evaluated when booking the original coating. The word lands as a reaffirmation of the same standard being applied to the maintenance plan, not an introduction to a new concept.

**Audience note:** V2 is a warm-only ad. The cold version of a warranty maintenance message lacks the hook — a buyer who hasn't had their car coated doesn't yet have a warranty to maintain. Run V2 exclusively against:
- Custom audience: confirmed ceramic and graphene coating customers (all-time, refreshed monthly — highest seed precision)
- Custom audience: buyers who visited the Membership service page in the last 30 days but did not book (these are buyers who already know the product exists and are evaluating; V2 closes the warranty logic gap that may have been the reason they hesitated)
- 1% LAL of confirmed coating customers — highest-precision prospecting for buyers who match the coating-customer profile but have not yet engaged with Pristine

Frequency cap for V2 on custom audiences: 3× per 14 days. The warrany maintenance argument is durable — it doesn't require scroll-stopping urgency copy, it requires repeated exposure in the right context.

### Variant 3 — The Summer Season (Angle: Melbourne summer starts now. The cars that look right in January are on a maintenance plan.)

**Funnel stage:** Problem-aware — cold and warm, summer trigger
**Audience:** Cold — Inner East + Bayside postcodes, prestige car ownership signals; Warm — 30-day site visitors, 60-day content engagers

**Primary text (106 chars):**
> Melbourne summer starts now. The cars that still look right in January are the ones on a maintenance plan.

*106 chars ✓*

**Headline (25 chars):**
> Before December Fills Up.

*25 chars ✓*

**Description (23 chars):**
> Studio-Grade. Book Now.

*23 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "Melbourne summer starts now." is a factual statement in mid-November that the Melbourne car owner reads as exact. Melbourne summer does not start on December 1 — it starts when the temperature crosses 28 degrees, when the Peninsula runs begin, when the Christmas social calendar opens up. For a car owner in Brighton or Toorak, "now" is accurate. The sentence places the reader at the start of the period they care most about, not at a point in the future.

"The cars that still look right in January are the ones on a maintenance plan." is a social observation, not a product pitch. It describes a pattern in the Melbourne prestige car community that any car-aware buyer will recognise: by late January, unprotected cars that started the season in good condition are showing the results of three months of summer surface stress — bird etch, insect residue, UV haze, and the trace marks from Christmas party car parks. The cars that don't show this are the ones that are maintained. The ad does not explain the mechanism; it describes the outcome and lets the buyer draw the connection.

"Before December Fills Up." in the headline is the action trigger. For a buyer who has been meaning to sort out a maintenance plan — and who knows from last summer that studio availability in January is tight — this headline confirms that the right window is now, not in December when the studio is at capacity. The phrasing is specific to how studio booking actually works: Pristine doesn't run out of stock, it runs out of time slots. "Fills Up" is the accurate language.

"Studio-Grade. Book Now." in the description is the quality anchor and the CTA in four words. "Studio-Grade" distinguishes the maintenance service from generic car washes and mobile detailing services that do not meet the standard required for warranty maintenance under Ceramic Pro or Gtechniq specifications. "Book Now" is a plain action with no additional framing needed.

**Audience note:** V3 runs on both cold and warm audiences, with different targeting approaches:

*Cold:* Inner East postcodes (Toorak, South Yarra, Hawthorn, Kew, Malvern, Camberwell, Balwyn) and Bayside (Brighton, Hampton, Sandringham, Beaumaris) — the postcodes where prestige car ownership is at scale. Interest signals: BMW, Porsche, Audi, Mercedes-Benz, Range Rover, Tesla, Lexus brand interests. Age 30–55. V3 on cold audiences works because "Melbourne summer starts now" is a statement that any Melbourne car owner reads immediately as true — it doesn't require prior Pristine exposure.

*Warm:* 30-day site visitors and 60-day Instagram and Facebook page engagers who have not converted. These buyers are already in Pristine's consideration set. V3's summer framing gives them a timing-based reason to act that is distinct from the warranty logic of V2 — if V2 did not convert them, V3's calendar trigger may.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**

- **Christmas gift static (V1 — cold, gift buyer):** A flat-lay or lifestyle composition that reads as a gift context without being literal: a prestige car key (Porsche, BMW, or Range Rover equivalent), a Pristine membership card or branded envelope, and a minimal seasonal prop (a sprig of eucalyptus, not generic Christmas iconography). Shot on a stone or marble surface — the same material category as the buyer's kitchen bench in Toorak or Brighton. No red or green. No ribbon. The gift frame is communicated through composition and context, not through seasonal colour. Text overlay: "A Pristine membership. For the car they already have." Works as Instagram Feed static and Facebook Feed. Pristine's existing brand photography (clean surfaces, studio-quality light) is the visual language. This format runs cold on Facebook Feed to the gift buyer demographic — it does not require car photography because the gift buyer is not primarily a car owner; they are shopping for someone who is.

- **Warranty maintenance asset (V2 — warm):** A close-up of a certified technician's hands performing a maintenance inspection on a ceramic-coated bonnet — the paint surface visible under studio lighting, clearly hydrophobic and in protected condition. The composition is the inside of the studio: controlled environment, professional tools, purpose-built space. Text overlay: "Certified maintenance. Warranty intact." This creative does not need to be dramatic — the warm audience already trusts the standard. The asset's job is to make the maintenance visit feel concrete and near, not aspirational. Works as Instagram Feed static and Facebook Feed. For the Reels format, a 10-second clip of the inspection process — no narration, no text until the final frame — with a closing frame: "Your maintenance plan. Book now."

- **Summer season static/video (V3 — cold + warm):** Two formats. Format A (Instagram Feed static): A single-frame image of a prestige car (Porsche Cayenne, BMW M5, Range Rover Sport equivalent) on the shore at Brighton Beach or on the foreshore road at Sorrento — specific Melbourne summer locations that the ICP recognises. The paint is in perfect condition: the light catches it cleanly. No visible damage. Caption: "Melbourne summer starts now. Keep it looking like this." The image carries the argument — a car in perfect condition at the start of summer, in a location the buyer drives to, is the ideal state the Membership plan maintains. Format B (Instagram Reels): A before/after split — same car, same location, shot in January. Left frame: clean, maintained. Right frame: UV haze, water marks, trace etching from the Christmas and New Year period. Caption: "The difference by January? A maintenance plan." The before/after is factual and unemotional — it describes what Melbourne summer does to unprotected paint, not as a catastrophe narrative but as a physics statement consistent with the brand voice.

- **Avoid:** Generic "happy car owner" lifestyle photography; stock photography of non-prestige vehicles; "best maintenance plan in Melbourne" in image copy; countdown timers or manufactured urgency overlays; photography that implies a mobile service — the Membership plan is studio-based maintenance.

**Format priority for Membership:**
1. Instagram Feed — V2 (warranty maintenance close-up); warm audience is Instagram-primary; the studio inspection visual performs best in a single-image format where the paint quality is visible
2. Facebook Feed — V1 (Christmas gift); gift buyer demographic skews Facebook; the flat-lay composition works in the Feed format and reaches household purchase decision-makers
3. Instagram Feed — V3 (summer season); prestige car summer content is native to Instagram Feed; the Bayside and Inner East ICP is in the feed
4. Instagram Reels — V3 (before/after, summer season); the transition format works well for before/after treatment; warm audience only
5. Stories — retargeting only; "Your Plan. This Week." message; frequency-capped; single-frame

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Warm (existing coating customers): V2 (warranty maintenance) only — no A/B required; this audience has the highest prior probability of conversion; test only after V2 establishes a CPA baseline
- Cold Inner East: V3 (summer cold) vs V1 (gift buyer) on the same postcode clusters — the summer angle vs. the gift angle is the primary hypothesis for November cold audiences; win-in-7-days criterion: >0.8% CTR and <$3.50 CPC
- Cold Bayside: V1 (gift buyer) vs V3 (summer Bayside variant) — Bayside buyer's summer social calendar is the primary risk context for V3; gift framing may outperform in a household-income context
- Retargeting: Retargeting message as primary; rotate to V2 after 2 impressions
- Kill any cold variant at <0.6% CTR after 600 impressions per ad set; consolidate to winner within 7 days
