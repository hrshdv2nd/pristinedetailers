# Email Sequence: Membership Retention
**Focus:** Re-engage members who haven't booked in 45+ days  
**Date written:** 2026-08-04  
**Melbourne season:** Late Winter (August)  
**Skill used:** churn-prevention  

---

## Context

These three emails target active members on a Pristine Detailers subscription who haven't booked in 45+ days. They're still being charged. They're not using it. The goal is to pull them back before they start questioning why they're paying for a service they've quietly stopped booking.

August is late winter in Melbourne — not the sharpest cold, but the accumulated end of it. Three months of frost deposits, road spray, brake dust iron fallout, and bay-wind grime have been sitting on these cars since June. The coating still works. But three months of winter is a long time for paint to be doing the job alone, and the members who care most about their cars know that.

This sequence uses the "end of winter reset" moment as natural motivation without manufacturing urgency. Email 1 checks in simply. Email 2 makes the cost of inaction concrete — specifically what three months without maintenance does to a ceramic coating heading into a UV-heavy spring. Email 3 offers something genuinely useful and season-appropriate.

The save offer this month is a free clay bar decontamination — distinct from June's interior detail add-on and July's iron decontamination. Clay bar is physical decontamination (versus iron decon's chemical approach) and is the right treatment for end-of-season bonded-contaminant buildup. Technically credible, timing-appropriate, and something members on quality cars will immediately recognise the value of.

Suppress the sequence if a booking is made at any point. Escalate to a human if Email 3 triggers a reply about cancelling or pausing.

Worth being specific about the ceramic maintenance: three months of Melbourne winter gradually reduces how efficiently a coating performs. The protection layer doesn't disappear, but its hydrophobicity weakens — water stops sheeting the way it did, contaminants start bonding where they used to bead off, and the barrier against UV works less efficiently just as Melbourne's UV index starts climbing again in September.

A maintenance detail now resets all of that before it compounds. It's also better timing than it sounds — late winter is when road grime has fully accumulated, and clearing it before spring means the coating performs at full capacity when the sun actually matters.

## Email 3 — Day 60: Save Offer

**Subject:** End of winter. Free clay bar treatment if you book this week.  
**Preview text:** Two months since your last booking — let's sort it  
**Send time:** Tuesday, 8:30am AEST  
**Tone:** Direct, honest, no campaign-speak — this is a person writing, not a platform

---

Hey [First Name],

Two months since your last detail. Going to keep this short.

Your membership is still active and I'd like to keep it that way — but only if it's genuinely useful to you. So let me make the next booking worth your while.

**Book any service before [DATE: 18th August] and I'll add a free clay bar decontamination.**

If you haven't had this before, a quick explanation: iron decontamination (the chemical treatment we talk about for brake dust and ferrous particles) dissolves what's on the surface. Clay bar goes further — it's a physical decontamination process that removes everything bonded *into* the surface: embedded brake dust residue, industrial fallout, rail dust, tree sap, road tar micro-traces, mineral deposits from winter rain. Three months of Melbourne winter puts all of this on paint that looks clean to the eye. You can't always see it, but you can feel it — drag a finger across the paint and it won't feel glassy the way it should. After a clay treatment, it does.

Left on a coated car long-term, these bonded contaminants create micro-pockets in the hydrophobic layer. The coating still works, but it works less efficiently — and it's one of the main reasons ceramic coating manufacturers recommend a full decontamination detail at least annually. End of winter is the natural moment for it.

That's the reason I'm offering it — not a gimmick, just good timing.

[Claim the free clay bar treatment →]  
*(Book by 18th August. Service can be scheduled within 30 days of booking. One per active membership.)*

If the membership isn't working — the timing's off, you've changed cars, or you'd rather pause than cancel — just reply here. No ticket system, no hold music. I'll reply.

Tom

## Seasonal Note — Late Winter Frame (August)

August is Melbourne's transition month — still cold, but the edge is coming off. Days are getting longer. Members with quality cars start thinking about spring, about whether the car's ready for warmer weather, about what three months of winter has actually done. That's the emotional moment this sequence works with: not "it's the worst of winter" (that was July), but "winter's almost done and it's left a mark."

The clay bar offer lands well here because it's genuinely the right treatment at the right time — three months of accumulated bonded contaminants, end of the freeze-thaw cycle, coating about to face a UV-heavy spring. It's not a manufactured incentive; it's something a good detailer would recommend anyway. That's the framing to use: we're offering it because it's the right thing to do right now, not because we needed an offer to put in an email.

The key differentiation from July's sequence: July was about the immediate problem (winter is happening *right now*, iron fallout is accumulating fast). August is about the transition — the car has been through something, here's the reset before the next season starts.

Keep the emails distinctly different from the July sequence in tone and technical content. Members who received July's emails are in this list too.
