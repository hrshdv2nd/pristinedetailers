# Melbourne Car Paint in November: The Summer Protection Guide for 2026

**Excerpt:** Melbourne's November UV is among the most damaging in Australia. If your car isn't coated before the first 30°C day, bird droppings can permanently etch the clear coat in under 10 minutes.

---

Melbourne's November UV — particularly for bayside suburbs like Brighton, Sandringham, and Mordialloc — is among the most damaging in Australia. A ceramic or graphene coating significantly reduces UV-induced paint oxidation but requires a specific maintenance routine from October through February: monthly wash, bi-annual spray sealant, and immediate attention to bird dropping acidity.

November is the last practical month to book a coating before Christmas. Here's what your paint is up against — and what to do about it.

## What November Does to Unprotected Paint

Melbourne sits at 37°S latitude. By November, UV index readings regularly hit 11–12 on 30°C days — the "extreme" band that the Bureau of Meteorology typically associates with tropical Queensland, not a bayside suburb in Victoria. For unprotected paint, the cumulative effect is oxidation: the clear coat dulls, yellows at the edges, and loses its chemical resistance to anything that lands on it.

Here's how the five main threats stack up across the suburbs this month:

| Threat | Why it peaks in November | Highest-risk suburbs |
|---|---|---|
| UV intensity | Sun angle increases sharply from Sept through December | Brighton, Sandringham, Mordialloc (bay-facing) |
| Bird drop acidity | Spring breeding season peaks; warm surfaces accelerate etching | Toorak, Hawthorn, Armadale (high tree canopy) |
| Salt air | Nor'westerlies push bay air inland | All Bayside suburbs from Elwood to Mordialloc |
| Heat on dark paint | Dark paint surface temp can exceed 80°C on a 32°C day | Inner east: Richmond, Prahran, South Yarra |
| Car park stone chips | Summer trade increases foot traffic at Westfield, Crown, St Collins Lane | CBD and inner east generally |

None of these threats are new. What changes in November is the intensity — and the speed at which damage compounds.

## Bird Drops Are More Dangerous Than Most Drivers Realise

Bird droppings have a pH between 3 and 4. At that acidity, on a paint surface sitting at 60°C or higher, the etching reaction starts within 10 minutes. Left for an hour on a hot November day, the drop can deform the clear coat permanently — a crater that no wash will fix and that requires machine polishing to remove.

On a ceramic or graphene-coated car, the same drop beads and slides off with less surface contact. Even if it sits, the coating's chemical resistance means the clear coat underneath is not in direct contact with the acid. The drop wipes clean with a damp cloth rather than leaving a mark you need to polish out.

That's not marketing. It's basic chemistry — the coating is a sacrificial barrier between the acidity and your paint.

## November Is Still Within the Coating Window — But Only Just

Ceramic and graphene coatings cure best at ambient temperatures between 10°C and 25°C, with controlled humidity. November in Melbourne still fits that window on most days. December does not: 35°C days with northerly winds create curing conditions that require more preparation, more time, and more margin for error.

More practically: December booking slots at Pristine fill in November. That's not urgency manufactured for a sales pitch — it's the same pattern each year. Melbourne's car owners who think about paint protection at Christmas are competing for the last slots before the January break.

If you're planning to have your car coated before summer peaks, the booking needs to happen this month.

## What the Right Protection Looks Like

For most Melbourne drivers with a car worth protecting:

**Ceramic coating ($750+):** Eight-year manufacturer warranty, UV-stable, hydrophobic, acid-resistant. Right for cars garaged or parked undercover most of the time.

**Graphene coating ($999+):** Denser molecular bonding than ceramic, better heat dissipation on dark paint, nine-year warranty. The upgrade that matters if your car sits in the sun regularly — daily drivers in Toorak, Brighton, and South Yarra deal with the highest combined load of UV, salt air, and bird drops.

**Add-ons:** Glass Coating ($150) for the windscreen — improves wet-weather visibility and blocks UV into the cabin. Wheel Coating ($200) for brake dust buildup, which turns aggressive in summer driving.

After coating, the maintenance routine is straightforward: a monthly hand wash, a spray sealant top-up every six months, and a damp cloth on any bird drop within the first hour. That's the entire routine for the next eight to nine years.
