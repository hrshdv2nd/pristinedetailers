## Platform: LinkedIn

**Caption:**

Automatic car washes are the single most common source of paint damage on prestige vehicles in Melbourne. Two passes of rotating brushes — or even touchless jets — introduce fine swirl marks throughout the clear coat. Each wash barely registers individually. Two years of twice-monthly washes on a daily driver produces a clear coat that looks normal in shade and visibly degraded in any direct light. Most owners don't know until they see it under inspection equipment.

Last week: an Audi RS6 in Hawthorn. Two years of automatic washes, outdoor parking under plane trees through the season, Melbourne's winter grit and wet. Under inspection lights: swirl damage across every panel, three bird-drop etch marks on the bonnet, leading edge oxidation starting to set in. Two-stage machine correction removed all of it — first pass to lift the deeper marks, second to refine the surface back to a true mirror finish. Five-year ceramic coating applied on-site at the owner's property over two sessions. The car didn't move.

The total investment was under 0.8% of the car's current market value. Correcting paint at this stage costs a fraction of what compound oxidation costs to address six months from now — and a fraction of what degraded clear coat costs at trade-in on a vehicle worth $120K+. Spring in Melbourne is the inflection point: correction work done now establishes a protected surface before summer UV loads. Deferred until December, the scope and cost of correction increases.
