## Issue 1 — `FAQSection`: Live `[SHOP ADDRESS]` placeholder (Critical bug)

**Element:** `FAQSection` → first FAQ answer, line 817
**Current copy:**
```
"Ceramic coating, graphene coating, and PPF installs are completed at our studio - [SHOP ADDRESS]. The one exception is window tinting..."
```

**Problem:**
`[SHOP ADDRESS]` renders literally in the browser. Any visitor who opens this FAQ — which appears directly above the final CTASection and is the last trust gate before a booking decision — sees an obviously unfinished website. This is not a subtle CRO problem; it actively signals that the page hasn't been reviewed. For a business selling $750–$7,900 services to owners of Porsches and Teslas, this is a credibility collapse.

The FAQ is also the only place on the homepage where a visitor can confirm the studio's physical location before booking in-person. It's doing real conversion work. Right now it's failing at the moment it matters.

**Recommendation:**
Replace `[SHOP ADDRESS]` with the actual studio address. If the address isn't ready to publish, use "our Melbourne studio" as a temporary fix — anything other than a bracket-wrapped placeholder.

## Issue 2 — `HeroSection`: "5,000+ car owners" contradicts documented proof point of 2,400+

**Element:** `HeroSection` → `<p>` at line 91
**Current copy:**
```
"Precision grade ceramic, graphene, and paint protection film helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."
```

**Problem:**
The business's own documented proof point (`product-marketing-context.md`) states "2,400+ cars protected." The hero claims 5,000+ — more than double. This isn't a rounding difference; it's a stat that cannot be substantiated if a prospect cross-references it, asks in-person, or compares it against the Google review count. The ICP for high-ticket coatings actively scrutinises who they trust with a $100K car. An inflated stat, once noticed, undermines everything else on the page.

**Recommendation:**
Update line 91 to use the verified figure. The quick-win copy section below shows the corrected line in full.

## Issue 3 — `FlagshipSection`: Floating stat card says "Graphene-infused" inside the Ceramic Coating section

**Element:** `FlagshipSection` → floating info card at lines 480–514
**Current content:**
```
Label: "Thickness"
Value: "2.4µm"
Sub-label: "Graphene-infused"
```

**Problem:**
The section heading (line 374) is unambiguous: "Ceramic coating. The last wax you'll ever need." The surrounding copy describes ceramic — hydrophobic, UV-stable, 5-year warranty, $750. But the floating card positioned prominently over the hero image says "2.4µm · Graphene-infused."

This creates a silent contradiction. A visitor who is reading carefully will wonder whether they're being shown ceramic or graphene specs. A visitor who isn't reading carefully may walk away thinking ceramic coating is graphene. Either outcome erodes the page's core argument: that Pristine Detailers knows what they're doing and are straight with their customers.

The `FlagshipSection` appears to have been adapted from graphene copy without updating the floating stat card.

**Recommendation:**
Option A (quick): Update the sub-label from "Graphene-infused" to "Nano-ceramic layer" or "Ceramic-bonded" — accurate to the section topic.
Option B (structural): Replace the thickness spec with a more conversion-relevant stat, e.g.:
- Label: "Warranty", Value: "Up to 8yr", Sub-label: "Manufacturer-backed" — this directly addresses the value objection ("isn't this expensive?") in the same visual element.

## Quick Win — Correct hero subtitle: stat and language

**Element:** `HeroSection` → `<p>` at line 91

**Before:**
> "Precision grade ceramic, graphene, and paint protection film helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."

**After:**
> "Studio-grade ceramic, graphene, and PPF installed by certified technicians. 2,400+ cars protected. Manufacturer-backed warranties up to 9 years."

**Why this is better:**
- "Studio-grade" is the brand's vocabulary (from product-marketing-context.md) and signals premium over "precision grade" which is a generic adjective
- Drops the inflated 5,000+ figure and uses the verified 2,400+
- Adds the warranty differentiator (up to 9 years) — the single most credible proof that this isn't a commodity service, and it's currently absent from the hero
- "Certified technicians" directly neutralises the #1 ICP objection: "I don't know who to trust with a car like this"
- Restructured into short declarative sentences — easier to scan on mobile

## Bigger Bet — Test category-explicit hero headline vs. brand-voice headline

**Hypothesis:**
The current hero headline is:
> "Where your car meets its best self."

This is brand-voice-led and lifestyle-oriented. It is not wrong — the copy has personality. But for a visitor arriving from Google on "ceramic coating Melbourne" or "PPF installer Melbourne," the headline confirms nothing about what this business does until they read the subtitle.

The ICP described in the product-marketing-context.md is "sceptical by nature" and their primary anxiety is "I don't know who to trust with a car like this." An abstract lifestyle headline does not answer that anxiety. A category-explicit headline does.

**Control:**
> "Where your car meets its best self."

**Variant A — Category + location:**
> "Melbourne's certified ceramic coating & PPF studio."

**Variant B — Outcome + differentiator:**
> "Your paint. Protected for years. Warrantied to prove it."

**What to measure:**
- Primary: Click-through on the hero "Book Now" CTA (tracked separately from `CTASection` CTA)
- Secondary: Scroll depth (do visitors who see the variant explore further, or convert faster?)
- Filter: Organic search sessions only — this is the audience for whom message match matters most

**Why this is a bet, not a certainty:**
The brand-voice headline may build affinity with return visitors and referral traffic — segments who already know what Pristine Detailers does and want the premium feel confirmed. The variant may win on cold organic sessions but underperform for warm traffic. Segment the results before making a universal switch. Run for 6 weeks minimum; this page likely gets modest traffic volume and needs time to reach significance.
