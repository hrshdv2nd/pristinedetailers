## Platform: Instagram

**Caption:**

Your ceramic coating won't stop a flying stone chip at 100km/h.

Ceramic coating is a chemical bond on your clear coat — hydrophobic, UV-stable, scratch-resistant. Outstanding product. Not a physical barrier.

PPF is a 200-micron polyurethane film over your paint. It absorbs impact. Self-healing. Stone chips stop at the film, not at your clear coat.

These two products solve different problems. Ceramic repels contamination and UV. PPF stops physical damage.

The right setup for most cars: PPF on the front bumper, bonnet, mirrors, and A-pillars — ceramic or graphene over everything else.

Once you understand what each one does, the combination is obvious.

## Platform: LinkedIn

**Caption:**

Most professionals who look after their vehicles assume "ceramic coating" covers everything. It covers a lot — contamination, UV, hydrophobic self-cleaning, up to 8 years of manufacturer warranty. What it doesn't cover is physical impact.

Paint Protection Film (PPF) is a separate product category. It's a polyurethane film bonded to your paint surface — typically 200 microns thick, self-healing under heat, designed to absorb stone chips and road debris before any of that energy reaches the clear coat. Ceramic or graphene coating goes on top of the film for the hydrophobic finish. They're layered products, not interchangeable ones.

The distinction matters practically for anyone commuting on Melbourne freeways. The Eastern, the Monash, and the Tullamarine regularly put stone chips into any vehicle in the outside lanes. Front bumpers, bonnets, and mirror housings take the most damage — a partial-front PPF kit protects exactly those zones. At $3,000+ for a full-front application, it's a meaningful investment. So is a paint correction on a car that's taken three years of freeway gravel to the bonnet.

If you're running a high-value personal vehicle or a client-facing company car and want a clear read on where PPF is worth it versus where ceramic alone is sufficient, we'll walk through it at no cost. Free consultation — pristinedetailers.com.au or DM directly.
