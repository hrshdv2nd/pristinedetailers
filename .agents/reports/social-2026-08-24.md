## Platform: LinkedIn

**Caption:**

Most professionals driving a company car or a personal vehicle worth $60K–$200K aren't thinking about paint protection — until they see a cluster of stone chips on the bonnet and start researching respray costs.

By then, the damage is done. And it's preventable.

Paint Protection Film (PPF) is a self-healing polyurethane film installed over a car's most exposed panels — bonnet, front bumper, A-pillars, mirrors. It physically absorbs stone chip impacts and road debris that would otherwise reach the clear coat. Unlike ceramic coating (a chemical bond that repels contaminants), PPF is a physical layer. The film takes the hit. Minor scratches in the film self-heal with heat. The paint underneath stays untouched.

The ROI is straightforward: a $3,000–$5,000 PPF installation on a $120K vehicle protects the asset's resale value across 3–5 years of daily use. Dealerships and private buyers will discount a car with visible stone chip damage. They won't discount one that still looks like it left the showroom. If you're managing a fleet, running a premium company vehicle, or simply driving something you've invested heavily in — PPF is maintenance, not an upgrade.
