## Seasonal Creative Brief

It's the first week of Melbourne spring. The brief for all three variants:

- The **swirl mark trigger** — a Toorak or Brighton buyer notices their car looks rough in September light for the first time since winter. They're not searching yet. Meta catches them before the search, plants the recognition, and sends them toward /booking.
- The **social proof validator** — a buyer who's been considering paint correction for weeks but hasn't pulled the trigger. A real Porsche owner's real testimonial from a real Melbourne suburb closes the last gap of trust.
- The **spring-reset identity** — the seasonal signal for appearance-driven car owners who want the car ready for the Grand Final, spring racing, or a Peninsula drive. Paint correction is the reset before the season begins.

Each variant targets a different emotional entry point. Run all three simultaneously in separate ad sets; let Meta optimise spend toward whichever resonates.

## Ad Variant 1 — Pain Point: The Car Wash Damage Hook

**Angle:** Swirl marks aren't bad luck. They're from the last car wash. This reframe turns a passive problem ("my paint looks rough") into an active one ("the thing I trusted caused this") — and gives Pristine a credible alternative.

**Recommended format:** Static image — Us vs. Them layout (left: swirl-marked paint in direct Melbourne sunlight / right: corrected paint, water beading cleanly). Alternatively, a before/after video reel (15 sec): hold on swirl marks in direct light for 5 seconds, cut to machine polish in action, cut to corrected finish with water beading. No music needed — ambient polish sound is more viscerally satisfying for the enthusiast ICP.

---

### Primary Text (125 chars visible in feed)

> Swirl marks aren't bad luck. They're from your last car wash. Machine polish removes them. We come to you.

**Char count:** 106 ✓

**Why this works:** The first sentence is a reframe — it turns a vague dissatisfaction ("my paint looks rough") into a specific, solvable cause ("the car wash did this"). The second sentence names the solution without embellishment. The third sentence closes the mobile objection in five words. No adjectives, no brand claims, no "please." This is the voice of a trusted technician friend who knows exactly what the problem is and exactly how to fix it.

**Hook strength:** "Swirl marks aren't bad luck" — pattern interrupt for someone scrolling past a feed of promotional content. It sounds like an opinion, not an ad. That's the entry point.

## Ad Variant 2 — Social Proof: The GT3 Testimonial

**Angle:** A real Porsche 911 GT3 owner in Toorak got a result better than factory finish. This isn't a claim from Pristine — it's a claim from a peer. For Melbourne's prestige car owner demographic, peer proof from the right car and the right suburb carries more weight than any feature list.

**Recommended format:** Review card static — dark background, quote in large type, car make/suburb attribution below. Clean and credible. Alternatively, a founder/tech voiceover short-form reel: a technician's hands on a corrected panel while the testimonial is read in voiceover — "The finish on my GT3 is better than factory. Water rolls off in sheets." — followed by the service name and booking CTA. Mobile-native, no studio needed.

This variant names that moment and positions paint correction as the reset before the season begins.

Alternatively, a 10-second reel: exterior pan of the car in sunlight (exposing swirl marks), technician sets up with professional equipment, cut to finished result — no narration needed, just clean timing and ambient sound. End card: "Spring Paint Reset. We come to you."

### Interest Targeting

Layer onto postcode targeting (do not run interest-only without postcode overlay — reach is too broad):

**Tier 1 (highest-signal interests):**
- BMW Australia, Porsche Australia, Mercedes-Benz, Range Rover, Audi Australia, Ferrari (brand-specific interest signals)
- Car detailing, automotive detailing, car care, auto polish

**Tier 2 (lifestyle interests that correlate with ICP):**
- Spring racing carnival, Melbourne Cup, horse racing (seasonal — Variant 3)
- Luxury goods, premium lifestyle
- Home improvement, property ownership (ICP correlation: owns home with driveway/garage)

**Age / Gender:** 28–55, all genders. Do not restrict to male — Range Rover Sport and Tesla female ownership in Brighton and Toorak is a real segment (Priya S.'s testimonial confirms this). Gender restriction would cut reach without a conversion-rate justification.

---

### Lookalike Audience Approach

1. **Primary lookalike (1%):** Based on ceramic coating + PPF customers (highest LTV purchasers). Source: customer list from Setmore or CRM, filtered to customers who booked ceramic coating or PPF (not one-off washes). These are the highest-spend buyers — a 1% lookalike in Melbourne represents the 1% of Melbourne users most similar to them.

2. **Secondary lookalike (2–3%):** Based on /booking page visitors (intent signal — reached the booking step). Broader than the customer lookalike; captures planning-mode buyers who haven't converted yet. Use for Variant 1 (pain point) and Variant 3 (seasonal) — these buyers are further from conversion and respond to education and urgency.

3. **Exclusions (mandatory):**
   - Current Membership subscribers — acquisition ads targeting existing subscribers waste spend and signal poor frequency management
   - Past buyers (last 90 days) — avoid re-acquiring a customer with an active relationship
   - Bounced visitors (<10 seconds on site) — low intent, poor match

## Retargeting Message — /booking Non-Converters

**Audience:** Users who visited `/booking` in the last 7 days and did not complete a booking. Window: 7 days. Frequency cap: 3 impressions/week. This is the highest-intent retargeting segment — they reached the booking step and stopped. The barrier is almost never price discovery (they've already seen the service page) — it's friction, timing, or uncertainty about whether to call or book.

---

### Retargeting Primary Text (125 chars visible in feed)

> Your paint correction is one booking away. Swirl marks don't fix themselves. Mobile — we come to you in Melbourne.

**Char count:** 113 ✓

**Why this message angle:** The buyer who reached /booking has already accepted the price and the service model — re-explaining either wastes the impression. "One booking away" is both an action prompt and a reassurance that the process is easy. "Swirl marks don't fix themselves" is a gentle nudge that uses the buyer's own reason for visiting the site (the paint problem) to motivate completion. "Mobile — we come to you" is the final trust confirmation — the buyer's most common anxiety is logistics, and this closes it.

## Creative Execution Notes

**What to shoot (if producing fresh assets this week):**
- A prestige car (BMW M3, Range Rover Sport, or Porsche) in direct spring sunlight showing swirl marks — a macro lens shot on the bonnet or door panel in Toorak or Brighton residential setting reads as immediately local and specific
- Technician at work with a machine polisher and professional-grade compounds — this is not a quick wash, it's a skilled service; the equipment signals that
- Before/after panel shots — the contrast is the conversion tool for this service; paint correction has the most visually dramatic results of any service in the lineup
- No generic car wash imagery; no studio white-background product shots; no stock photography

**Caption/text overlay (if using Reels/Stories):**
- Font: clean, readable, dark text on light or semi-transparent overlay — avoid white-on-bright-background legibility issues in outdoor shots
- Safe zone: keep all text within the central 720×1200px area for Reels to avoid cropping on different devices
- Sound: ambient polish sound or no music is better than a trending audio track for this brand — the tone is craft and expertise, not entertainment

## Performance Signals to Watch

| Signal | Threshold | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| CTR (feed) | Below 1.5% | Test new hook — Variant 1's car wash angle may be too conceptual; try a direct "Swirl marks?" opener |
| CPM | Above $18 | Audience too narrow — expand postcode list to include Tier 2 suburbs before restricting on interest signals |
| Video completion (if running reel) | Below 25% at 5 sec | Hook isn't landing — the swirl-mark visual needs to appear in the first 1 second, not after a pan shot |
| Link CTR | Below 0.8% | Landing page expectation mismatch — add a "from $300" price signal to the primary text so clickers arrive with a price frame |
| Retargeting conversion rate | Below 15% | The /booking friction is structural (form complexity, payment step, scheduling availability) — flag to ops team, not an ad problem |

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne specificity used:** Toorak (GT3 owner attribution), Bayside (Variant 3 description), spring racing as a timing hook, Mornington Peninsula gravel angle for postcode targeting — all of these are real, specific references the ICP recognises as their own context, not generic Melbourne tourism copy.
- **Tone check:** "Swirl marks aren't bad luck" — observation, not a claim. "Better than factory finish" — customer language verbatim, not Pristine's own claim. "Spring sunlight finds every swirl mark" — descriptive, not promotional. No "best in Melbourne," no "passionate about cars," no generic superlatives.
- **Brand voice:** These ads speak like a knowledgeable friend who happens to run a detailing operation — direct, specific, unbothered by trying too hard. The reader should feel like they've been let in on something, not sold to.
