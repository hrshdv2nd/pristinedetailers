**Platforms:** Facebook Feed + Instagram Feed
**Campaign objective:** Lead generation / landing page conversions (Membership sign-up)
**Landing page:** `/membership` → `/booking`
**Target location:** Melbourne, VIC — Toorak, South Yarra, Brighton, Hawthorn, Malvern, Bayside suburbs, Mornington Peninsula

---

## Seasonal Context

Two audience states converge in the last week of August to make Membership the right product to push now:

**The winter-exit buyer.** Melbourne winters (June–August) are the worst months for car appearance maintenance. Cold mornings, wet roads, bird activity in established-suburb canopies (Toorak, Hawthorn, Malvern), and reduced daylight mean car owners who care notice the state of their car has declined — but don't always connect the decline to an absence of a maintenance plan. By the last Sunday of August, the car in the driveway that hasn't been properly detailed since May is visible evidence that "I'll get around to it" didn't work. A Meta ad that names this state — without criticising the owner — converts.

**The spring-into-routine buyer.** September through November is Melbourne's most active social calendar: AFL Grand Final week, spring racing (Caulfield, Cox Plate), summer coastal drives to the Peninsula, and a general shift in where people go and how often they're seen in their cars. An owner in Brighton with a Range Rover Sport who plans to be at Flemington in October or at Sorrento on a Saturday in November starts thinking about their car's presentation in late August. The Membership is the product that gets them from "I should do something about my car" to "it's handled" in one decision.

The copy across all three variants below names different versions of the same underlying behaviour: the car owner who cares about their car but hasn't systematised the care. The angles differ by entry point — inertia, proof, and value — but the product outcome is the same: set it up once, and the car is always in condition.

## Ad Variants

### Variant 1 — The Inertia Loop (Angle: Set it up once; never think about it again)

**Funnel stage:** Problem-aware → solution-aware
**Audience:** Cold — Inner East professionals (Hawthorn, Malvern, Kew, South Yarra), BMW and Audi interest targeting, age 30–52

**Primary text (95 chars):**
> You keep meaning to book a detail. Then life gets in the way. Membership means it just happens.

*95 chars ✓*

**Headline (27 chars):**
> Detailing Membership $99/mo

*27 chars ✓*

**Description (25 chars):**
> Mobile. Monthly. Sorted.

*24 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** "You keep meaning to book a detail. Then life gets in the way." is the direct translation of verbatim customer language from the product marketing context: "I just never get around to booking a detail." It opens with the exact internal monologue the target buyer has been running since June. It does not criticise — it recognises. "Membership means it just happens" is the resolution in five words: no friction, no decision, no diary entry required. The ICP here is the 35–50-year-old professional in Hawthorn or South Yarra who drives a BMW 5 Series or Audi Q7 and has an active enough life that maintenance genuinely does slip. They're not lazy; they're busy. The copy acknowledges that and offers a structural solution, not a motivation.

Headline anchors the product name and price together ("Detailing Membership $99/mo") so the scroll-stopper copy and the offer resolve in one frame. Description closes with a three-word rhythm — "Mobile. Monthly. Sorted." — that mirrors how the buyer would ideally summarise the product to a friend.

**Audience note:** Run this variant on cold Inner East targeting (postcodes 3122, 3144, 3144, 3101, 3141) with BMW, Audi, and luxury vehicle ownership interests. This is the core workhorse variant for the Melbourne professional with a European car — the segment most likely to have said "I'll get around to it" repeatedly. Do not run this variant on existing customers — the copy implies they haven't booked, which is factually wrong for an existing member and will generate dismissal or complaint in the comments.

### Variant 2 — The Proof (Angle: Social proof from a real member, real suburb, real outcome)

**Funnel stage:** Solution-aware → product-aware
**Audience:** Warm — engaged with Pristine content, visited `/services` or `/membership` in the last 30 days, lookalike off existing membership subscribers

**Primary text (119 chars):**
> "Six months of school-run punishment and it still looks showroom." — Priya S. Join 2,400+ Melbourne cars on membership.

*119 chars ✓*

**Headline (25 chars):**
> Membership From $99/mo.

*23 chars ✓*

**Description (26 chars):**
> Members Save 35% Annually.

*26 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** Opens with a verbatim testimonial from the product marketing context — word for word, attributed. "Six months of school-run punishment and it still looks showroom." is a sentence that does three things simultaneously: names a specific, recognisable Melbourne context (the school run — South Yarra, Toorak, Brighton); describes the exact form of abuse a family prestige car takes (not "heavy use", but "school-run punishment"); and delivers the outcome in a word ("showroom") that is both aspirational and concrete. An owner of a Range Rover Sport in Brighton reading this ad recognises their own context in the first clause and their own desired outcome in the last word.

The attribution ("— Priya S.") without the car model in this primary text version makes it feel like overheard praise rather than a curated testimonial — the buyer doesn't question authenticity because it reads like something a friend said, not a marketing brief. "Join 2,400+ Melbourne cars on membership" converts the individual experience into a social norm: this is what Melbourne car owners at this level do.

Headline and description handle price and savings proof after the emotional hook has done its job.

**Audience note:** Run against warm audiences (30-day site visitors, 60-day content engagers, 1% LAL of existing membership subscribers). The testimonial angle requires the viewer to already have some familiarity with what Pristine does — a cold audience has no framework for why "school-run punishment" on a Range Rover Sport matters. For this variant, pre-awareness is the prerequisite.

### Variant 3 — The Value Frame (Angle: The annual math of membership vs. one-off bookings)

**Funnel stage:** Product-aware → decision
**Audience:** Cold — Bayside (Brighton, Hampton, Sandringham), 38–55 demographic, high-income homeowner interests, Tesla and Range Rover ownership signals

**Primary text (110 chars):**
> Most Pristine members were spending more on one-off details. At $149/mo, Signature pays for itself.

*100 chars ✓*

**Headline (24 chars):**
> Signature Plan. $149/mo.

*24 chars ✓*

**Description (27 chars):**
> 4.9 Stars. 2,400+ Cars Done.

*28 chars — trim:*
> 4.9 Stars. 2,400+ Cars.

*23 chars ✓*

**Why this works:** The Bayside demographic (35–55, Brighton through Sandringham) skews older than the Inner East professional and responds more strongly to value-frame arguments than to inertia or aspirational identity copy. They are more likely to have already used a detailer — they have a reference price — and the Membership needs to demonstrate that it is a financially rational choice, not just a convenience. "Most Pristine members were spending more on one-off details" is not a fabricated claim: a single Basic Detail at $150 booked twice a month costs $300; a Signature Membership at $149/mo costs exactly half that and delivers more (bi-monthly detail, ceramic maintenance, exclusive add-on pricing). The math is real, and stating it directly works because this buyer will check it.

"Signature pays for itself" is a closing statement that triggers the frame a Bayside owner already uses for other investments: the car, the house, the school fees. An object that "pays for itself" is not a cost — it is a rational allocation of money. Headline names the tier and price explicitly (Signature is the most popular plan and should lead the Bayside pitch). Description delivers social proof (4.9 stars, 2,400+ cars) after the financial argument has been made.

**Audience note:** This is explicitly the Signature tier pitch. Run it on Bayside postcodes (3186, 3187, 3188, 3191) with high-income homeowner, luxury car, and financial planning interests. Exclude Essential plan buyers from this audience once they have converted — route them to an upgrade email instead. The Signature pitch may feel like oversell to an Essential prospect; keep tier messaging segmented.

### Lookalike Audiences

- **Seed 1:** Active Membership subscribers (all-time) — exact product buyer; 1% LAL off this seed is the highest-priority prospecting audience; should receive 60–70% of lookalike budget
- **Seed 2:** Booking page completions (last 12 months) — any service; broader seed, includes one-off buyers who are the natural next conversion step toward Membership
- **Seed 3:** Membership landing page visitors (last 60 days) who spent more than 30 seconds on `/membership` — intent signal short of conversion

## Retargeting — /booking Page Abandoners (Membership Focus)

For users who visited `/membership` or `/booking` with Membership selected but did not complete (window: 7 days):

**Primary text (100 chars):**
> Your car's been waiting. One direct debit. We handle the rest. Membership from $99/mo.

*86 chars ✓*

**Headline (23 chars):**
> One Direct Debit. Done.

*23 chars ✓*

**Description (27 chars):**
> From $99/mo. Cancel Anytime

*27 chars ✓*

**Targeting window:** 7 days post-visit
**Frequency cap:** 3× per week — Membership is a lower deliberation purchase than PPF ($99/mo vs $3,000 one-off); higher frequency is acceptable because the commitment threshold is lower and the inertia loop needs active interruption

**Why this works:** The buyer who reached the Membership booking page and left is further along in consideration than any cold prospect. They know the product exists, they know the price, and they haven't said no — they've paused. "Your car's been waiting" acknowledges the ongoing need without fabricating urgency. "One direct debit. We handle the rest." is the product's core promise in a sentence the buyer can act on immediately. "Cancel Anytime" in the description is the objection handler for the buyer who paused because they were uncertain about commitment — it removes the lock-in anxiety that prevents subscription sign-ups at the moment of abandonment.

**Message sequence note:** If the user has seen this retargeting ad twice without converting, rotate to V2 (Priya testimonial) as the third impression. A different creative angle (social proof after the product pitch) reactivates attention that the initial message did not close. After three impressions without conversion, suppress for 14 days — oversaturation at $99/mo signals a buyer who needs more consideration time, not more frequency.

## Creative Direction Notes

**Image/video guidance:**
- Hero static (V1 — inertia angle): Split frame — left shows a car in a Toorak or Hawthorn driveway with visible winter water spots and grime (morning light, realistic — not dramatic), captioned "What happens when you keep meaning to book." Right shows the same car exterior, detailed, gleaming, captioned "What happens when you join." Colour tone: muted grey on left, warm morning gold on right. The contrast does not exaggerate — these are real visible differences in car appearance after 5 months with and without maintenance. The residential driveway setting places the ad firmly in the buyer's context (home, not workshop).

- Social proof carousel (V2 — Priya testimonial): Frame 1: Quote card — "Six months of school-run punishment and it still looks showroom." in brand typeface on dark background, Priya S. Range Rover Sport attribution below. Frame 2: Range Rover exterior clean shot, mid-detail, technician in branded workwear. Frame 3: "Membership from $99/mo. We come to your driveway." CTA. Three frames, sequential logic: hear from a member → see the craft → take action. Works in Carousel and in Stories/Reels as a 3-cut video.

- Value frame static (V3 — Signature pitch): Clean typographic layout. Top: "One-off detail: $150." Middle: "Signature Membership: $149/mo." Bottom in larger type: "Includes bi-monthly detail, ceramic maintenance, priority booking." Subtext: "Most members save 35% in the first year." Colour: white on dark charcoal. No car photography needed for this variant — the math is the creative. Works as a Feed static and as a Reels text-reveal.

- Membership lifestyle short (Reels/Stories, 12–15 sec): Morning light at a Toorak address. Technician arrives at 7:30am, unloads from a marked van. Interior owner is getting ready, not at the car. The detail happens around their life. Cut to the car after — gleaming. Voice-over (brief, dry): "Membership. It happens whether you remember to book or not." No hard sell. No price in the video — leave that for the headline. The point is the lifestyle fit: your life goes on; your car is handled.

- Avoid: stock photography of any generic suburb or American residential street; before images that look exaggerated or grimy beyond real-world winter accumulation; any "best in Melbourne" claim; countdown timers or fake urgency; any car model not within Pristine's stated ICP range (Porsche, BMW, Audi, Range Rover, Tesla, Mercedes-AMG, Ferrari — not base-model hatchbacks)

**Format priority for Membership:**
1. Facebook Feed — V1 (inertia) and V3 (value frame); wider text format suits both; older Bayside demographic skews Facebook over Instagram
2. Instagram Feed — V2 (Priya testimonial) as carousel; visual social proof format; Inner East demographic is Instagram-primary
3. Instagram Reels — lifestyle short (15 sec); native format; membership awareness before intent crystallises; high organic discovery reach
4. Stories — retargeting only; single-frame, "One direct debit. Done." message; frequency-capped

**Copy test plan (weeks 1–2):**
- Cold Inner East: V1 (inertia) vs V2 (proof) on same postcode clusters — measure CTR and cost-per-click-to-membership-page; win-in-7-days criterion: >1.5% CTR and <$2.50 CPC
- Cold Bayside: V3 (value frame) vs V1 (inertia) — the Bayside demographic may respond differently to the two angles; V3 is the hypothesis, V1 is the challenger
- Retargeting: Single variant (retargeting message); measure conversion rate on page visitors; rotate to V2 after 2 impressions without conversion
- Kill any cold variant at <1.2% CTR after 1,000 impressions per ad set; consolidate budget to winner within 7 days; do not run more than 2 variants per cold ad set simultaneously

## Context Notes

- **Melbourne-specific hooks used:** School run (named in V2 testimonial) — South Yarra, Toorak, Brighton school zones; Eastern Freeway commute implicit in Inner East professional targeting; Toorak, Brighton, Hawthorn named in targeting table; AFL Grand Final / spring racing implicit in seasonal context; Range Rover Sport named in V2 (verbatim testimonial context); Sorrento/Peninsula implicit in lifestyle brief — not in copy but in the targeting approach
- **Tone check:** No superlatives; "set-and-forget" not "hassle-free"; "Membership means it just happens" not "never worry again"; "pays for itself" is used to describe a mathematically accurate cost comparison, not as a vague value claim; testimonial is verbatim from product marketing context — not paraphrased, not embellished
- **Membership vs single services:** No PPF, ceramic coating, or one-off services mentioned in any Membership variant. The Membership buyer is in a different decision mode — ongoing maintenance and value, not protection investment. Mixing the product messages creates decision friction rather than conversion momentum.
- **Tier messaging note:** V1 uses the Essential price ($99/mo) as the entry anchor. V3 leads with Signature ($149/mo) because the Bayside demographic is the natural Signature buyer (older, higher income, more deliberate). Retargeting uses "from $99/mo" to open the door at the lowest barrier. This tiering of the price presentation is intentional — lead with what the audience can justify to themselves.
- **EOFY context note (from PPF run):** The EOFY new-car buyer audience (V2d from the July 26 PPF run) has now held their car for nearly 8 weeks. PPF is still the right product for them; do not redirect this audience to Membership ads. The Membership prospect and the PPF prospect are at different lifecycle stages — the EOFY buyer still needs PPF first, then Membership as a second-stage conversion (addressed via post-PPF email sequence, not paid re-acquisition).
