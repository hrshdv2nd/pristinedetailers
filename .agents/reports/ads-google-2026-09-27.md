## Seasonal Context

Week 40 is the second Paint Correction rotation of the spring cycle, and the timing is sharper than the Week 36 opener. Three conditions are simultaneously active this week that did not exist in early September.

**The AFL Grand Final aftermath.** The Grand Final was played yesterday (Saturday, 26 September). Tens of thousands of cars from Toorak, South Yarra, Brighton, and the Bayside corridor drove into the Richmond and CBD precinct — Swan Street, Punt Road, Albert Park, the MCG car parks, the side streets of Cremorne. Prestige car owners who parked in those zones yesterday now have door dings, car park scratches, and contamination from high-density event parking that they are noticing this morning in their driveways. This is not hypothetical: it happens every Grand Final Saturday. A Toorak resident driving their Range Rover Sport to a Punt Road post-match function and back encounters dozens of opportunities for a door ding or a close-pass scrape. The paint correction buyer who searches "car scratch removal Melbourne" or "paint correction Melbourne" on Sunday morning the 27th or Monday the 28th is operating at peak urgency. This campaign must be live by 6am Monday.

**Spring Racing at Flemington is three weeks away.** The Melbourne Cup Carnival at Flemington begins on Thursday 22 October. The Spring Racing Carnival is, alongside the Grand Final, the single highest-stakes car presentation event in the Melbourne social calendar for the Toorak and Bayside ICP. Valet parking at Flemington, the car park at the members-only reserve, and the Saturday afternoon drive down to the Birdcage are all moments when paint quality is visible and social. A car owner who has been deferring "sorting the paint" since August is now looking at a hard deadline. The ad copy does not need to manufacture urgency — the buyer's own social calendar does it.

**Spring light is at its harshest.** Melbourne's October light — low-angle morning sun combined with clear skies after the August-September rain — makes swirl marks and oxidation visible at angles that winter overcast concealed. The car owner who looked at their paint in June and thought "it's fine" is looking at it in direct spring sunlight this week and seeing the problem. The purchase window for paint correction is not gradual — it spikes sharply when the angle of light changes. We are in that spike now.

These three buying conditions converge in Week 40. Budget should be front-loaded Monday–Wednesday when Grand Final weekend decisions translate into Monday bookings and the research cycle from Saturday's events converts.

The buyer who has been deferring paint correction since August now has a named, dated event to book against. - **H4 ("From $300 — We Come to You") → Rotate Position 3.** Price anchoring and the mobile differentiator in one headline. The em dash separates price from delivery model, giving each its own emphasis. "We Come to You" eliminates the workshop logistics objection in four words. The combined message tells the buyer: it's accessible and it comes to you. That is the complete competitive differentiation versus fixed-studio competitors in one headline.

"One Visit" eliminates the multiple-appointment objection that buyers assume a correction service will require. Run this alongside H4 in position 3; the ad group rotation will reveal which angle closes better for non-seasonal intent buyers.

| D1 | Spring sun shows every swirl mark. Machine polish in one mobile visit. Book now. | 80 |

**Why D1 works:** Opens with a factual observation — "Spring sun shows every swirl mark" — that the Melbourne ICP will recognise from their own driveway or car park this week. Low-angle October morning sun on paintwork is how most Toorak and Bayside car owners discover they have a paint problem. The ad does not tell them they have a problem — it confirms what they already see. "Machine polish in one mobile visit" answers the two questions a buyer has after recognising the problem: how it's fixed (machine polish, not hand wax) and how long it takes (one visit). "Book now" is a direct, non-hedging CTA appropriate for a buyer who is already at decision stage.

**Why D2 works:** D2 chains two time-specific urgency frames — yesterday's event (Grand Final) and the upcoming event (spring racing) — with the solution verb ("Correct") bridging them. The buyer reading this has a lived reference point for the first frame (they were there, or they know the Grand Final car park chaos) and a forward urgency frame for the second (Flemington is three weeks away). "Certified" and "4.9 stars" close the credibility gap without lengthening the description — both are shorthand for a standard the buyer recognises. D2 should be the primary description in the Grand Final–themed ad group (Ad Group 2) and secondary in the direct-service-intent group.

### Negative Keywords — 3 to Exclude

```
[DIY paint correction]
[paint correction course]
[panel beater Melbourne]
```

**Rationale:**

- `[DIY paint correction]` — self-application intent; this buyer is looking to hire a random orbital polisher and apply correction compound themselves; they are the explicit anti-persona for a certified professional service; their click wastes budget and degrades Quality Score; the word "DIY" in a modifier should be treated as a permanent negative across all Pristine campaigns

- `[paint correction course]` — education intent, not service intent; common search from car enthusiasts and aspiring detailers looking to learn technique, not hire a professional; this buyer's click produces an immediate bounce on the service booking page; the longer they stay, the more the algorithm penalises the ad for relevance; exclude before campaign launch

- `[panel beater Melbourne]` — structural bodywork intent (dents, collision damage, rust, resprays); incompatible with machine polishing for clear coat correction; a buyer whose car needs a panel beater after a Grand Final car park incident is a different buyer entirely from the swirl mark or light scratch ICP; showing a $300 paint correction ad to someone searching for a $2,000+ structural repair produces a confused click and a fast bounce; the two searches can co-occur this week given Grand Final car park events, so excluding panel beater terms is specifically important for Week 40

> **Headline:** Paint Correction Still Available
> **Description:** Swirl marks gone in one mobile visit. From $300. Certified. Toorak to Bayside. Book now.

**Headline chars:** "Paint Correction Still Available" = 31 — trim to:
"Your Paint Correction Spot's Here" = 33 — still long. Use:
> **Headline:** Book Your Paint Correction (27 chars) ✓

**Description chars:** "Swirl marks gone in one mobile visit. From $300. Certified. Toorak to Bayside. Book." = 84 chars ✓

**Retargeting logic:** The buyer who visited the paint correction page and did not convert has either a scheduling objection ("I'll do it later") or a price uncertainty. The retargeting message should not repeat the headline copy from the display campaign — it should reconfirm the two facts that close hesitant buyers: the outcome is fast ("one mobile visit") and the price is known ("From $300"). "Toorak to Bayside" confirms service coverage for the buyer who may have left because they were uncertain whether Pristine operates in their suburb. This week, the Grand Final framing is appropriate in retargeting too — a buyer who visited the page last week and hasn't booked may respond to "Spring Racing Carnival is 3 weeks away" as a concrete booking deadline. Consider A/B: standard retargeting (outcome + price) vs. racing-deadline retargeting for this Week 40 window.

**Frequency cap:** 3 impressions/week, 7-day window.  
**Do not discount.** The ICP buyer in Toorak or Brighton is not converting on price; they are converting on timing. A 10% discount undermines premium positioning and trains the buyer to wait for an offer before the next conversion.

## Upsell Pathway Note

Paint Correction is the confirmed prerequisite for Ceramic Coating. This week's campaign captures two distinct pre-coating buyers: (a) buyers who searched for paint correction first and are unaware of the ceramic coating pathway, and (b) buyers who searched [paint correction before ceramic coating] and are already on the full journey. For buyer (a), the post-booking confirmation page and the 48-hour follow-up email should introduce ceramic coating with a single specific line: "Your paint is corrected — it's the perfect base for a 5-year ceramic coat. Same certified technician. We come back to you." For buyer (b), the upsell is on the landing page itself; it should be above the fold.

Combined job value: Paint Correction ($300+) + Ceramic Coating ($750+) = $1,050+ minimum. For a single acquisition. The CPA threshold for the pre-coating ad group should be set against the $1,050 combined value, not the $300 paint correction value alone.
