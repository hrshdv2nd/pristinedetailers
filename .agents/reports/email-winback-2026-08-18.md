## Campaign Brief

**Segment:** Customers who booked once (ceramic coating, PPF, paint correction, or full detail) but have not returned in 90+ days.  
**Goal:** One re-booking before the spring surge — not a membership pitch, just get them back while the timing genuinely matters.  
**Psychology:** Loss aversion + availability heuristic + temporal urgency. They already paid for protection. That protection is carrying six months of winter contamination right now. Spring UV hits in about three weeks and it's the trigger that turns embedded contamination into permanent damage.  
**Offer:** Free wheel and tyre detail (normally $89) with any exterior or maintenance service booked before 31 August 2026.  
**Sequence:** 2 emails. Day 90 → Day 97.

---

## Seasonal Context (August — Late Melbourne Winter / Pre-Spring)

- August 18 is 13 days from September — the point where Melbourne's UV index starts climbing and spring sun becomes consistent.
- Customers who've had a ceramic coating, PPF, or paint correction since May–June are now at the tail end of their first (or second) winter without maintenance. This is the highest-contamination window of the year.
- **Iron fallout and brake dust:** Melbourne winters are wet, which means more braking, more fallout, more contamination embedding in porous or ageing coating surfaces. Iron fallout turns orange and etches when it reacts with moisture — it's invisible before it bonds.
- **Salt spray (Bayside, Mornington Peninsula):** Customers south of the Yarra are getting road spray with elevated salt content on lower panels and wheel arches all winter. Salt doesn't rinse off with rain — it dries and re-bonds.
- **Spring UV is the threat, not winter:** This is the key insight for August messaging. The UV index climbs from September 1. Accumulated winter contaminants — iron, salt, bird dropping residue — get baked into whatever surface they're sitting on. A maintained coating sheds them. A neglected coating locks them in.
- **August is the pre-spring booking surge:** By September our schedule fills for spring cleans. August is the last window where customers can book without competing for slots. This is a real urgency driver, not manufactured scarcity.
- **Wheels are the forgotten panel:** After a Melbourne winter, wheel faces and barrel lips carry the heaviest contamination load — brake dust, iron fallout, salt residue, and road tar. Customers rarely address this independently. Offering it as a free add-on is a high-perceived-value inclusion at relatively low labour cost.

## Psychological Framework

**Primary lever: Loss Aversion (Prospect Theory)**  
They spent real money — typically $800–$3,500+ — on their coating or PPF. The frame is not "come back" but "your existing investment is about to take a hit." A maintained coating going into spring handles the UV spike. A neglected one doesn't. That's not sales pressure — it's physics. People protect past gains more vigorously than they pursue future benefits.

**Secondary lever: Availability Heuristic**  
Most customers don't visualise what accumulated winter contamination looks like on their paint. Email 1 makes it visible: embedded iron fallout that turns orange under moisture, salt residue bonding to alloys, bird dropping acids working on a surface they haven't thought about since June. Making the invisible damage specific and concrete brings it out of the abstract.

**Tertiary lever: Scarcity (Real, Not Manufactured)**  
The August 31 deadline exists because September fills up. That's factually true — spring is the business's busiest booking period. Mentioning it gives the offer a legitimate urgency driver without false countdown timers or fabricated stock limits.

**Supporting lever: Contrast Effect**  
"Spring UV hits a maintained coating" vs. "spring UV hits an untreated coating" is a clear before/after. The contrast does the persuasion work without needing hyperbole — the physical reality is stark enough.

## Email 1 of 2

**Send timing:** Day 90 after last service  
**Goal:** Make the reader understand what the end of winter means for their specific car and coating — without alarm, without pressure. The email earns the right to make an offer in Email 2.

---

**Subject line:** What winter just did to your coating — before spring finds out.

**Preview text:** August is the window between "it's fine" and "too late to prep." Here's where your coating stands.

---

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

It's been about three months since we came out — which puts you at the end of Melbourne's winter carrying whatever the season left behind on your paint.

Winter doesn't look dangerous. That's the trap.

UV is low, temperatures stay mild, nothing looks wrong from the driver's seat. But from April through August, contamination builds on your paint surface without the pressure-washing and sunlight that summer naturally brings. Six months of brake dust, iron fallout, bird droppings, and salt spray — slowly embedding into whatever coating or clearcoat is sitting underneath.

Here's what September does to that.

The first consistent spring sun in Melbourne — mid-September, UV index climbing — is when everything that's accumulated on your paint gets cooked into it. On a maintained coating, those contaminants haven't had time to bond. On a coating that's carried a full winter without attention, they're already embedded. The UV doesn't create the damage. It makes it permanent.

What that looks like in practice: your paint starts looking flat after two weeks of spring sun. Not dramatically — just that low-grade dullness that wasn't there when the coating was fresh. By summer it's visible. By the time it's obvious and you book something, it's a paint correction job, not a maintenance visit.

There's a three-to-four week window before September arrives. That window is now.

**If you had ceramic coating with us:** the hydrophobic layer is still active, but it's thinner than it was. A contamination wash and an SiO2 top-up will bring it back to full protection before spring. Without that, you're heading into the highest-UV months with a partially spent coating.

**If you had PPF with us:** the film is still working, but check your edge seals. Winter's freeze-thaw cycling (even in Melbourne's mild version) and constant moisture can start to lift PPF edges where contamination has built up. Once an edge starts to lift, water gets underneath.

**If you had a paint correction with us:** the correction removed the existing damage. But if no ceramic was applied afterward, that fresh clearcoat has been unprotected all winter. Spring UV without a coating layer will start oxidising it.

You invested real money in this car. The maintenance to protect that investment is a fraction of that cost — and August is when it actually matters.

If you want a straight read on what your car probably needs, reply and tell me what you had done and roughly when — I'll give you an honest answer, not a pitch.

Or if you're ready to book:  
[Book a maintenance detail →](https://pristinedetailers.com.au/booking)

— The Pristine Detailers team

**CTA:** Book a maintenance detail  
**CTA URL:** /booking  
**Tone check:** Informative and direct. The coating-type variants (ceramic / PPF / paint correction) give it specificity that generic emails don't have — it reads like it was written for this person, not a list. No urgency in Email 1. The offer comes in Email 2.

## A/B Test Suggestions

- **Email 1 subject line variant A:** "What winter just did to your coating — before spring finds out." vs. **variant B:** "Three to four weeks before Melbourne spring hits your paint. A check-in."  
  Tests ominous/intriguing hook vs. direct calendar urgency.

- **Email 2 offer framing:** "Free wheel and tyre detail (normally $89)" vs. "We'll do your wheels and tyres while we're there — on us."  
  Tests dollar anchoring vs. conversational/casual framing.

- **Email 2 urgency driver A:** September booking surge angle ("Spring is our busiest period") vs. **variant B:** Hard date only ("Offer ends 31 August").  
  Tests social scarcity (slots filling) vs. deadline scarcity (calendar date).

- **Email 1 coating variant vs. generic:** Send the specific coating-type paragraph to the matched segment vs. a generic "your coating/protection" version to a holdout.  
  Tests personalisation lift — expected to increase Email 1 click rate 15–30%.
