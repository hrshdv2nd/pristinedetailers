## Issue 1 — FAQ answer contains live `[SHOP ADDRESS]` placeholder text

**Element:** `FAQSection` → first FAQ answer at line 817–818
**Current copy:** `"Ceramic coating, graphene coating, and PPF installs are completed at our studio - [SHOP ADDRESS]. The one exception is window tinting..."`

**Problem:**
A literal template placeholder string — `[SHOP ADDRESS]` — is live in production. Any visitor who opens the first FAQ ("Do you come to my home or office?") sees this.

This is the single highest-trust question on the page. The visitor asking it is trying to decide if they can physically get their car to the studio. The answer tells them nothing useful and signals that the website is unfinished or unmanaged. For a business selling $750–$7,900 precision services, an obviously unfilled placeholder directly erodes the "certified, obsessive, studio-grade" positioning.

The damage is disproportionate: this FAQ is likely the first one a mobile visitor opens, because travel logistics are the primary objection to studio-based services.

**Recommendation:**
Replace `[SHOP ADDRESS]` with the actual street address. If the address is intentionally withheld for security reasons, use "our Berwick studio" or the suburb only, consistent with how the suburbs list on the page presents the service area. Either is better than the current placeholder.

**Effort:** 5 minutes. **Priority: ship today.**

## Issue 2 — Gallery "View gallery" link dead for the third consecutive audit

**Element:** `GallerySection` → "View gallery" link at line 792
**Current code:** `href="#"`

**Problem:**
This was first flagged on June 16, re-flagged on July 14, and it is still `href="#"` — a no-op anchor that scrolls the page to the top rather than navigating anywhere. A visitor who clicks "View gallery" after seeing eight strong work photos (BMW M4 ceramic, Tesla Plaid, Porsche GT3-adjacent work) gets sent nowhere. The click intent — "I want to see more of this" — is high-purchase-intent behaviour, and it's currently being wasted.

Three audit cycles without a fix means this is stuck somewhere between implementation and priority, not forgotten. The `/gallery` route exists (`components/pages/gallery.tsx` is in the project). There is no technical reason for this to remain broken.

**Recommendation:**
Change `href="#"` to `href="/gallery"` at line 792. This is a one-character-then-seven-characters edit.

```tsx
// Line 792: Change this:
<a href="#" style={...}>View gallery <Arrow /></a>

// To this:
<a href="/gallery" style={...}>View gallery <Arrow /></a>
```

**Effort:** 2 minutes. This is the third escalation. If Jordan cannot ship this, identify the actual blocker.

## Issue 3 — Hero subhead claims "5,000+ car owners" but business data says "2,400+ cars protected"

**Element:** `HeroSection` → `<p>` at lines 90–92
**Current copy:** `"Precision grade ceramic, graphene, and paint protection film helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."`

**Problem:**
The product marketing context document (`product-marketing-context.md`) records the business's own proof point as **"2,400+ cars protected."** The hero is advertising **5,000+** — more than double the documented figure. This isn't a rounding difference; it's a claim that can't be substantiated from the business's own records.

A visitor who reads this, books a call, and then hears any figure that contradicts 5,000+ will notice. The ICP — car enthusiasts who research installers — is exactly the type to cross-check claims. Inflated social proof undermines the credibility the certification claims are trying to build.

The copy also has secondary issues:
- "Precision grade" is jargon with no proof attached (not in the approved language list either)
- "helping... protect their investment" is the kind of passive, clichéd phrasing the brand voice guide explicitly warns against

**Recommendation:**
Use the verified figure and rewrite to be specific:

> Before: `"Precision grade ceramic, graphene, and paint protection film helping 5,000+ car owners protect their investment."`

> After: `"Ceramic coating, graphene coating, and PPF by certified technicians. 2,400+ cars protected — 4.9 ★ across 39 reviews."`

This replaces an inflated number with an accurate one, adds the review score with context, and replaces vague phrasing with two concrete, verifiable proof points.

## Quick Win — FlagshipSection warranty claim is 3 years short

**Element:** `FlagshipSection` → `<p>` at line 385
**Problem:**
The flagship ceramic section says "lasting up to 5 years." The business's documented ceramic warranty is **up to 8 years manufacturer-backed**. This claim is both inaccurate (loses 3 years of a real competitive advantage) and was flagged as part of July 14's Issue 3 context.

This isn't a design change or a positioning test — it's a factual correction that makes the warranty claim stronger, not weaker.

**Effort:** 30-second edit on line 385. Ships alongside any other fix this sprint.

## Bigger Bet — A/B test the hero H1: poetic brand voice vs. outcome-specific positioning

**Current H1 (control):**
> "Where your car meets its best self."

**Hypothesis:**
The hero H1 is the first thing a visitor reads after landing from a Google search like "ceramic coating Melbourne" or "PPF installer Melbourne." The current headline is brand-expressive but category-ambiguous — it doesn't confirm what service this is, who it's for, or why it's worth staying. The category and location don't appear until the pill badge above the H1 (12px text) and the body copy below (19px secondary text).

Visitors who land from local search have already signalled high intent. They don't need brand warmth; they need confirmation they've landed in the right place before they'll scroll.

**Variant:**
> "Melbourne's certified ceramic coating, graphene & PPF studio."

Or, more aggressive:
> "Your car's paint, protected for up to 8 years. Certified studio, Melbourne."

**What to measure:**
- Primary: Click-through on hero "Book Now" CTA
- Secondary: Scroll depth past hero section
- Segment: Mobile traffic only (highest local search share)

**Expected trade-off:**
The variant will score better on immediate clarity for cold search traffic. It may score lower on brand warmth for visitors arriving from Instagram or word-of-mouth who already know the brand. Run both; consider the traffic split before declaring a winner.

**Why this is a bet, not a quick win:**
The current H1 may be doing brand-differentiation work that isn't captured in CTA click rates alone. Test for 4–6 weeks with a minimum of 400 sessions per variant. Do not change the H1 permanently without data.
