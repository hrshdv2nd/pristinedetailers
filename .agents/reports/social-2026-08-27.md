## Platform: LinkedIn

**Caption:**

Melbourne's UV index roughly doubles from August to October — from ~3 (moderate) to 6–7 (high). Most professionals driving prestige vehicles haven't thought about what that means for paint that hasn't been protected since before winter.

Here's the dynamic: winter is a low-UV environment, which means contamination — road grime, biological fallout, tannin from plane trees on St Kilda Road and Royal Parade, bird etch from urban bird activity — accumulates on unprotected paintwork without the photochemical acceleration that makes summer the obvious enemy. What's building since May is still sitting on the clear coat when September's UV arrives. At that point, UV locks chemical contamination into clear coat faster than most owners realise, and what could have been a decontamination job becomes a paint correction job.

The window is late August. Before Melbourne's UV index climbs, the contamination can be removed cleanly. For a car that already has a ceramic coating, now is the right time for a ceramic top-up and maintenance detail — the coating has absorbed 6 months of winter conditions and a fresh hydrophobic layer prepares it for the UV load ahead. For an uncoated car, applying ceramic now means it cures in stable late-winter temperatures and is fully protective by the time spring hits.

Pristine Detailers operates fully mobile across Melbourne's Inner East and Bayside — no workshop drop-off.
