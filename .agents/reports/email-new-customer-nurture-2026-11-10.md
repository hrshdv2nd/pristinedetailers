**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

We've finished up with the [car model — e.g. "3 Series"]. Everything looked great on the way out.

Before you get behind the wheel and forget about it — a quick note on what's actually happening to your paint right now.

The coating is curing. That's a 7-day process where the chemistry bonds to your clear coat, and during that window a few things matter:

**No washing for 7 days.** Not even a rinse. Water sitting on the surface during the cure can leave spots that are frustratingly difficult to remove once the coating has hardened. Melbourne's spring rain is usually light — if you get caught in a shower, that's fine. Just don't actively wash it.

**Spring pollen is a problem this week specifically.** November in Melbourne means flowering trees, and pollen deposits on your bonnet will combine with morning dew into a sticky film. During the cure window, don't rub it — a dry microfibre cloth to lift it gently is fine. After day 7, it'll clean off easily.

**Tree sap.** If you're parking under a eucalyptus or flowering ornamental (common in Toorak, Hawthorn, South Yarra right now), check the roof and bonnet in the morning. During cure, fresh sap is easier to lift with a quick gentle wipe than it will be once it's been baked on by the afternoon sun.

After day 7, you'll know the coating is working: water will roll off in sheets rather than spreading, and the paint will stay cleaner for longer between washes.

If anything looks off before then, reply here — I read every reply.

## Email 2 of 4 — Day 7

```
Send:    7 days after service completion
Subject: Your coating is fully cured. What to do now.
Preview: Spring in Melbourne is UV season — here's what that means for your paint.
```

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Seven days in. The coating is fully cured and it's doing what it was designed to do.

This is the right moment to talk about keeping it in good condition — because the habits you build in the first few months are the ones that matter most for the long term.

**On washing:**

Stop using automatic car washes with rotating brushes. Permanently. Those brushes don't distinguish between your ceramic coating and a 10-year-old Ford — they put swirl marks through both equally. If you need a quick clean on the go, a touchless wash is acceptable. Better: a two-bucket hand wash at home with a quality microfibre mitt and a pH-neutral car shampoo.

Standard dish soap — the stuff in a lot of homes — strips the hydrophobic layer from your coating over time. Most people only notice when the water stops beading. It's worth getting a proper car shampoo and keeping it in the garage.

**What spring is actually doing to your paint:**

November is when UV in Melbourne starts climbing toward summer peaks. Surface temperatures on a parked dark car can hit 60°C on a warm afternoon, and UV is the main reason coatings degrade faster than their warranty period should allow. Your coating handles this — that's what it's for — but keeping the paint out of full-day sun exposure when you're not driving isn't a bad habit.

Pollen and sap season runs through November into December. Neither will etch your coating the way bird droppings can etch unprotected paint, but both left in the sun for hours turn into something you have to work to remove. A weekly rinse is enough to keep the surface clean and the coating performing.

**Bird droppings:**

The magpies are nesting. This isn't a joke. Droppings from birds in this season are more acidic than at other times of year, and they can etch unprotected paint within a few hours on a hot day. Your coating gives you a buffer — but lift them quickly when you see them, don't let them sit.

Your coating will reward these habits. It doesn't need a lot — just consistent, low-effort attention.

- **Personalise the car:** If CRM captures vehicle make/model, use it in Email 1 and Email 4. "Your GT3" lands very differently than "your car" for the ICP.
- **Spring timing is specific:** This version replaces the June winter sequence. The UV, pollen, sap, and nesting bird references are accurate for November–December Melbourne. Revisit for January to remove pollen references and shift focus to summer heat management.
- **Glass coating referral offer:** Switched from interior detail (winter sequence) to glass coating — more relevant for spring/summer driving conditions and a higher perceived value to this ICP.
- **Sender name:** Send as a person — Tom, or the technician who did the job if trackable in the CRM. First-name senders consistently outperform "Pristine Detailers" in opens.
- **Exit condition:** Anyone who joins membership exits this sequence to the Member Onboarding track. Don't pitch membership to someone who already has it.
- **Pre-Christmas angle:** Email 4 is timed at ~45 days post-booking. For customers coated in October, that's mid-November — directly before the December new-car buying wave. The referral offer is calibrated to that moment.
- **Subject line A/B test candidates:**
  - Email 3: "Three weeks in — how's it looking?" vs. "What your coating needs before summer"
  - Email 4: "A small favour — and something for your network" vs. "Before your mate puts chips in their new car…"
